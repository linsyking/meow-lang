\begin{abstract}
Search-and-replace is a primitive every programmer knows; this note takes it seriously as a model of computation. We study the single operation $[A/B]S$ -- replace, in one left-to-right pass over $S$, every occurrence of $B$ by $A$, taking the leftmost match first and never rescanning inserted text -- over a finite alphabet. A chain of elementary lemmas turns this one operation into a complete toolkit: escaping and unescaping, concatenation, head and tail, equality, selection, and simultaneous multiple substitution are each exactly a short composition of substitutions, for arbitrary strings. We then formalize ``built from substitution'' as a small expression calculus and measure its power: every reachable function is computable in polynomial time, with degree bounded by its expression, while quadratic growth is reachable and exponential growth is not. Finally we locate the primitive's design choices by varying them one at a time -- once-only, positional, right-to-left, rescanning, and Markov-style iteration -- and then admitting both directions at once, obtaining concrete separations, collapses, a single-function collapse criterion for the union, a classification of the divergent small rules, and, by promoting the iteration from rules to expressions, an exact characterization: one fixed-point node over a flat pipeline computes precisely the partial computable functions. Allowing recursive definitions -- the \texttt{meow} language's own feature -- and varying only the runtime then separates sharply: eager and lazy-argument evaluation leave the reachable class unchanged, while evaluating a pass's replacement only when its pattern occurs -- the operator's own non-strictness -- makes the calculus universal, though without recursion that same non-strictness buys definedness, not power, and a coinductive reading on infinite strings reaches beyond every finite-state emitter. Finally the alphabet itself is varied: a comma-free coding conjugates the reachable class onto its conjugate over any other alphabet of at least two letters, in both directions, so no larger alphabet adds power up to conjugation, the once and reversal questions are each a single question across alphabets, and the unary edge splits -- reachable from below, structurally blocked from above. Every computational claim is verified by exhaustive enumeration over the finite domains stated where it occurs.

\keywords{string substitution \and string rewriting \and finite alphabets \and rewriting calculus}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Search-and-replace is everywhere in computing: text editors, regular expressions, templating macros, and the escaping rules of every string literal. This note is about what that single button can do when it is the \emph{only} thing on the toolbar.

Fix a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ and consider the operation
\begin{align*}
    [A/B]\,S,
\end{align*}
which scans $S$ once from left to right and replaces every occurrence of $B$ by $A$: the \emph{leftmost} match is taken first, matches do not overlap, and the scan never restarts inside the text it has just inserted. For a first taste, let $\Sigma = \{\mathtt{b}, \mathtt{x}\}$ and escape every $\mathtt{b}$ by prefixing an $\mathtt{x}$, the way a backslash escapes a quote:
\begin{align*}
    \mathtt{enc}(S) \triangleq [\mathtt{xb}/\mathtt{b}]\,S, \qquad \mathtt{dec}(T) \triangleq [\mathtt{b}/\mathtt{xb}]\,T.
\end{align*}
Then $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(S)) = S$ for \emph{every} string $S$ -- the round trip that makes string literals work, here a two-line theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:enc} below; the reader is invited to find the subtlety: why is the second pass never confused by the $\mathtt{x}$'s the first pass inserted?). Pushing further: there is a \emph{fixed} template string and a chain of substitutions that concatenates \emph{arbitrary} strings through it,
\begin{align*}
    \mathtt{cat}(X,Y) \;=\; \mathtt{dec}\big([\mathtt{enc}(X)/\mathtt{xb}^2]\,[\mathtt{enc}(Y)/\mathtt{xb}^3]\,(\mathtt{xb}^2\mathtt{xb}^3)\big) \;=\; XY,
\end{align*}
and, harder still, one substitution chain that performs $n$ \emph{simultaneous} substitutions $\{X_i \mapsto Y_i\}_{i \le n}$ with no restrictions whatsoever on the patterns (Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution}).

Three design choices are built into $[A/B]$: the scan goes left to right and takes the leftmost match; one pass replaces \emph{all} occurrences; and the scan never re-enters inserted text. Each is load-bearing. This note develops the theory of the chosen primitive along three axes.

\paragraph{The toolkit (Section~\ref{sec:toolkit}).} We prove the basic lemmas of the primitive (elimination, independence, tail and head elimination, double substitution) and then build, each as an explicit composition of substitutions with a correctness proof: escaping and unescaping, concatenation, head and tail, equality testing, an \texttt{if} selector, and finally multiple substitution $\mathtt{rep}_n$, which performs $n$ pattern-replacement pairs in one shot over an arbitrary alphabet, patterns unrestricted.

\paragraph{The calculus (Section~\ref{sec:calculus}).} ``Constructible from substitution'' is made precise by a small expression calculus with variables, string constants, one substitution constructor, and concatenation. A syntactic safety condition implies totality; concatenation is eliminable (every expression is equivalent to a pure pipeline of passes); and every reachable function is computable in polynomial time, with the exponent bounded by the expression. The hierarchy is nontrivial: quadratic growth is reachable, while $X \mapsto X^{2^{|X|}}$ is not.

\paragraph{The variants (Section~\ref{sec:variants}).} We vary the design choices one at a time -- single-occurrence substitution, its $k$-th-occurrence generalization, positional substitution, right-to-left scanning, rescanning of inserted text, and Markov-style iteration to a fixed point -- and measure the damage: which variants can simulate which, what survives of the toolkit, and how the growth rates separate. Among other things, the once-only primitive provably cannot simulate a full pass; the $k$-th-occurrence family between them spreads into a mosaic of pairwise incomparable fragments whose relation to once reduces to marker freshness; rescan-based substitution agrees with the safe primitive exactly on a decidable local condition; Markov iteration reaches arbitrary towers of exponentials while the bounded pipeline stays polynomial; and promoting the iteration from rules to expressions -- iterating a pipeline to its fixed point -- pins the class exactly: one such node over a flat pipeline computes precisely the partial computable functions. The union of both scanning directions collapses onto the baseline exactly when the right pass, as a function of its arguments, is itself reachable -- which every search says it is not. A census of all binary rules with $|A|, |B| \le 4$ classifies every divergent one.

\paragraph{Alphabet invariance (Section~\ref{sec:alphabet}).} The alphabet itself is then varied. A \emph{good coding} -- injective, letter-by-letter, with a comma-free dictionary in the sense of Golomb -- conjugates the calculus between any two alphabets of at least two letters, and the reachable class is invariant under the conjugation in both directions: a function is reachable over the one iff its conjugate is reachable over the other, with node count preserved up to a constant of the alphabet pair. All four scanning primitives are coding-equivariant, so each variant's hinge is a single question across alphabets, and reversal is reachable over one alphabet of at least two letters iff over every -- a single negative answer would settle every alphabet. The unary edge is sharp: reachable from below by coding, and structurally unreachable from above, since no injective homomorphism collapses two letters onto one; the unary class itself is eventually polynomially bounded, with floor-halving absent from the entire space of small expressions.

\paragraph{Recursive definitions (Section~\ref{sec:recursion}).} The \texttt{meow} language recurses; the calculus can too, with named definitions and call nodes, mutual recursion included. What that buys depends only on the runtime. Under eager evaluation and under lazy arguments, nothing: every recursive definition either inlines to a plain expression or never returns, and the reachable class is unchanged. Under lazy \emph{passes} -- the replacement of a pass being evaluated only when its pattern occurs, which is the operator's own non-strictness -- everything: guarded recursion through pattern gates computes exactly the partial computable functions, reversal and the exponential function become total recursions, and totality of a definition is undecidable. A coinductive reading on infinite strings replaces termination by productivity and outgrows every finite-state emitter -- and the same reading of the variants' iteration operator splits it from its finite one: least versus greatest fixpoint in a single rule.

Everything finite in this note -- every identity, counterexample, census, and run of the recursive machines -- was verified by exhaustive enumeration over the domains stated in situ, by scripts accompanying the paper; the proofs are otherwise pen-and-paper.

\subsection{Related Work}

\paragraph{String rewriting.} The classical theory of semi-Thue systems makes the opposite trade-off to ours: rules are fixed strings, and the emphasis is on the \emph{relation} they generate -- the word problem, confluence, termination \cite{thue14,book93}. Our primitive meets that theory at exactly one point: the restart variant of Section~\ref{sec:variants} is the leftmost-strategy normalization of the single rule $B \to A$ (Definition~\ref{def:markov}) -- a one-rule semi-Thue system under a deterministic strategy. There our termination question (Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}) becomes the celebrated one-rule termination problem: undecidable already for systems with three rules \cite{matiyasevich05}, open for one, and decidable for substantial special classes -- left-hand sides of the shape $a^*b^*$ \cite{senizergues96}, rules with an inhibitor \cite{mcnaughton01}, grid rules \cite{geser02}, rules with one pair of overlaps \cite{geser03}, single-threaded derivations \cite{moczydlowski05}, the counter family $0^p1^q \to 1^r0^s$ \cite{zantema00} -- with Geser's habilitation thesis as the survey \cite{geser01}. These classes decide termination under \emph{all} strategies; none decides the leftmost-strategy question our variant asks, and we know of no reduction between the two problems in either direction. On the census domain of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination} the two notions coincide -- every rule that diverges under some strategy already diverges under leftmost -- so the hard case of the variant is the classical open problem with a strategy restriction, and the classical decision methods apply only as sufficient conditions.

\paragraph{What is not covered.} The rest of this note is not a chapter of semi-Thue theory, in either direction, for three structural reasons. First, patterns are \emph{variable}: a pass like $[X_1/\sigma]$ instantiates its pattern from the input at run time, which no fixed rule set does. Constant patterns keep every pass inside the finite-state world of rational transductions \cite{berstel79,elgot65}; the variable ones are what the constructions of Section~\ref{sec:toolkit} -- concatenation, equality, multiple substitution -- rely on. Second, composition is \emph{bounded}: an expression is a finite pipeline applied once, not a relation iterated to normal form; totality is structural (Theorem~\ref{thm:totality}) and every reachable function is polynomial-time (Theorem~\ref{thm:fp}), whereas rewriting iterated to a normal form is Turing-complete \cite{post47,markov54}. Third, the semantics is a \emph{single sweep}: the pass never re-enters inserted text, so $[\,a/aa\,]$ is a total function while the rewriting rule $a \to aa$ diverges under every strategy. Consequently the open problems of this note -- the exact characterization of the reachable class, the two hinges of Section~\ref{sec:variants} (once-reachability and reversal), the growth dichotomy for total restart rules -- have no classical counterpart to inherit an answer from; and conversely, the questions semi-Thue theory does ask -- confluence, word problems, monadic systems \cite{book93} -- have no meaning for a deterministic single pass. Section~\ref{sec:recursion} sharpens the second reason from a trade-off into a border: recursion added to the calculus is inert under every call-by-value runtime, and reaches Turing completeness exactly when the operator's own non-strictness -- the replacement of a pass, evaluated only when the pattern occurs -- is admitted as the recursion's guard.

\paragraph{Substitution-based programming.} A folk practice programs by substitution alone, and each of its universality stories buys the loop somewhere else. Macro processors reach Turing completeness with unbounded recursion and rescanning of expanded text: \texttt{m4} \cite{kernighan77m4} and \TeX{} \cite{knuth84} are the standard examples, the \texttt{C++} template mechanism was sketched Turing complete by Veldhuizen \cite{veldhuizen95tc}, and Rust's \texttt{macro\_rules!} expansion simulates tag systems, with expansion halting exactly when the encoded machine does \cite{tlborm}. The C preprocessor, the closest practical relative of our primitive, sits on the fence: the standard marks a macro name unavailable while it is being expanded -- the ``blue paint'' rule -- blocking direct self-reference, and an analysis of the C++20 rules concludes that ``the real limit is how much time and memory we have for cpp, not the fact that cpp isn't turing complete'' \cite{mazieres21}; recursion is recovered only by deferred-expansion tricks that hide the recursive call from the rescan, an unformalized cousin of the laziness clause of Section~\ref{sec:recursion}. Esolangs isolate pieces of the idea: the /// language's single operation is repeated substitution on its own text, its Turing completeness resting on an embedded Bitwise Cyclic Tag interpreter \cite{slashes}, and sed is Turing complete through labels and branches rather than through its \texttt{s} command alone \cite{krumins-sed}. The nearest formal statement we know is an informal note of Wehar \cite{wehar}: a language with variable assignment, global replace, and a universal program is Turing complete -- a construction that already leans on the operator equation $[\,A/B\,]S = S$ ($B$ absent) as its conditional. None of these isolates the single never-rescanning sweep as the only data operation, states the evaluation rule under which recursion buys universality, or separates it from the strict runtimes; that border is what Section~\ref{sec:recursion} draws, and the lazy-pass rule itself is a neededness criterion in the sense of Huet and L\'evy's call-by-need computations \cite{huetlevy79} -- the replacement of $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ is needed exactly when $P$ occurs -- not previously instantiated for string substitution.

\paragraph{Codes, transductions, stringology.} The constant-pattern fragment of the calculus sits inside the classical theory of sequential transductions \cite{berstel79,elgot65}: each pass is a left-to-right finite-state replacement, which is the content of Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}. The encoder round trip is the decoding of a prefix code -- the comma code of Section~\ref{sec:toolkit} is a uniform one -- in the sense of the theory of codes \cite{berstel85,lothaire97}. The linear-time bound on a single pass underlying Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} is classical pattern matching \cite{knuth77,aho75}, and the periodicity facts used in Sections~\ref{sec:toolkit} and~\ref{sec:variants} are those of the combinatorics of words \cite{fine65,lothaire97}. The modern stream-machine form of that boundary is the streaming string transducer, again exactly the finite-state world \cite{alur10}; recursion under lazy passes is what leaves it (Section~\ref{sec:recursion}).

\paragraph{Growth of one-rule systems.} Our growth questions for restart rules have their classical cousins in the derivational complexity of one-rule systems \cite{kobayashi01}; the census methodology of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v) follows Kurth \cite{kurth90,kurth96}; both nodes of the tower of Corollary~\ref{cor:towers} are rules of the completely characterized family $0^p1^q \to 1^r0^s$ \cite{zantema00}, and modern automated termination tooling for such systems rests on match-boundedness \cite{geser04}.

\section{The Substitution Toolkit}
\label{sec:toolkit}

Characters are elements of a finite alphabet $\Sigma$; a string is a finite sequence of characters. Throughout Sections~\ref{sec:toolkit}--\ref{sec:limits} the alphabet is fixed with $|\Sigma| \ge 2$; the infinite case behaves differently and is not considered, and Section~\ref{sec:variants}, which varies the primitive itself, also tracks the unary case $|\Sigma| = 1$ explicitly wherever it differs.

We use the following notation:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Sigma$ denotes a finite alphabet with at least two elements; $\Sigma^*$ is the set of finite strings over $\Sigma$.
    \item Single capital letters denote strings (possibly empty); single lower-case letters denote characters.
    \item $\epsilon$ denotes the empty string; $\epsilon A=A\epsilon=A$ for any string $A$. $|A|$ is the length of $A$. $AB$ is the concatenation of $A$ and $B$ (also written $A\cdot B$ when a dot aids readability); $A^n$ is the $n$-fold concatenation of $A$.
    \item $S[i]$ is the character of $S$ at position $i$, positions starting at $0$; $S[i{:}j]$ is the substring from position $i$ (inclusive) to $j$ (exclusive). An omitted bound is $0$ (left) or $|S|$ (right): $S[{:}j]$, $S[i{:}]$.
    \item $[A/B]$ is the substitution function $\Sigma^* \rightarrow \Sigma^*$, defined in Definition~\ref{def:subst} below; $[A/\epsilon]$ is \textbf{undefined}.
    \item $A\subset B\Leftrightarrow \exists C, D,\ B = CAD$ ($A$ \emph{occurs in} $B$). In particular $A\subset A$.
    \item $A\sqsubset B\Leftrightarrow \exists C, B = AC$ ($A$ is a \emph{prefix} of $B$).
    \item $A\sqsupset B\Leftrightarrow \exists C, B = CA$ ($A$ is a \emph{suffix} of $B$).
    \item $A \cap B = \varnothing$ means the strings $A$ and $B$ share no character.
    \item A string $Y$ is \emph{unbordered} if
    \begin{align*}
        \forall W \in \Sigma^*,\quad \bigl(W \sqsubset Y \wedge W \sqsupset Y\bigr) \;\Rightarrow\; \bigl(W = \epsilon \vee W = Y\bigr),
    \end{align*}
    i.e.\ $\nexists\, W \notin \{\epsilon, Y\}$ with $W \sqsubset Y \wedge W \sqsupset Y$. Borders are a classical object of the combinatorics of words \cite{lothaire97}.
    \item Juxtaposition is function composition, and substitutions compose \textbf{right-to-left}: in $[A/B][C/D]\,E$ the substitution $[C/D]$ is applied first. This convention is load-bearing; see Remark~\ref{rem:order}.
    \item $\prod_i^{m\rightarrow n} X(i) \triangleq X(m)X(m+1)\cdots X(n)$.
    \item $\prod_i^{m\leftarrow n} X(i) \triangleq X(n)X(n-1)\cdots X(m)$.
\end{itemize}

There are several possible implementation of $\mathtt{replace}(B, A)$. We now assume the following recursive \textit{left-to-right} implementation:

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{(Left-to-Right) String Substitution})
    \label{def:subst}
    Given two strings $A, B$, for any string $C$,
    \begin{align}
        [A/B]C = \begin{cases}
            C,\quad B \not\subset C\\
            XA[A/B]Y,\quad (X, Y) = \arg\min_{(X', Y')} \{ |X'| | C = X'BY' \}
        \end{cases}
    \end{align}
    That is: scanning $C$ from the left, replace every occurrence of $B$ by $A$, where the scanning never restarts inside inserted text. In particular, inserted text is never rescanned, so e.g. $[ab/b]\mathtt{b} = \mathtt{ab}$, not $\mathtt{a}\,\mathtt{ab}$. This matches the \texttt{String::replace} of mainstream implementations.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Identity Substitution})
    If $A\neq \epsilon$,
    \begin{align}
        [A/A]S = S
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $|S|$. If $A \not\subset S$, then $[A/A]S=S$. Otherwise let $S = XAY$ with $A \not\subset X$; then $[A/A]S = XA[A/A]Y = XAY = S$ by the induction hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Direct Substitution})
    If $B\neq \epsilon$,
    \begin{align}
        [A/B]B = A
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The leftmost occurrence of $B$ in $B$ is at position $0$; after the replacement the scan resumes at the end of the inserted $A$, facing the empty string.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Substitution Elimination})
    If $B\not\subset S$,
    \begin{align}
        [A/B]S = S
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Immediate from Definition~\ref{def:subst}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Independent Substitution})
    If $B \neq \epsilon, A \cap B = \varnothing, A \cap C = \varnothing, C \neq \epsilon$, then
    \begin{align}
        A\subset [B/C]S \Leftrightarrow A \subset S
    \end{align}
    \label{thm:independent substitution}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Both directions are trivial when $A = \epsilon$ (the empty string occurs in every string), so assume $A \neq \epsilon$.

    First we prove $\Leftarrow$.
    If $A \subset S$, let $S = XAY$. Since $A \cap C = \varnothing$, no occurrence of $C$ straddles $A$ (an occurrence touching $A$ shares a character with it): we have $[B/C](XAY) = ([B/C]X)A([B/C]Y)$. Thus $A \subset [B/C]S$.

    Now we prove $\Rightarrow$.
    If $A \subset [B/C]S$, we do induction on $|S|$.

    If $|S| = 0$, then $A \subset [B/C]\epsilon = \epsilon$ would force $A = \epsilon$, excluded; there is nothing to prove. Suppose the statement is true for $|S|\leq n$. Now consider the case when $|S| = n+1$.

    Consider whether $C \subset S$. If $C \not\subset S$, then $[B/C]S = S$, we are done. If $C \subset S$, let $S = XCY$ exhibit the \emph{leftmost} occurrence of $C$ (no occurrence of $C$ starts at a position $< |X|$).

    Then we have $[B/C](XCY) = XB([B/C]Y)$. Since $B \neq \epsilon$ and $A \cap B = \varnothing$, no occurrence of $A$ straddles the inserted $B$, so $A \subset X \lor A \subset [B/C]Y$. By IH on $Y$, we have $A \subset X \lor A \subset Y$. In either case $A\subset XCY$ Thus $A \subset S$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The hypothesis $B \neq \epsilon$ is necessary: when $B = \epsilon$ the substitution deletes $C$, and deletion can \emph{create} occurrences of $A$ by merging its neighbors. Take $A = \mathtt{aa}$, $C = \mathtt{b}$, $S = \mathtt{aba}$: $[\epsilon/\mathtt{b}]\mathtt{aba} = \mathtt{aa}$, so $A \subset [B/C]S$, yet $A \not\subset S$. (The $\Leftarrow$ direction holds also for $B = \epsilon$.)
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Tail Elimination})
    $B\neq\epsilon, B\cap A=\varnothing$, then
    \begin{align}
        CB\subset AB \Rightarrow C \sqsupset A
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    We first claim every occurrence of $B$ inside $AB$ is the final $B$. Suppose $B$ occurs at position $i$, so $AB[i{:}i+|B|] = B$. If $i < |A|$, then $AB[i] = A[i]$, a character of $A$, while $AB[i] = B[0]$, a character of $B$; these share no character, a contradiction (note $B[0]$ exists since $B\neq\epsilon$). Hence $i = |A|$, the final $B$.

    Now write $AB = X\,C\,B\,D$ (some $X, D$). The trailing $B$ of the occurrence is an occurrence of $B$ inside $AB$, hence is the final $B$; so $|X|+|C| = |A|$ (and $D = \epsilon$), and $C \sqsupset A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Head Elimination})
    $B\neq\epsilon, B\cap A=\varnothing$, then
    \begin{align}
        BC\subset BA \Rightarrow C \sqsubset A
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Symmetric to the Tail Elimination: every occurrence of $B$ inside $BA$ is the initial $B$ (an occurrence at position $i \ge 1$ covers position $i+|B|-1 \ge |B|$, which lies in $A$'s territory, again forcing a shared character). Hence in $BA = X\,BC\,D$ the leading $B$ is the initial one, $X = \epsilon$, and $C \sqsubset A$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Stepping-into})
    $A\neq \epsilon$, then
    \begin{align}
        [C/A] (AB) = C([C/A]B)
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    The leftmost occurrence of $A$ in $AB$ is at position $0$; Definition~\ref{def:subst} gives $[C/A](AB) = C[C/A]B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Double Substitution Lemma})
    $X,Y$ are nonempty, $X\subset Y$, and $Y$ is unbordered, then
    \begin{align}
        [X/Y][Y/X]Z=Z
    \end{align}
    \label{lem:double substitution}
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}
    Both hypotheses are necessary:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item If $Y$ is bordered, the lemma fails: take $X=\mathtt{ba}\subset Y=\mathtt{aba}$ (the border $\mathtt{a}$) and $Z=\mathtt{baabba}$. The greedy scan finds $\mathtt{ba}$ at positions $0$ and $4$, so $[Y/X]Z = \mathtt{aba}\cdot\mathtt{ab}\cdot\mathtt{aba} = \mathtt{abaababa}$; now $[X/Y]$ finds $\mathtt{aba}$ at positions $0$ and $3$, yielding $\mathtt{bababa} \neq Z$. The failure is a \emph{spurious} $\mathtt{aba}$ straddling the junction of two inserted copies -- exactly what unborderedness rules out in case (ii) of the proof.
        \item If $X \not\subset Y$ it fails even for unbordered $Y$: take $X=\mathtt{a}$, $Y=\mathtt{bb}$, $Z=\mathtt{aba}$: $[Y/X]Z = \mathtt{bbbbb}$, and $[X/Y](\mathtt{bbbbb}) = \mathtt{aab} \neq Z$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
    Let $W = [Y/X]Z$. By Definition~\ref{def:subst} (leftmost-first, non-restarting), the greedy scan yields a decomposition
    \begin{align}
        W = Z_0\,Y\,Z_1\,Y\,Z_2\cdots Y\,Z_k
    \end{align}
    where each gap $Z_i$ contains no occurrence of $X$. Since $X\subset Y$, each $Z_i$ contains no occurrence of $Y$ either.

    We claim: \emph{every occurrence of $Y$ in $W$ starts at the head of one of the exhibited copies.} Let an occurrence start at position $p$; there are three cases.
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $p$ is the head of an exhibited copy: done.
        \item $p$ lies inside some gap $Z_i$. If the occurrence ended inside $Z_i$ as well, then $Y\subset Z_i$, hence $X \subset Z_i$ -- contradiction. (For the last gap $Z_k$ there is nothing after, so the occurrence must end inside it.) So it straddles the end of $Z_i$: $Y = AB$ where $A$ is a nonempty suffix of $Z_i$ and $B$ is a nonempty prefix of the exhibited copy following $Z_i$. Since $A\neq\epsilon$ and $AB = Y$, $B$ is a \emph{proper} prefix of that copy, i.e.\ a proper prefix of $Y$; and $B$ is simultaneously a suffix of $Y$ (as $Y = AB$), i.e.\ a border of $Y$ -- contradicting unborderedness.
        \item $p$ lies strictly inside an exhibited copy, at offset $i>0$. Comparing the occurrence with the exhibited copy character by character gives $Y[j] = Y[i+j]$ for all $0\le j < |Y|-i$, i.e.\ the prefix of $Y$ of length $|Y|-i$ equals its suffix of length $|Y|-i$: a proper border of $Y$ -- contradiction.
    \end{enumerate}
    Now compute $[X/Y]W$ by the greedy scan. The leftmost occurrence of $Y$ is the first exhibited copy; it is replaced by $X$, and the scan resumes after the inserted $X$ -- which it never re-enters -- facing a string of the same form $Z_i\,Y\,Z_{i+1}\cdots$. By the claim, no occurrence of $Y$ begins inside a gap, and occurrences inside the inserted $X$ are behind the scan. Induction over the exhibited copies gives
    \begin{align}
        [X/Y]W = Z_0\,X\,Z_1\cdots X\,Z_k = Z,
    \end{align}
    the last equality holding because the greedy scan of $[Y/X]$ selected exactly the occurrences of $X$ at these positions.
\end{proof}

The first construction is the escape of the introduction: one substitution escapes a character, one undoes it.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Encoding / Decoding})
    \label{thm:enc}
    Let $b, x \in \Sigma$ with $b \neq x$. We define the following operations on strings:
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{enc}_{b,x}(S) & \triangleq [xb/b]S \\
        \mathtt{dec}_{b,x}(S) & \triangleq [b/xb]S
    \end{align}

    Then we have:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item For any string $S$, we have:
              \begin{align}
                  \mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(S)) & =S
              \end{align}
        \item \begin{align}
                  bb \not\subset \mathtt{enc}(S)
              \end{align}
        \item \begin{align}
                  \mathtt{enc}(X)\mathtt{enc}(Y) = \mathtt{enc}(XY)
              \end{align}
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    (iii) Because the pattern $b$ is a single character, its matches never straddle any boundary. Formally, for any single character $a$ we have $\mathtt{enc}(aS) = \mathtt{enc}(a)\,\mathtt{enc}(S)$, where $\mathtt{enc}(a) = a$ if $a \neq b$ and $\mathtt{enc}(b) = xb$; this is immediate from Definition~\ref{def:subst} (if $a\neq b$, the scan skips $a$ and continues on $S$; if $a = b$, the leftmost match is at position $0$). The claim follows by induction on $|X|$, peeling one character at a time.

    (ii) By induction on $|S|$, using the same peeling: if $S = aS'$ with $a\neq b$, then $\mathtt{enc}(S) = a\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$, and a $bb$ occurrence inside $a\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$ must lie inside $\mathtt{enc}(S')$ (a straddling occurrence would force $a = b$) -- contradiction by IH. If $S = bS'$, then $\mathtt{enc}(S) = xb\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$: a $bb$ occurrence would either lie inside $\mathtt{enc}(S')$ (contradiction by IH) or straddle, requiring the $x$ of $xb$ to be a $b$ -- impossible. In particular $\mathtt{enc}(S)$ never begins with $b$.

    (i) Take $X := b$ and $Y := xb$ in the Double Substitution Lemma: $b \subset xb$, and $xb$ is unbordered (a proper prefix $\mathtt{x}$ is not a suffix). Note $[Y/X] = [xb/b] = \mathtt{enc}$ and $[X/Y] = [b/xb] = \mathtt{dec}$, so
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(S)) = [b/xb][xb/b]\,S = S.
    \end{align}
\end{proof}

This is the engine behind every construction that follows, and behind the character-escaping function at the end of the section.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Encoding Monotonicity})
    $A\subset B \Rightarrow \mathtt{enc}(A)\subset \mathtt{enc}(B)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    By (iii), $\mathtt{enc}$ is a monoid morphism (a homomorphism for concatenation): $\mathtt{enc}(B) = \mathtt{enc}(CAD) = \mathtt{enc}(C)\,\mathtt{enc}(A)\,\mathtt{enc}(D)$, which exhibits $\mathtt{enc}(A)$ inside $\mathtt{enc}(B)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The converse \emph{fails}: for $A = \mathtt{x}$, $B = \mathtt{b}$ we have $\mathtt{enc}(A) = \mathtt{x} \subset \mathtt{enc}(B) = \mathtt{xb}$ but $A \not\subset B$. Only the forward direction holds, and only the forward direction is used in this note.
\end{remark}

We will need the \texttt{cat} macro extensively:

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{String Concatenation})
    \label{thm:cat}
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{cat}(X,Y) \triangleq \mathtt{dec}_{b,x}\left([\mathtt{enc}_{b,x}(X)/xb^2][\mathtt{enc}_{b,x}(Y)/xb^3]\,(xb^2xb^3)\right)
    \end{align}
    where $xb^k$ abbreviates $\underbrace{xb\cdots b}_{k\text{ times}}$. Then for any $X,Y$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{cat}(X,Y) = XY
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:cat-markers}
    The markers $xb^k$ with $k \ge 2$ are chosen so that every occurrence of a marker in the intermediate strings is a whole marker. A marker contains $bb$, and an $\mathtt{enc}$-image contains none (Theorem~\ref{thm:enc}(ii)), so a marker never occurs inside an image; and a marker begins with $x$, so an occurrence straddling the junction between an image and a marker would need its $b$'s to come from the image's end -- two $b$'s, which the image does not contain. The order of the two substitutions matters as well: $xb^2 \subset xb^3$, so the pattern $xb^2$ would also match inside a surviving $xb^3$ marker if $[\mathtt{enc}(Y)/xb^3]$ did not run first.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
    Write $E_X = \mathtt{enc}(X)$, $E_Y = \mathtt{enc}(Y)$.

    \emph{Step 1} ($[E_Y/xb^3]$, applied first): in $xb^2xb^3$ the pattern $xb^3$ occurs exactly once, at position $3$. An occurrence starting at position $0$ would read $xb^2x \neq xb^3$; none starts at positions $1$ or $2$, since $xb^3$ begins with $x$. The substitution yields $xb^2\,E_Y$.

    \emph{Step 2} ($[E_X/xb^2]$): in $xb^2\,E_Y$ the leftmost occurrence of $xb^2$ is at position $0$. There is no other occurrence: none starts at positions $1$ or $2$ (the pattern begins with $x$), none lies inside $E_Y$ ($xb^2$ contains $bb$, $E_Y$ does not), and none straddles the junction (the same argument as in Remark~\ref{rem:cat-markers}: it would need two $b$'s at the end of $E_Y$, impossible). The substitution yields $E_X\,E_Y$.

    \emph{Step 3}: $E_XE_Y = \mathtt{enc}(X)\mathtt{enc}(Y) = \mathtt{enc}(XY)$ by (iii), and $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(XY)) = XY$ by (i).
\end{proof}

Concatenation of arbitrary strings is therefore itself a substitution
expression -- and it is worth being precise about what that does and
does not mean.  Once the calculus of Section~\ref{sec:calculus} admits
$E_1E_2$ as a constructor, the function $(X, Y) \mapsto XY$ is
realized by the term $XY$ itself, trivially; Theorem~\ref{thm:cat} is
not a reachability claim.  Its content is that a \emph{pure pipeline
of passes} computes the function: the naive $[\,X/a\,][\,Y/b\,](ab)$
fails -- the $[\,X/a\,]$ pass replaces the $a$'s of $Y$ as well, and
no choice of bare markers repairs it, any fixed marker word occurring
inside some $Y$ -- and the escape is the repair.  That is the content
the calculus cashes in as the eliminability of the constructor
(Theorem~\ref{thm:core}: concatenation is conservative sugar) and each
variant of Section~\ref{sec:variants} must re-earn under its own
semantics -- the once-calculus with no escape at all, its
leftmost-only discipline making the naive markers safe
(Theorem~\ref{thm:once-toolkit}(i)).  From now on we write $XY$
freely, reading it as an abbreviation of $\mathtt{cat}(X,Y)$.

The primitives can also take strings apart: the first character of a string, and the rest.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Single Character Substitution})

    Suppose $\Omega  = \{\omega_1,\cdots,\omega_M\}\subset \Sigma,\sigma \in \Sigma$, then for any string $S$,
    \begin{align}
        [\sigma/\Omega]S \triangleq [\sigma/\omega_1][\sigma/\omega_2]\cdots[\sigma/\omega_M]S
    \end{align}
    is well-defined, i.e. the order of substitution does not matter. And when $\Omega=\Sigma$,
    \begin{align}
        [\sigma/\Sigma]S = \underbrace{\sigma\cdots\sigma}_{|S|}
    \end{align}
    \label{lem:single character substitution}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    For two distinct characters $\omega \neq \omega'$, the substitutions $[\sigma/\omega]$ and $[\sigma/\omega']$ commute: both only ever touch positions holding $\omega$ or $\omega'$, and each replaces the scanned character by $\sigma$ regardless. Formally, for any character $c$: $[\sigma/\omega']([\sigma/\omega]c) = [\sigma/\omega]([\sigma/\omega']c)$ (both give $c$ if $c \notin \{\omega,\omega'\}$, and $\sigma$ otherwise), and by induction on $|S|$ this extends to whole strings, since neither pattern can straddle a boundary. Hence all orders coincide.

    When $\Omega = \Sigma$: every character of $S$ is some $\omega_i$, so scanning $[\sigma/\omega_i]$ replaces each $\omega_i$ by $\sigma$; by the first part the composition over all $i$ replaces every character by $\sigma$, and no inserted $\sigma$ is rescanned (Definition~\ref{def:subst}), so the result is $\sigma^{|S|}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Head and Tail})
    \label{thm:headtail}
    Assume that $\Sigma = \{\sigma_1,\cdots,\sigma_N\}$ (so $N \ge 2$).

    We define the following functions (let $\mathtt{enc}=\mathtt{enc}_{\sigma_1,\sigma_2},\mathtt{dec}=\mathtt{dec}_{\sigma_1,\sigma_2}$):
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{tail}(X) & \triangleq \mathtt{dec}\big([\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1][\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2][\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1] \notag\\
        & \qquad\cdot (\textstyle\prod_i^{3\rightarrow N}[\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_i])\,(\sigma_1\sigma_1\,\mathtt{enc}(X))\big) \\
        \mathtt{head}(X) & \triangleq \mathtt{dec}([\epsilon/\mathtt{enc}(\mathtt{tail}(X))\sigma_1\sigma_1](\mathtt{enc}(X)\sigma_1\sigma_1))
    \end{align}
    Then for every character $a$ and string $S$:
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{tail}(\epsilon) & = \epsilon, & \mathtt{tail}(aS) & = S, \\
        \mathtt{head}(\epsilon) & = \epsilon, & \mathtt{head}(aS) & = a.
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:order}
    The right-to-left composition convention is essential here: in $\mathtt{tail}$, the substitution $[\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1]$ \emph{must} run before $[\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2]$ (for $a = \sigma_1$, the text begins $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\cdots$, where both patterns match at position $0$, and only the longer deletion is correct). Reading the composition left-to-right would compute something else.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
    Throughout, $b = \sigma_1$ and $x = \sigma_2$, so by the Encoding Theorem: (F1) $\mathtt{enc}(W)$ contains no $\sigma_1\sigma_1$, (F2) $\mathtt{enc}(W)$ never begins with $\sigma_1$, and (F3) $\mathtt{enc}(W)$ ends with $\sigma_1$ iff $W$ does (its last unit is a single character $c \neq \sigma_1$, or the block $\sigma_2\sigma_1$).

    \emph{tail.} Let $T_0 = \sigma_1\sigma_1\,\mathtt{enc}(X)$. Every deletion pattern contains $\sigma_1\sigma_1$, so by (F1) it can only match at position $0$. Case analysis on $X$:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $X = \epsilon$: $T_0 = \sigma_1\sigma_1$; no pattern $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_i$ or $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ matches (not enough characters), and the final $[\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1]$ deletes the marker. The result is $\mathtt{dec}(\epsilon) = \epsilon$.
        \item $X = aS'$, $a = \sigma_i$ with $i \ge 3$: $T_0 = \sigma_1\sigma_1\,\sigma_i\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$. The pattern $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_i$ matches at $0$, all other longer patterns fail on their third or fourth character, and the deletion leaves $\mathtt{enc}(S')$, on which every remaining pattern is inert by (F1). The result is $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(S')) = S'$.
        \item $X = aS'$, $a = \sigma_2$: $T_0 = \sigma_1\sigma_1\,\sigma_2\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$ (as $a \neq b$). The pattern $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2$ matches at $0$; the pattern $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ does not (the character after $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2$ is the first character of $\mathtt{enc}(S')$, which is not $\sigma_1$ by (F2)). Same conclusion.
        \item $X = aS'$, $a = \sigma_1$: $\mathtt{enc}(X) = \sigma_2\sigma_1\,\mathtt{enc}(S')$, so $T_0$ begins $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$, and the pattern $\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ matches at $0$ (this is why it must run before $[\epsilon/\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2]$, cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:order}). The deletion leaves $\mathtt{enc}(S')$; same conclusion.
    \end{itemize}

    \emph{head.} Let $T = \mathtt{enc}(X)\,\sigma_1\sigma_1$. First locate the occurrences of $\sigma_1\sigma_1$ in $T$: by (F1) none lies inside $\mathtt{enc}(X)$; the only other candidates are the final marker and the junction $\mathtt{enc}(X)\cdot\sigma_1\sigma_1$, and the junction is an occurrence exactly when $\mathtt{enc}(X)$ ends with $\sigma_1$, i.e.\ when $X$ ends with $\sigma_1$ (F3).

    If $X = \epsilon$, the pattern is $\sigma_1\sigma_1$ and $T = \sigma_1\sigma_1$: deleting the marker leaves $\epsilon$, and $\mathtt{dec}(\epsilon) = \epsilon$.

    If $X = a$ is a single character, the pattern is again $\sigma_1\sigma_1$ and $T = \mathtt{enc}(a)\,\sigma_1\sigma_1$. For $a \neq \sigma_1$ the junction is not an occurrence and the only match is the marker; deleting it leaves $\mathtt{enc}(a)$. For $a = \sigma_1$ we have $T = \sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_1$ and the \emph{leftmost} occurrence of $\sigma_1\sigma_1$ is the junction one, at position $1$; deleting it still leaves $\sigma_2\sigma_1 = \mathtt{enc}(a)$. In both cases $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(a)) = a$.

    Finally let $X = aS'$ with $S' \neq \epsilon$, so the pattern is $Q = \mathtt{enc}(S')\,\sigma_1\sigma_1$ and $T = \mathtt{enc}(a)\,\mathtt{enc}(S')\,\sigma_1\sigma_1$. The $\sigma_1\sigma_1$-suffix of any occurrence of $Q$ must sit at the marker or at the junction, so the occurrence starts at $|\mathtt{enc}(a)|$ or at $|\mathtt{enc}(a)|-1$. The first candidate is a real match: $Q$ is exactly the suffix of $T$ of length $|Q|$. The second is impossible:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item if $a = \sigma_1$, so $\mathtt{enc}(a) = \sigma_2\sigma_1$, the occurrence would start at the $\sigma_1$ inside $\mathtt{enc}(a)$, but $Q$ begins with $\mathtt{enc}(S')[0] \neq \sigma_1$ by (F2);
        \item if $a \neq \sigma_1$, so $\mathtt{enc}(a) = a$, the occurrence would start at position $0$, i.e.\ $Q$ would be a prefix of $T$. Comparing characters gives $a = \mathtt{enc}(S')[0]$ and $\mathtt{enc}(S')[j] = \mathtt{enc}(S')[j-1]$ for $1 \le j < |\mathtt{enc}(S')|$, so $\mathtt{enc}(S') = a^{|\mathtt{enc}(S')|}$; but then the next compared character is $\mathtt{enc}(S')$'s last, $a \neq \sigma_1$, against $Q$'s $\sigma_1$ -- a mismatch.
    \end{itemize}
    Hence the leftmost (and only) occurrence of $Q$ in $T$ is the suffix one; the deletion leaves $\mathtt{enc}(a)$, and $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(a)) = a$.
\end{proof}

Equality of strings is definable too. The idea: frame each side with border markers, and let one substitution test whether the frames coincide.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Border Encoding Decoding})
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{benc}_{b, x}(X) & \triangleq xb^2\,\mathtt{enc}_{b, x}(X)\,xb^2 \\
        \mathtt{bdec}_{b, x}(X) & \triangleq [\epsilon/xb^2]X
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
    The border marker is again of the form $xb^k$, $k \ge 2$: it contains $bb$, so it never occurs inside an $\mathtt{enc}$-image, and it begins with $x$, so an occurrence straddling a junction adjacent to an image would need the image to end in $bb$, which it never does.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Border Decoding})
    $\mathtt{bdec}(\mathtt{benc}(X)) = \mathtt{enc}(X)$, and the only occurrences of $xb^2$ in $\mathtt{benc}(X)$ are the two borders.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    The pattern $xb^2$ contains $bb$, so it cannot occur inside $\mathtt{enc}(X)$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:enc}(ii)); nor can it straddle the junction $\mathtt{enc}(X)\cdot xb^2$: an occurrence starting inside the image would need its two $b$'s at the image's end, impossible, and one starting at the border's $x$ is the border itself. Hence the greedy scan of $[\epsilon/xb^2]$ deletes exactly the two borders.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Border Injectivity})
    $\mathtt{benc}(Y) \subset \mathtt{benc}(X) \Leftrightarrow X = Y$.
    \label{lem:benc injectivity}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    ($\Leftarrow$) Immediate. ($\Rightarrow$) By the Border Decoding Lemma, the only occurrences of $xb^2$ in $\mathtt{benc}(X)$ are its two borders. An occurrence of $\mathtt{benc}(Y) = xb^2\,\mathtt{enc}(Y)\,xb^2$ inside $\mathtt{benc}(X)$ must start at an $xb^2$ occurrence: at the left border, its trailing border must then sit at position $3 + |\mathtt{enc}(Y)|$, which by the occurrence analysis is either the right border -- giving $|\mathtt{enc}(Y)| = |\mathtt{enc}(X)|$ and $\mathtt{enc}(Y) = \mathtt{enc}(X)$, hence $Y = X$ ($\mathtt{enc}$ is injective by (i)) -- or inside the image / straddling, both impossible. Starting at the right border, the occurrence would need $3 + |\mathtt{enc}(Y)| + 3$ characters from there, but only $3$ remain.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Equality})
    Choose any two distinct characters $\top, \bot \in \Sigma$. We define equality by:
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{eq}(X,Y) \triangleq [\bot/\mathtt{benc}_{b, x}(X)][\top/\mathtt{benc}_{b, x}(Y)](\mathtt{benc}_{b, x}(X))
    \end{align}
    Then we claim that for any $X,Y$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{eq}(X,Y) = \begin{cases}
                               \top,\quad X=Y \\
                               \bot,\quad X\neq Y
                           \end{cases}
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    If $X = Y$: $[\top/\mathtt{benc}(Y)](\mathtt{benc}(X)) = [\top/\mathtt{benc}(X)](\mathtt{benc}(X)) = \top$ by Direct Substitution (the whole string is the leftmost and only occurrence). Then $[\bot/\mathtt{benc}(X)](\top) = \top$ by Substitution Elimination, since $|\mathtt{benc}(X)| \geq 4 > 1 = |\top|$.

    If $X \neq Y$: by Border Injectivity (contrapositive), $\mathtt{benc}(Y) \not\subset \mathtt{benc}(X)$, so the $\top$-substitution is inert; and $[\bot/\mathtt{benc}(X)](\mathtt{benc}(X)) = \bot$ by Direct Substitution.
\end{proof}

An \texttt{if} selector is definable in the same style:

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Selection})
    Let $\top, \bot \in \Sigma$ be distinct, with $\top \neq b$ (recall $b$ is the first character of the $\mathtt{enc}$ above). Define
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{if}(C,X,Y) \triangleq \mathtt{dec}_{b,x}\big([\mathtt{enc}_{b, x}(Y)/bb]\left([\mathtt{enc}_{b, x}(X)/\top][bb/\bot]C\right)\big)
    \end{align}
    Then we claim that for any $C,X,Y$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{if}(C,X,Y) = \begin{cases}
                               X,\quad C=\top \\
                               Y,\quad C=\bot
                           \end{cases}
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Read the composition right-to-left (Remark~\ref{rem:order}).

    If $C = \top$: the rightmost substitution $[bb/\bot]$ is inert ($\bot \not\subset \top$ since $\top \neq \bot$), leaving $\top$. Then $[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top](\top) = \mathtt{enc}(X)$ by Direct Substitution. Finally $[\mathtt{enc}(Y)/bb](\mathtt{enc}(X)) = \mathtt{enc}(X)$ by Substitution Elimination, since $\mathtt{enc}$-images contain no $bb$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:enc}(ii)). So $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(X)) = X$.

    If $C = \bot$: $[bb/\bot](\bot) = bb$ by Direct Substitution. Then $[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top](bb) = bb$: were it not inert, $bb$ would contain $\top$, forcing $\top = b$ -- excluded. Finally $[\mathtt{enc}(Y)/bb](bb) = \mathtt{enc}(Y)$, and $\mathtt{dec}(\mathtt{enc}(Y)) = Y$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    Both the hypothesis and the order are essential.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \emph{$\top \neq b$ is needed}: if $\top = b$, take $C = \bot$, any $X \neq \epsilon$: then $[bb/\bot](\bot) = bb$ and $[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top](bb) = [\mathtt{enc}(X)/b](bb)$ replaces \emph{both} characters, corrupting the marker for the final substitution.
        \item \emph{The order is load-bearing}: swapping the inner two substitutions to
        \begin{align*}
            [bb/\bot]\,[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top]
        \end{align*}
        breaks the $C = \top$ case as soon as $X$ contains $\bot$: $[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top](\top) = \mathtt{enc}(X)$ is then followed by $[bb/\bot]$, which fires inside $\mathtt{enc}(X)$.
    \end{itemize}
    This is the same phenomenon as Remark~\ref{rem:order}: each substitution in the pipeline assumes the invariants established by the ones to its right.
\end{remark}

Next we construct multiple substitution. Its semantics requires some care: iterating $[Y_1/X_1], [Y_2/X_2], \dots$ sequentially would let later passes rescan inserted text, so the intended semantics \emph{freezes} matches instead:

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Multiple Substitution, Semantics})
    \label{def:rep}
    Fix $n \geq 1$ and nonempty patterns $X_1, \dots, X_n$. The multiple substitution
    \begin{align*}
        \mathtt{rep}_n(S;\, X_1,Y_1;\,\dots;\,X_n,Y_n)
    \end{align*}
    is defined by \emph{freezing}: scan $S$ from the left for occurrences of $X_1$ -- leftmost-first, non-overlapping, as in Definition~\ref{def:subst} -- and \emph{freeze} the matched positions; then scan the unfrozen positions for occurrences of $X_2$ and freeze; and so on up to $X_n$. (A match is admissible only if it lies entirely in unfrozen positions; each scan is greedy leftmost-first.) Finally, replace every frozen occurrence of $X_i$ by $Y_i$, simultaneously.
\end{definition}

We now compute $\mathtt{rep}_n$. The construction encodes the input so that patterns, frozen positions, and inserted text cannot interfere: every character $c$ is escaped as the two-character block $xc$ -- a \emph{comma} in front of every character, whence the name. The code is a uniform prefix code -- all code words have length $2$ -- in the sense of the theory of codes \cite{berstel85}.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Comma Code})
    Let $x \in \Sigma$ and fix an enumeration $\Sigma \setminus \{x\} = \{c_1, \cdots, c_M\}$ (the order is immaterial). Define
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(S) & \triangleq [xc_1/c_1]\cdots[xc_M/c_M][xx/x]\,S \\
        \mathtt{dec}^2_{x}(S) & \triangleq [x/xx][c_M/xc_M]\cdots[c_1/xc_1]\,S
    \end{align}
    (right-to-left as always: in $\mathtt{enc}^2$ the doubling pass $[xx/x]$ runs \emph{first}, so that the commas inserted by the later passes are not themselves doubled; in $\mathtt{dec}^2$ the halving pass runs \emph{last}). Every character $c$ is escaped as the block $xc$: all code words have length $2$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Comma Code})
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(S)$ is the concatenation of the blocks $xS[0],\, xS[1],\, \dots,\, xS[|S|-1]$. In particular every $x$ of an image is the comma of a block, every character other than $x$ is data, and $\mathtt{dec}^2_{x}(\mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(S)) = S$.
        \item An $\mathtt{enc}^2$-image contains no $bb$ and never begins with $b$. Consequently, in a concatenation of $\mathtt{enc}^2$-images and markers $xb^{i+1}$ ($i \geq 1$), every occurrence of a marker starts at the head of one of the markers and is a prefix of it -- the whole marker if the two have the same length, and no occurrence at all if it is longer than the marker it starts at (whatever follows a marker begins with $x$, or is the end).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    (i) For $\mathtt{enc}^2$: after the doubling pass the text is a concatenation of units, one per character of $S$ -- the block $xx$ for each $x$, a bare $c$ for each $c \neq x$ -- and each pass $[xc/c]$ replaces precisely its own bare units $c$ by the block $xc$: a single-character pattern never straddles a boundary, and no other unit contains $c$ (a processed block $xc'$ holds $c' \neq c$, and $xx$ holds none), while inserted text is never rescanned. At the end every unit is a block $xc$, which is the claim. For $\mathtt{dec}^2$: in an image every $x$ is a comma, so a pattern $xc$ ($c \neq x$) can only match at a comma, i.e.\ exactly at the blocks with data $c$ (a data character would have to equal the pattern's first character $x$, and a data $x$ is followed by a comma, not by $c$). Each pass collapses its own blocks to bare characters, which no later pass touches, and after all of them the text is $S$ with each $x$ doubled; the final pass $[x/xx]$ halves it back, the $x$-runs being exactly concatenations of the intact $xx$-blocks.
    (ii) Every $b$ of an image is a data character immediately followed by the next block's comma, so $b$-runs have length $\leq 1$. A marker contains $bb$, so it cannot occur inside an image; it begins with $x$, so an occurrence straddling a junction adjacent to an image would need its $b$'s from the image's end -- impossible. An occurrence starting at a marker's head reads along that marker's $b$-run, which ends with the marker (an image or another marker follows, both beginning with $x$, or the text ends); so the occurrence is a prefix of the marker, whole exactly when the lengths agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Multiple Substitution})

    Let $n \in \mathbb{N}^*$, let $b, x \in \Sigma$ with $b \neq x$, and let $X_1, \dots, X_n \neq \epsilon$ be arbitrary patterns. With the abbreviations $E_Z \triangleq \mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(Z)$ and $m_i \triangleq xb^{i+1}$, we define the following functions:
    \begin{multline}
        \mathtt{rep}_n(S, X_1, Y_1, \cdots, X_n, Y_n) \triangleq \\
        \mathtt{dec}^2_{x}\left((\prod_i^{1\rightarrow n} [E_{Y_i}/m_i])(\prod_i^{1\leftarrow n} ([E_{X_i}b/m_{i+1}][m_i/E_{X_i}]))\,E_S\right)
    \end{multline}
    (recall the products compose right-to-left: the renaming passes run $i = 1, 2, \dots, n$, the instantiation passes run $i = n, \dots, 1$). Then for all $S, Y_1, \dots, Y_n$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{rep}_n(S, X_1, Y_1, \cdots, X_n, Y_n) \;=\; \mathtt{rep}_n(S;\, X_1,Y_1;\,\dots;\,X_n,Y_n).
    \end{align}
    No restriction on the patterns is needed beyond $X_i \neq \epsilon$. \label{lem:multiple substitution}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Write $E = E_{X_i}$ for the round-$i$ pattern and $k = |X_i|$. Call an intermediate text \emph{normal} if it is a concatenation of \emph{fragments} ($\mathtt{enc}^2$-images) and markers $m_1, \dots, m_j$, every marker followed by a non-$b$ character or the end. Its \emph{skeleton} is the string read off from the data characters of its fragments. We show by induction on $i$ that after round $i$ (the composition $[E b/m_{i+1}][m_i/E]$) the text is normal, its skeleton being $S$ with exactly the matches frozen by Definition~\ref{def:rep} through round $i$ replaced by markers $m_i$. By the Comma Code Lemma, fragments have $b$-runs $\leq 1$, and every occurrence of a marker starts at a marker's head.

    \emph{Classification of matches.} $E$ begins with the comma $x$, so an occurrence of $E$ in a normal text starts at ($\alpha$) a block comma, ($\beta$) a data character, or ($\gamma$) a marker's $x$ (deeper marker positions hold $b \neq E[0]$).
    \begin{itemize}
        \item ($\alpha$) Reading from a comma, $E$ alternates commas and data. If the next $k$ blocks are intact and spell $X_i$, the occurrence is \emph{genuine}: a run of $k$ skeleton characters spelling $X_i$ (comma-aligned blocks decode to characters, and $E_W = E$ implies $W = X_i$ by injectivity of $\mathtt{enc}^2$, i.e.\ the Comma Code Lemma (i)). Otherwise the read runs off the fragment's end into a marker: $E$'s comma slot meets the marker's $x$ and its final data slot meets the marker's first $b$ -- possible only when $X_i$ ends with $b$ -- the match covering $(k-1)$ intact blocks plus the marker's first two characters. Such a match is \emph{spurious}, and is followed by the marker's second $b$ (markers have at least two).
        \item ($\beta$) A data character $d$ must equal $E[0] = x$; then $E[1] = x_1$ meets the next comma $x$, forcing $x_1 = x$, and inductively $X_i = x^k$, so $E = (xx)^k$ is all $x$'s. But then the comma one position earlier also starts an occurrence (it is an $x$, followed by the same $2k-1$ characters), and the greedy scan reaches a data position only across the comma before it, which must just have failed to match -- whereas a ($\beta$)-occurrence at $d$ implies an occurrence at that comma, which would have matched and consumed $d$. So ($\beta$)-occurrences are never taken: they are \emph{shadowed}.
        \item ($\gamma$) $E[1] = x_1$ meets the marker's first $b$, forcing $x_1 = b$; $E[2]$ is a comma slot and meets the marker's second $b$ -- dead unless $k = 1$, i.e.\ $X_i = b$ and $E = xb$, which matches the first two characters of every marker: \emph{spurious}, followed by the marker's next $b$.
    \end{itemize}
    No occurrence crosses a marker beyond its first two characters, and no genuine match starts inside a marker.

    \emph{The rename $[m_i/E]$.} The genuine matches are exactly the comma-aligned, within-one-fragment occurrences spelling $X_i$ -- that is, the occurrences of $X_i$ among the unfrozen skeleton runs, which cannot straddle a frozen site (frozen sites are markers, and crossing one makes the occurrence spurious or dead). The greedy scan takes them leftmost-first, resuming after each insertion at the next comma (the next skeleton position): non-overlapping, in text order -- precisely the round-$i$ freezing of Definition~\ref{def:rep}, applied to the skeleton furnished by the induction hypothesis. \emph{No spurious match can shadow a genuine one}: ($\beta$) is never taken; ($\gamma$) covers only marker characters, and the first comma after the marker lies beyond it; an ($\alpha$)-spurious match covers $(k-1)$ blocks immediately followed by a marker, and from any of those blocks a genuine match would need $k$ intact blocks -- fewer than $k$ remain before the marker. So the scan takes the spurious matches only where they overlap no genuine match. After a spurious match the scan resumes inside the eaten marker's $b$'s (dead for $E$, which begins with $x$) and rejoins the next comma without loss: those $k-1$ blocks cannot host a round-$i$ match start. Genuine markers are followed by non-$b$ (a comma, a marker's $x$, or the end); spurious ones by at least one $b$ -- the remains of the eaten marker.

    \emph{The repair $[E b/m_{i+1}]$.} The pattern $m_{i+1} = xb^{i+2}$ occurs in the renamed text exactly at the spurious markers followed by their first remaining $b$: an occurrence must begin at a marker's $x$ (fragments have $b$-runs $\leq 1$); an older marker $m_j$, $j \leq i$, supplies only $j + 1 \leq i + 1$ $b$'s and is followed by a non-$b$; and a genuine $m_i$ is followed by a non-$b$. The replacement $E b$ restores the text exactly: in the ($\alpha$) case $E b$ spells the $(k-1)$ blocks, the eaten marker's $x$, and one $b$ (recall $X_i$ ends with $b$), so followed by the marker's remaining $b$'s it reproduces the eaten text character for character; in the ($\gamma$) case ($X_i = b$) it spells $x$ and two $b$'s, again exactly the eaten text. The text is normal again, with markers $m_1, \dots, m_i$ at exactly the round-$i$ frozen sites.

    \emph{The instantiation $[E_{Y_i}/m_i]$, $i = n, \dots, 1$.} At pass $i$ the remaining markers are $m_1, \dots, m_i$, and $m_i$ is the longest. By the Comma Code Lemma (ii) every occurrence of $m_i$ starts at a marker head and is that marker's prefix, hence (nothing longer remains) the whole marker; so the pass replaces exactly the round-$i$ markers by the images $E_{Y_i}$, which are fragments and hence undisturbed by the later passes (whose patterns contain $bb$).

    \emph{Decoding.} The final text is $\mathtt{enc}^2$ of the skeleton with each frozen occurrence of $X_i$ replaced by $Y_i$ -- that is, $\mathtt{enc}^2(Z)$ with $Z$ the right-hand side of Definition~\ref{def:rep} -- and $\mathtt{dec}^2$ returns $Z$. The only pass patterns that can be empty are the $E_{X_i}$, exactly when $X_i = \epsilon$: the undefinedness of the expression matches that of the semantics.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:comma}
    The \emph{uniformity} of the code is what makes the construction work. Running the same architecture over the non-uniform code $\mathtt{enc}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:enc} ($xb$ for $b$, bare $c$ for $c \neq b$) instead fails: code words of different lengths let an image of a pattern start at a unit boundary and end \emph{mid-unit} -- at an escape $x$ -- and such a spurious occurrence can precede and overlap a genuine one, which the scan then never reaches (it does not restart inside inserted text). Concretely, with $b = \sigma_1$, $x = \sigma_2$, $S = \sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2$, $X_1 = \sigma_1\sigma_2$, $Y_1 = \sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_1$, $X_2 = \sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_2$, $Y_2 = \sigma_1\sigma_1$, the $\mathtt{enc}$-based pipeline returns $\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2$ instead of $\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_2\sigma_1$; the condition ``every $X_i$ is a single character or does not end with $x$'' rules out such patterns and is essential for that variant (the exact characterization of the pattern families for which it is sound remains open). In the comma code all code words have length $2$: the parity is locked, every occurrence starting at a unit boundary is genuine (or runs off the fragment's end into a marker, where the uniform repair catches it), and every misaligned occurrence is preceded, one position earlier, by an overlapping aligned one -- the shadowing failure is structurally impossible.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:total-rep}
    As a partial function, $\mathtt{rep}_n$ is undefined when some $X_i = \epsilon$ (the expression itself is undefined: its renaming pattern $\mathtt{enc}^2(X_i)$ is empty). A \emph{total} variant, agreeing with Definition~\ref{def:rep} wherever the latter is defined and treating empty patterns as inert, does exist -- but not by the obvious guard: since the calculus evaluates eagerly (Definition~\ref{def:den}), an $\mathtt{if}(\mathtt{eq}(X_i,\epsilon), \text{identity}, \text{round}_i)$ still \emph{evaluates} $\text{round}_i$, whose pattern is empty, and is itself undefined. The guard must be applied to the \emph{pattern}. Replace each renaming pattern $E_{X_i}$ by
    \begin{align}
        E_{X_i} \cdot G_i, \qquad G_i \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{eq}(X_i, \epsilon),\; m_{n+3},\; \epsilon\big),
    \end{align}
    where $m_{n+3} = xb^{n+4}$. All sub-expressions here are total: the patterns of $\mathtt{if}$ are the nonempty constants $\top$ and $bb$, those of $\mathtt{eq}$ are the bordered images $xb^2\,\mathtt{enc}(X_i)\,xb^2$ (never empty), and that of $\mathtt{dec}$ is $xb$. If $X_i \neq \epsilon$ then $G_i = \epsilon$ and the pattern is exactly $E_{X_i}$, so the round behaves as before; if $X_i = \epsilon$ the pattern is the whole $m_{n+3}$, which at that moment occurs nowhere (the text is normal with markers $m_1, \dots, m_{i-1}$ only, and every marker head supplies at most $i \leq n$ consecutive $b$'s, fewer than $n+4$), so the round is inert -- and its repair pass is inert too, the text then containing no marker followed by a $b$. \emph{Verified} ($\Sigma = \{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}$ and $\{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b},\mathtt{c}\}$, all rule sets with $n \leq 3$ containing at least one empty pattern, patterns and arguments of length $\leq 1$ and $\leq 4$ respectively: $317{,}855$ evaluations, agreement with the skip-empty-patterns semantics in all of them).
\end{remark}

Multiple substitution also subsumes the earlier constructions, as two quick examples show.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Concatenation via Multiple Substitution})
    Let $X,Y$ be two strings, choose any $a\neq b$ in $\Sigma$, define
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{cat}(X,Y) \triangleq \mathtt{rep}_{2}(ab, a, X, b, Y)
    \end{align}
    the usual string concatenation.
    For any string $X,Y$, we have
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{cat}(X,Y) = XY
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Both patterns $a$ and $b$ are single characters, and Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} places no restriction on the patterns, so it applies for \emph{any} choice of the encoding characters $b, x$ -- including $a = x$ or $a = b$, which matters when $|\Sigma| = 2$. By Definition~\ref{def:rep}, the first round freezes the occurrence of $a$ at position $0$ of $ab$; the second round freezes the occurrence of $b$ at position $1$ (still unfrozen). Replacing gives $X\,Y$.
\end{proof}

Finally we define character escaping in general. The concept needed is a pair of \emph{escaped} and \emph{escaping} alphabets.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Escaping Function})
    An \emph{escaping function} of $\Sigma$ is a bijection $f: U \rightarrow V$ between two nonempty subsets $U, V \subset \Sigma$ that has a fixed point: $f(x_0) = x_0$ for some $x_0 \in U$. Then $U$ is the \textbf{escaped alphabet} of $f$ and $V$ its \textbf{escaping alphabet}.
    \label{def:escaping}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Character Escaping})
    \label{thm:escape}

    Let $f$ be an escaping function of $\Sigma$ (Definition~\ref{def:escaping}) with escaped alphabet $U=\{u_1,\cdots,u_n\}$; without loss of generality $f(u_1) = u_1$ (the enumeration of $U$ is chosen so that the fixed point comes \emph{first}). Let $b, x \in \Sigma$ with $b\neq x$ be the characters of the substitution construction. We define:
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{escape}_f(S)   & \triangleq \mathtt{rep}_n(S,u_1,u_1f(u_1),\cdots, u_n,u_1f(u_n))  \\
        \mathtt{unescape}_f(S) & \triangleq \mathtt{rep}_n(S,u_1f(u_1),u_1,\cdots, u_1f(u_n), u_n)
    \end{align}
    Then, for any string $S$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{unescape}_f(\mathtt{escape}_f(S)) = S
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    By Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} the two $\mathtt{rep}$ expressions compute their freezing semantics, so we argue purely in the semantics of Definition~\ref{def:rep}.

    \emph{Step 1: what $\mathtt{escape}$ does.} Its patterns $u_1, \dots, u_n$ are distinct single characters, so each character of $S$ lying in $U$ is frozen, in its own round, and every other character is untouched. Hence
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{escape}_f(S) \;=\; S\text{ with every } u_i \text{ replaced by } u_1 f(u_i),
    \end{align}
    simultaneously. Call each block $u_1f(u_i)$ a \emph{pair}; the pair of $u_1$ is $u_1u_1$.

    \emph{Step 2: every $u_1$ of $\mathtt{escape}_f(S)$ begins a pair, except the second character of a $u_1u_1$-pair.} The characters of $\mathtt{escape}_f(S)$ are either untouched characters (none of which is in $U$, in particular none is $u_1$), pair-heads $u_1$, or second characters $f(u_i)$. A second character equals $u_1$ iff $f(u_i) = u_1 = f(u_1)$, i.e.\ $i = 1$ by injectivity: only the $u_1u_1$-pair's second character.

    \emph{Step 3: $\mathtt{unescape}$ inverts.} Its first-round pattern is $u_1f(u_1) = u_1u_1$. By Step 2, occurrences of $u_1u_1$ start at pair-heads: a pair-head $u_1$ followed by $f(u_i) = u_1$ -- exactly the $u_1u_1$-pairs -- or the second character of a $u_1u_1$-pair followed by the next pair's head. The greedy leftmost scan takes the former first, and resuming after it destroys the latter (a chain $u_1u_1u_1u_1\cdots$ of consecutive $u_1u_1$-pairs is consumed pairwise). So round 1 freezes exactly the $u_1u_1$-pairs.

    Round $i \geq 2$ has pattern $u_1 f(u_i)$. In the unfrozen text, an occurrence must start at a pair-head $u_1$ (the only unfrozen $u_1$'s, by Step 2), followed by $f(u_j)$ with $f(u_j) = f(u_i)$, i.e.\ $j = i$ by injectivity: exactly the $i$-pairs. Occurrences straddling pair boundaries or frozen regions are inadmissible.

    Finally, replacing every frozen pair $u_1f(u_i)$ by $u_i$ and leaving untouched characters alone reconstructs $S$ exactly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{The Right End})
    \label{prop:last}
    Over any $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$, the functions $\mathtt{last}(X)$ (the final character; $\epsilon$ on $\epsilon$) and $\mathtt{init}(X) \triangleq X$ without its final character are $L$-expressible; so is the right rotation $\mathtt{cat}(\mathtt{last}(X), \mathtt{init}(X))$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Plant a fresh anchor at the right end and test it with constant patterns. Let $A = bb$ and $T = \mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(X) \cdot A$: by the Comma Code Lemma (ii) no $\mathtt{enc}^2$-image contains $bb$, so $A$ occurs in $T$ only at the end, and for each character $\sigma$ the constant pattern $P_\sigma \triangleq x\sigma bb$ occurs in $T$ only at the junction -- which it does exactly when the final block of $\mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(X)$ is $x\sigma$, that is, when $\mathtt{last}(X) = \sigma$. Occurrence of a constant is decidable by one pass: $[\,c/P_\sigma\,]\,T = T$ iff $P_\sigma$ does not occur (a firing pass rewrites the occurrence to $c \neq P_\sigma$, changing the text), so $\mathtt{eq}$ turns it into a condition, and $\mathtt{last}$ is the $\mathtt{if}$-chain over $\Sigma$ on these tests. For $\mathtt{init}$, take the matching branch: the pass $[\,\epsilon/P_\sigma\,]\,T$ deletes the final block together with the anchor -- each $P_\sigma$ contains $bb$, so at most one fires -- and $\mathtt{dec}^2$ of the result is $X$ without its last character. \emph{Verified} ($\Sigma = \{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}$, all $511$ strings of length $\leq 8$: $\mathtt{last}$, $\mathtt{init}$ and the rotation exact on all).
\end{proof}

\section{The Substitution Calculus}
\label{sec:calculus}

Section~2 built, one by one, a repertoire of functions -- concatenation, head and tail, equality, selection, multiple substitution, escaping -- out of the single operation $[A/B]$. This section formalizes that activity itself: what does it mean for a function to be \emph{constructible from substitution alone}? The $\mathtt{rep}_n$ of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} and the $\mathtt{cat}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:cat} will fall out as instances of the general notion.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Substitution Expressions})
    \label{def:exp}
    For $n \geq 0$, the set $\mathrm{Exp}_n$ of \emph{$n$-ary substitution expressions} over variables $X_1, \dots, X_n$ is defined inductively:
    \begin{mathpar}
        \inferrule{1 \le i \le n}{X_i \in \mathrm{Exp}_n} \and
        \inferrule{W \in \Sigma^*}{W \in \mathrm{Exp}_n} \and
        \inferrule{P, R, E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n}{[\,R/P\,]\,E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n} \and
        \inferrule{E_1, E_2 \in \mathrm{Exp}_n}{E_1\,E_2 \in \mathrm{Exp}_n}
    \end{mathpar}
    A \emph{core} expression is one without $E_1E_2$ nodes. We write $E[F_1/X_1, \dots, F_n/X_n]$ for the (capture-free -- there are no binders) replacement of each variable $X_i$ by the expression $F_i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Denotation})
    \label{def:den}
    $\llbracket E \rrbracket : (\Sigma^*)^n \rightharpoonup \Sigma^*$ is the partial function defined by
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket X_i \rrbracket(S_1, \dots, S_n) & = S_i \\
        \llbracket W \rrbracket(S_1, \dots, S_n) & = W \\
        \llbracket [\,R/P\,]\,E \rrbracket(S_1, \dots, S_n) & = [\,\llbracket R \rrbracket(\vec S)/\llbracket P \rrbracket(\vec S)\,]\,\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S) \\
        \llbracket E_1 E_2 \rrbracket(S_1, \dots, S_n) & = \llbracket E_1 \rrbracket(\vec S)\,\llbracket E_2 \rrbracket(\vec S)
    \end{align}
    where the third line is \emph{undefined} when $\llbracket P \rrbracket(\vec S) = \epsilon$ (recall $[A/\epsilon]$ is undefined), and undefinedness propagates.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Reachable Functions})
    \label{def:reachable}
    A total function $f : (\Sigma^*)^n \rightarrow \Sigma^*$ is \emph{reachable} if $f = \llbracket E \rrbracket$ for some $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n$. A partial function is \emph{reachable} if it is the restriction of $\llbracket E \rrbracket$ to a subdomain of its domain of definition, for some $E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Composition / $\beta$})
    \label{lem:beta}
    For $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n$ and $F_1, \dots, F_n \in \mathrm{Exp}_m$,
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket E[F_1/X_1, \dots, F_n/X_n] \rrbracket(\vec T) \;=\; \llbracket E \rrbracket\big(\llbracket F_1 \rrbracket(\vec T), \dots, \llbracket F_n \rrbracket(\vec T)\big),
    \end{align}
    whenever the right-hand side is defined. If moreover $X_i$ occurs in $E$ while $\llbracket F_i \rrbracket(\vec T)$ is undefined, then the left-hand side is undefined as well; in particular, if every $X_i$ occurs in $E$, the two sides are undefined together and the equation holds as an identity of partial functions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$, proving the equation and the strictness clause together. The equation, for defined right-hand sides, is immediate for $X_i$ and $W$. For strictness: a variable occurs in $E_1E_2$ or in $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ only by occurring in one of the immediate sub-expressions, and both constructors are strict in all their parts (Definition~\ref{def:den}), so the induction hypothesis propagates undefinedness; $W$ contains no variables. For $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ the equation follows because substitution into the three sub-expressions commutes with the operation by construction, and the undefinedness conditions match:
    \begin{align*}
        \llbracket P[F_i/X_i] \rrbracket(\vec T) \;=\; \llbracket P \rrbracket\big(\llbracket F_1 \rrbracket(\vec T), \dots\big)
    \end{align*}
    is $\epsilon$ exactly when the right-hand side's pattern is. The occurrence condition is necessary: for $E = W$ with some $\llbracket F_i \rrbracket(\vec T)$ undefined, the left-hand side is the defined value $W$ while the right-hand side is not.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    \label{cor:closure}
    The reachable functions are closed under composition: if $g$ is $n$-ary reachable and $h_1, \dots, h_n$ are $m$-ary reachable, then so is
    \begin{align*}
        \vec T \;\mapsto\; g\big(h_1(\vec T), \dots, h_n(\vec T)\big).
    \end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Take $E_g[E_{h_1}/X_1, \dots, E_{h_n}/X_n]$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} its denotation agrees with the composite wherever the composite is defined, and it may be defined at points where some $h_i$ is not -- namely when $E_g$ does not contain the corresponding variable. The composite is thus the restriction of this denotation to the subdomain on which every $h_i$ is defined, reachable by the restriction clause of Definition~\ref{def:reachable}. (An exact witness, defined precisely where the composite is, exists: wrap $E_g$ in the identity passes $[\,E_{h_i}\sigma/E_{h_i}\sigma\,]$, one per unused $X_i$; each forces its discarded argument without changing the value, by Identity Substitution.)
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Anchored and Safe Expressions})
    \label{def:safe}
    Define two syntactic predicates by structural recursion:
    \begin{mathpar}
        \mathrm{Anch}(X_i) \equiv \mathrm{false} \and
        \mathrm{Anch}(W) \equiv (W \neq \epsilon) \and \\
        \mathrm{Anch}(E_1E_2) \equiv \mathrm{Anch}(E_1) \vee \mathrm{Anch}(E_2) \and
        \mathrm{Anch}([\,R/P\,]\,E) \equiv \mathrm{false}
    \end{mathpar}
    and $\mathrm{Safe}(X_i) \equiv \mathrm{Safe}(W) \equiv \mathrm{true}$, $\mathrm{Safe}(E_1E_2) \equiv \mathrm{Safe}(E_1) \wedge \mathrm{Safe}(E_2)$, and
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{Safe}([\,R/P\,]\,E) \;\equiv\; \mathrm{Anch}(P) \wedge \mathrm{Safe}(P) \wedge \mathrm{Safe}(R) \wedge \mathrm{Safe}(E).
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Totality})
    \label{thm:totality}
    If $\mathrm{Safe}(E)$, then $\llbracket E \rrbracket$ is a total function; moreover if also $\mathrm{Anch}(E)$, then $\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S) \neq \epsilon$ for all $\vec S$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$, proving both clauses together. For $W$ both are immediate. For $X_i$, totality is immediate and Anch is false. For $E_1E_2$: both components are total by IH, and if either is anchored, its value (nonempty by IH) is a nonempty substring of the product. For $[\,R/P\,]\,E$: $\mathrm{Anch}(P)$ with $\mathrm{Safe}(P)$ makes $\llbracket P \rrbracket(\vec S) \neq \epsilon$ for every $\vec S$ by IH, so the substitution is always defined, and totality of $R$ and $E$ finishes the case.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Concatenation Elimination})
    \label{thm:core}
    For every $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n$ there is a \emph{core} expression $E^\circ \in \mathrm{Exp}_n$ with $\llbracket E^\circ \rrbracket = \llbracket E \rrbracket$ (as partial functions). Hence the concatenation constructor is conservative sugar: it adds no denotations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$; only $E_1E_2$ is nontrivial. Let
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{cat}^\circ \;\triangleq\; [b/xb]\,\Big([\,\mathtt{enc}(X_1)/xb^2\,][\,\mathtt{enc}(X_2)/xb^3\,]\,(xb^2xb^3)\Big) \;\in\; \mathrm{Exp}_2,
    \end{align}
    the expression of Theorem~\ref{thm:cat} written with variables ($\mathtt{enc}(X_i)$ abbreviates the core pipeline $[\,xb/b\,]\,X_i$). By Theorem~\ref{thm:cat}, $\llbracket \mathtt{cat}^\circ \rrbracket$ is the total function $(X, Y) \mapsto XY$. Now set
    \begin{align}
        (E_1E_2)^\circ \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{cat}^\circ\big[E_1^\circ/X_1,\, E_2^\circ/X_2\big],
    \end{align}
    which is core by construction. By Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} and the induction hypothesis,
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket (E_1E_2)^\circ \rrbracket(\vec S) = \llbracket \mathtt{cat}^\circ \rrbracket\big(\llbracket E_1^\circ \rrbracket(\vec S), \llbracket E_2^\circ \rrbracket(\vec S)\big) = \llbracket E_1 \rrbracket(\vec S)\,\llbracket E_2 \rrbracket(\vec S),
    \end{align}
    with undefinedness matching by Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:core} is the precise content of the slogan after Theorem~\ref{thm:cat}: ``we can now directly write $XY$ in the expression.'' It is also exactly the architecture of the \texttt{meow} language: a macro body is an expression, macro expansion is expression substitution, and Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} proves expansion value-preserving -- and definedness-preserving whenever the body uses all its parameters; the gap is exactly macro laziness, an ignored argument never being evaluated.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Instances})
    \label{prop:instances}
    The following are reachable, via the expressions of Section~\ref{sec:toolkit}:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item every constant $\vec S \mapsto W$ and every projection $\vec S \mapsto S_i$;
        \item $\mathtt{enc}_{b,x}$, $\mathtt{dec}_{b,x}$, $\mathtt{cat}$, $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{eq}$, $\mathtt{if}$ (besides $b \neq x$, the constructions need two further characters $\top \neq \bot$ with $\top \neq b$, available since $|\Sigma| \geq 2$: take $\top = x$ if necessary), and, for a fixed escaping function $f$, $\mathtt{escape}_f$ and $\mathtt{unescape}_f$;
        \item $\mathtt{rep}_n$, for each fixed $n$, as a partial function on the domain $\{X_i \neq \epsilon \ \forall i\}$: the only variable patterns in the expression of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} are the renamings' $\mathtt{enc}^2(X_i)$, whose values are empty exactly when $X_i = \epsilon$, so the expression is defined exactly there and computes the semantics of Definition~\ref{def:rep}; a total variant on all of $(\Sigma^*)^{2n+1}$ is given by Remark~\ref{rem:total-rep}.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\section{Power and Limits of Reachable Functions}
\label{sec:limits}

How strong is the substitution calculus? This section gives a pipeline normal form, a polynomial-time soundness theorem, and a first separation.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Pipeline Normal Form})
    \label{prop:pipeline}
    Every core expression is of the form $[\,R_k/P_k\,]\cdots[\,R_1/P_1\,]\,C$ with $C$ a variable or a constant. A core program is thus a \emph{finite pipeline of passes} over an atomic scrutinee, applied right-to-left.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Flatten the spine: the scrutinee of $[\,R/P\,]$ is either atomic, or itself of the form $[\,R'/P'\,]\,E$, in which case the two juxtapose. Iterate.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Duplication Degree})
    \label{def:deg}
    For an expression $E$ define $\deg(E)$ by
    \begin{mathpar}
        \deg(X_i) = 1 \and
        \deg(W) = 0 \and \\
        \deg(E_1E_2) = \max(\deg E_1, \deg E_2) \and
        \deg([\,R/P\,]\,E) = \deg(E) + \deg(R).
    \end{mathpar}
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Length Bound})
    \label{lem:length}
    For every $E$ there is a constant $C_E$ such that for all $\vec S$, writing $M = \max(1, |S_1|, \dots, |S_n|)$,
    \begin{align}
        \big|\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big| \;\le\; C_E \cdot (1+M)^{\deg(E)}
    \end{align}
    whenever the left side is defined.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$. The key step: if $P_0 \neq \epsilon$ then $[\,R_0/P_0\,]\,T$ has length at most $|T| \cdot (1 + |R_0|)$, because the greedy matches of $P_0$ are disjoint, so there are at most $|T|$ of them, each contributing at most $|R_0|$ characters in place of at least one. All remaining cases are routine arithmetic; for $E_1E_2$ use $\deg(E_1E_2) = \max$ and absorb coefficients into $C_{E_1E_2} = C_{E_1} + C_{E_2}$; for $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ chain the bound with $1 + C_R(1+M)^{\deg R} \le (1 + C_R)(1+M)^{\deg R}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Soundness: Reachability Implies Polynomial Time})
    \label{thm:fp}
    Every reachable function is computable in polynomial time; the exponent is bounded by a computable function of a defining expression alone.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Given $E$ with $\llbracket E \rrbracket = f$, use Theorem~\ref{thm:core} to make $E$ core and Proposition~\ref{prop:pipeline} to read it as a pipeline $[\,R_k/P_k\,]\cdots[\,R_1/P_1\,]\,C$. Evaluate right-to-left; every intermediate value is $\llbracket E' \rrbracket(\vec S)$ for a sub-expression $E'$ of $E$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:length} every intermediate has length at most $C(1+M)^d$ with $C, d$ from $E$. A single pass is a left-to-right scan, doable in time linear in (current length) $\times$ (pattern plus replacement length) by the classical pattern-matching machines \cite{knuth77,aho75}, both factors bounded by the same quantity. Summing over the $k$ passes gives time polynomial in $M$, hence in the input size.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Polynomial Growth Is Real})
    The length function in unary, $S \mapsto \sigma^{|S|}$, is reachable (Lemma~\ref{lem:single character substitution}: it is $[\,\sigma/\Sigma\,]\,X_1$). So is $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$: it is
    \begin{align}
        [\,X_1/\sigma\,]\,[\,\sigma/\Sigma\,]\,X_1,
    \end{align}
    of degree $2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    $[\,\sigma/\Sigma\,]\,X_1 = \sigma^{|X_1|}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:single character substitution}; substituting $X_1$ for each $\sigma$ yields $X_1^{|X_1|}$. The degree computation is immediate from Definition~\ref{def:deg}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{An Unreachable Function})
    The function $X \mapsto X^{2^{|X|}}$ is not reachable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Its output length is $|X| \cdot 2^{|X|}$, which eventually exceeds $C \cdot (1+|X|)^d$ for any fixed $C, d$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:length} no expression can produce it on all inputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    So the calculus sits strictly inside the computable functions -- and strictly inside polynomial time in a growth-rate sense. The contrast with \emph{Markov algorithms} \cite{markov54} (rewriting applied until no rule matches) is instructive: unbounded iteration of substitution lifts the system to Turing completeness -- the word problem for semi-Thue systems is undecidable \cite{post47} -- while any bounded pipeline of passes stays polynomial. Where exactly the bounded-pipeline class sits is open:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Give an exact characterization of the reachable functions. With constant patterns, each pass is close to a rational transduction \cite{berstel79,elgot65}; variable patterns (as in $\mathtt{rep}_n$'s renamings) take the class beyond that. Is there a known matching class in the landscape of formal language theory \cite{rozenberg97}?
        \item Is string reversal, $S \mapsto \mathrm{reverse}(S)$, reachable? (The right end is not the obstruction -- $\mathtt{last}$ and $\mathtt{init}$ are reachable, Proposition~\ref{prop:last}; under recursive definitions with lazy passes reversal is a three-line recursion, Section~\ref{sec:recursion}. The question is whether the bounded calculus can \emph{reorder}, not whether it can read.)
        \item Is equivalence of two expressions ($\llbracket E \rrbracket = \llbracket E' \rrbracket$ as partial functions) decidable?
        \item Which functions are reachable over \emph{every} alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$? Does a larger alphabet ever add power (both the $\mathtt{enc}$- and $\mathtt{enc}^2$-constructions must enumerate $\Sigma$)? Section~\ref{sec:alphabet} settles the conjugation-invariant form: up to conjugation by a good coding the reachable class is the same over every alphabet of at least two letters, and no larger alphabet adds power in that sense -- what varies between alphabets is only which \emph{native} functions the conjugates land on.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Variants of the Primitive}
\label{sec:variants}

Definition~\ref{def:subst} made three design choices: the scan proceeds left to right and takes the leftmost match; one pass replaces \emph{all} occurrences; and the scan never re-enters inserted text. Each choice is load-bearing, in a different way. This section varies them one at a time and measures the damage -- to the toolkit of Section~2 and to the class of reachable functions of Section~3.

A \emph{variant calculus} is the grammar of Definition~\ref{def:exp} with the node $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ reinterpreted: sub-expressions still denote by Definition~\ref{def:den}, and the node applies the variant primitive to $\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)$ with pattern $\llbracket P \rrbracket(\vec S)$ and replacement $\llbracket R \rrbracket(\vec S)$, undefinedness propagating as there. $V$-reachability is as in Definition~\ref{def:reachable}; we write $V \sqsubseteq W$ when every $V$-reachable function is $W$-reachable, and $L$ for the calculus of Sections~2--4; $\mathrm{rev}(T)$ is the reversal of $T$. Statements below marked \emph{verified} were confirmed by exhaustive enumeration over the finite domains stated where they occur.

\subsection{One Occurrence at a Time}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Once Substitution})
    \label{def:once}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$ and $C = XBY$ with $|X|$ minimal,
    \begin{align}
        [\,A/B\,]_1\,C \;\triangleq\; XAY,
    \end{align}
    and $[\,A/B\,]_1\,C = C$ when $B \not\subset C$; $[\,A/\epsilon\,]_1$ is undefined. A once-pass makes one splice: it replaces the leftmost occurrence, and only it.
\end{definition}

What dies is everything simultaneous: $[\,xb/b\,]_1$ escapes only the first $b$, so $\mathtt{enc}$, $\mathtt{dec}$, $\mathtt{rep}_n$ and the escape round trip all fail as written. What survives is everything single-site -- and it rebuilds more simply than in $L$, because ``replace only the leftmost'' is a built-in form of marker protection.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Doubled Marker})
    \label{lem:doubled}
    Let $X \neq \epsilon$ and $\sigma \in \Sigma$. Every occurrence of $XX\sigma$ in $XXX$ has $\sigma = X[0]$, and the leftmost one is at position $0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    An occurrence at $q$ needs $q + 2|X| + 1 \leq 3|X|$, so $q \leq |X|-1$, and it gives $X^3[q{:}q+2|X|] = X^2$; in particular $X^3[q{:}q+|X|] = X$. But $X^3[q{:}q+|X|]$ is the factor of length $|X|$ of the periodic word $X^3$ starting at offset $q$ -- the rotation of $X$ by $q$. So $X$ equals its own rotation by $q$, which forces $X$ to be a power of the word of length $p = \gcd(q, |X|)$ -- a classical periodicity fact \cite{fine65,lothaire97} -- with $p \mid q$ (and $q > 0$, else there is nothing to prove). Then $\sigma = X^3[q+2|X|] = X[q] = X[0]$, while $XX\,X[0]$ occurs at position $0$ and every occurrence of $X^2$ in $X^3$ other than at $0$ sits at a positive multiple of $p$.
\end{proof}

The point: $XX$ is a marker that is \emph{fresh with respect to $X$ by length} ($|XX| = 2|X| > |X|$) and nevertheless survives whole inside $XXX$. It replaces the $\mathtt{enc}$-based markers wherever a bounded-length fresh marker is needed.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Once Toolkit})
    \label{thm:once-toolkit}
    Over any $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$, with fixed distinct characters $\top, \bot, d$ as required:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{cat}(X,Y) = [\,X/a\,]_1[\,Y/b\,]_1\,(ab)$ for any two characters $a \neq b$;
        \item $\mathtt{tail}(X) = \prod_{\sigma \in \Sigma} [\,\epsilon/XX\sigma\,]_1\,(XXX)$, the factors in any order (at most one can fire);
        \item $\mathtt{head}(X) = [\,\epsilon/\,\mathtt{tail}(X)\cdot X\cdot d\,]_1\,(X\cdot X\cdot d)$, for any character $d$;
        \item $\mathtt{eq}(X,Y) = [\,\top/\,\mathtt{eqind}(X,Y)\,]_1\,(\bot)$, where
        \begin{align}
            \mathtt{eqind}(X,Y) \;\triangleq\; [\,\bot/X\cdot YY\cdot\bot d\,]_1\,(Y\cdot YY\cdot\bot d);
        \end{align}
        \item $\mathtt{if}(C,X,Y) = [\,Y/\bot dXdYd\,]_1[\,X/\top dXdYd\,]_1\,(C\cdot d\cdot X\cdot d\cdot Y\cdot d)$, for $C \in \{\top,\bot\}$.
    \end{enumerate}
    All are once-core, so concatenation is eliminable in the once-calculus: the induction of Theorem~\ref{thm:core} transfers verbatim, using (i).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    (i) The leftmost $b$ of $ab$ is at position $1$, giving $aY$; the leftmost $a$ of $aY$ is at position $0$ -- always the marker, whatever $Y$ contains -- giving $XY$. No $\mathtt{enc}$ is needed, in contrast to Theorem~\ref{thm:cat}. (ii) By Lemma~\ref{lem:doubled}, the factor with $\sigma = X[0]$ fires at position $0$, deleting the first $2|X|+1$ characters of $XXX$ and leaving $X[1:]$; the factors with $\sigma \neq X[0]$ are inert, and after the firing pass the string is shorter than every remaining pattern. For $X = \epsilon$ the scrutinee is $\epsilon$ and every factor is inert. (iii) Write $X = cR$. The pattern $R\cdot X\cdot d$ occurs in the scrutinee $cR\cdot cR\cdot d$ at position $1$, and deleting it leaves $c$. An occurrence at position $0$ forces, compared character by character, $X = c^k$ with $c = d$; then the pattern is $c^{2k}$ and deleting it from $c^{2k+1}$ still leaves exactly one $c$. The pattern is always nonempty (it ends in $d$), including for $X = \epsilon$, where it is $d$ and the scrutinee is $d$. (iv) If $X = Y$, pattern and scrutinee of $\mathtt{eqind}$ coincide and the whole string is the leftmost occurrence, giving $\bot$. If $X \neq Y$: no match can be whole (it would force $|X| = |Y|$ and $X\cdot YY = Y\cdot YY$), a proper match leaves $U\bot V$ with at least two characters, and when the pattern is longer than the scrutinee the pass is inert, leaving the scrutinee -- also of length at least $2$. So $\mathtt{eqind} \notin \{\epsilon, \bot\}$, and the final pass, on the fixed one-character scrutinee $\bot$, fires exactly when its pattern is $\bot$. (v) The pattern $\top dXdYd$ has exactly the length of the scrutinee, so it can occur only at position $0$, and does so iff $C = \top$; the second pass is inert on the result $X$ by length. (Verified: cat, tail and head on all inputs of length $\leq 4$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$ and $\leq 3$ over $|\Sigma| = 3$; eq and if on all inputs of length $\leq 3$ over both; with the head character $d$ ranging over the alphabet and the tail factors in both orders.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Once Invariants})
    \label{thm:once-invariants}
    Let $E$ be a once-expression. For a character $c$, let $\Phi_c(E)$ be the number of $c$'s in the constant leaves of $E$ and $\nu_i(E)$ the number of occurrences of $X_i$ in $E$; let $w(E)$ be the number of variable leaves; write $\#c(T)$ for the number of $c$'s of $T$ and $\mathrm{mr}(T)$ for the longest run of a single character in $T$. Then, whenever defined:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item \emph{(fresh character)} $\#c\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big) \;\leq\; \Phi_c(E) + \sum_i \nu_i(E)\,\#c(S_i)$;
        \item \emph{(max run)} there are constants $\alpha(E), \beta_1(E), \dots, \beta_n(E)$ with
        $\mathrm{mr}\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big) \leq \alpha(E) + \sum_i \beta_i(E)\,\mathrm{mr}(S_i)$;
        \item \emph{(balance)} for characters $x \neq y$ and $\Psi(T) = \#x(T) - \#y(T)$, there are $K(E), v_i(E)$ with $\big|\Psi\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big)\big| \leq K(E) + \sum_i v_i(E)\,\big|\Psi(S_i)\big|$;
        \item \emph{(linear growth)} $\big|\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big| \;\leq\; C_E + w(E)\cdot \max_i |S_i|$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    One induction on $E$; the node case reduces to a computation about a single splice $T = U P_0 V \mapsto U R_0 V$ (with $P_0 \neq \epsilon$):
    $\#c(UR_0V) = \#c(T) - \#c(P_0) + \#c(R_0) \leq \#c(T) + \#c(R_0)$;
    a run of $UR_0V$ is contained in $U$, in $R_0$, in $V$, or straddles a splice point, contributing at most $\mathrm{mr}(U) + \mathrm{mr}(R_0) + \mathrm{mr}(V) \leq 2\,\mathrm{mr}(T) + \mathrm{mr}(R_0)$;
    $\Psi$ is additive over concatenation and $|\Psi(U)| + |\Psi(V)| \leq |\Psi(T)| + |\Psi(P_0)|$, so $|\Psi(UR_0V)| \leq |\Psi(T)| + |\Psi(P_0)| + |\Psi(R_0)|$;
    and $|UR_0V| \leq |T| - |P_0| + |R_0| \leq |T| + |R_0|$.
    Substituting the induction hypotheses for $E$ and $R$ and adding the leaf budgets along the tree gives the coefficient recursions.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    \label{cor:once-sep}
    Over $|\Sigma| \geq 2$, none of the following is reachable in the once-calculus $\mathrm{ONCE}$: replace-all $[\,a/b\,]$, $\mathtt{enc}$, $\mathtt{dec}$, $[\,xx/x\,]$, $S \mapsto \sigma^{|S|}$, $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$, or $\mathtt{escape}_f$ for any escaping function with $|U| \geq 2$. In particular $L \not\sqsubseteq \mathrm{ONCE}$. The separation survives the unary alphabet: over $|\Sigma| = 1$ the items needing two distinct characters vanish and $S \mapsto \sigma^{|S|}$ degenerates to the identity (trivially once-reachable), but $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ is still $L$-reachable -- the expression $[\,X_1/\sigma\,][\,\sigma/\Sigma\,]\,X_1$ of Section~\ref{sec:limits} works verbatim, $[\,\sigma/\Sigma\,]$ being the identity over the unary alphabet -- and is killed by (iv) as before. So $L \not\sqsubseteq \mathrm{ONCE}$ over every finite alphabet, and the once-calculus has growth degree at most $1$ against $L$'s unbounded degree.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Each is killed on a one-parameter input family. $[\,a/b\,]$ maps $b^n \mapsto a^n$ with $\#a = n$ against the constant $\Phi_a(E)$, by (i); likewise $\mathtt{enc}$ on $b^n$ with $\#x = n$. $\mathtt{dec}$ maps $(xb)^n \mapsto b^n$ with $\mathrm{mr} = n$ against input runs of length $1$, by (ii). $[\,xx/x\,]$ maps $x^n y^n \mapsto x^{\lceil n/2\rceil}y^n$ with $|\Psi| = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ against $\Psi = 0$, by (iii). $S \mapsto \sigma^{|S|}$ has $\#\sigma = |S|$ on $\sigma$-free inputs, by (i). $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ is quadratic against (iv). $\mathtt{escape}_f$ maps $u_2^n \mapsto (u_1 f(u_2))^n$ with $\#u_1 = n$ against $0$, by (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Unary Simulation})
    \label{prop:unary-once}
    Restricted to the unary domain $\{b\}^*$, the function $b^n \mapsto b^{n-1}$ -- delete one $b$ -- is $L$-reachable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Let $H \triangleq [\,b/a\,][\,\epsilon/b\,][\,a/bb\,]$ (read right-to-left, as always): on $b^n$ the first pass gives $a^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}b^{\,n \bmod 2}$, the second deletes the residue, the third restores, so $H(b^n) = b^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ -- floor-halving. Now set
    \begin{align}
        D(X) \;\triangleq\; [\,H(X)/H(X)\,b\,]\,X .
    \end{align}
    On $X = b^n$ the pattern $H(X)\,b = b^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1}$ is longer than half the scrutinee, so two disjoint occurrences are impossible and the greedy scan finds at most one, necessarily at position $0$; replacing it by $H(X)$ leaves $b^{n-1}$. For $n = 0$ the pattern is $b$ and the scrutinee $\epsilon$: inert. (Verified for $n \leq 39$.)
\end{proof}

So any proof that the once-primitive is not $L$-reachable must use general position -- on unary, or otherwise anchored, domains, computed needles do the job.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{The Leftmost Deletion, over Two Letters})
    \label{prop:del-leftmost}
    Over $\Sigma = \{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}\}$, ``delete the leftmost $b$'' -- the once-node $[\,\epsilon/b\,]_1$ -- is $L$-reachable. The pipeline
    \begin{align}
        W \;\triangleq\; [\,a/ab\,]\,[\,ab/aa\,]\,[\,b/ba\,]\,[\,ab/b\,]\,[\,aa/a\,]
    \end{align}
    computes $[\,a/b\,]_1$ on every binary string, and $[\,\epsilon/b\,]_1 X = \mathtt{tail}\big(W(X)\big)$ whenever $b$ occurs in $X$; the no-$b$ case is the identity, and the two cases are joined by the selection on an occurrence test (Proposition~\ref{prop:last}'s device).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Read $W$ right to left, tracking the $a$-gaps of $X = a^{g_0}\,b\,a^{g_1}\,b \cdots b\,a^{g_k}$ (either end gap may be empty). The first three passes fix every $b$ in place and act on gap lengths only: $[\,aa/a\,]$ doubles each gap; $[\,ab/b\,]$ prefixes every $b$ with an $a$, adding one to the gap before it; $[\,b/ba\,]$ deletes the $a$ after every $b$ -- its matches are disjoint, one $a$ per $b$ -- subtracting one from the gap after it. Every gap before a $b$ is thereby even except the first, which has no $b$ before it to pay the debit; the trailing gap is odd too, but borders no $b$. The last two passes convert parity into position: $[\,ab/aa\,]$ pairs each maximal $aa$ of a gap into $ab$, so an odd gap before a $b$ leaves a single residue $a$ immediately in front of its $b$, and $[\,a/ab\,]$ collapses the pairs back -- consuming, in particular, the residue together with the first $b$ as one $ab \to a$. The first gap is the odd one, so the first $b$ is replaced by $a$ and nothing else moves: the even gaps pair off exactly, and the trailing residue has no $b$ to pair with. If $b$ does not occur, the doubling and the pairing round-trip ($a^{g} \to a^{2g} \to (ab)^{g} \to a^{g}$) and the middle passes are inert: $W(X) = X$. For the deletion, $[\,a/b\,]_1 X$ begins with the merged leading run $a^{g_0}a$, so one $\mathtt{tail}$ removes exactly the inserted character. (Verified: $W$, as a genuine five-node pipeline, on all $2{,}047$ binary strings of length $\leq 10$ and $400{,}000$ random strings of length $\leq 59$; the guarded deletion on all $511$ binary strings of length $\leq 8$ and $3{,}000$ random strings of length $\leq 39$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    \emph{The mirror and the hinge.} The rightmost variant $[\,A/B\,]_1^{\mathrm{R}}$ (replace the rightmost occurrence) is the rev-conjugate of the once-calculus, and the conjugation of Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation} below applies verbatim. Its anchor is the unconditional Suffix Substitution lemma, $A \neq \epsilon \Rightarrow [\,C/A\,]_1^{\mathrm{R}}(BA) = B\,C$: the rightmost occurrence of $A$ in $BA$ is always the final one. This gives the two-pass \emph{zipper} $\mathtt{cat}(X,Y) = [\,Y/a\,]_1^{\mathrm{R}}[\,X/b\,]_1^{\mathrm{R}}(ba)$. Nothing provable separates the two once-calculi without settling reversal: by conjugation, $L \sqsubseteq \mathrm{ONCE}^{\mathrm{R}}$ iff $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq \mathrm{ONCE}$, and the pair ``delete the leftmost $b$'' / ``delete the rightmost $b$'' are mutual witnesses of one statement. By Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation}, Proposition~\ref{prop:del-leftmost} transfers: over two letters, the rightmost deletion $[\,\epsilon/b\,]_1^{\mathrm{R}}$ is $\mathrm{R}$-reachable -- the mirror of $W$, every constant reversed and the factor order mirrored. Where the rightmost calculus genuinely struggles is destructuring: $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{eq}$, $\mathtt{if}$ are absent from every once-rightmost expression of size $\leq 7$ and every constant-pattern pipeline of depth $\leq 4$ -- deleting a left-anchored variable-length region seems to require computing that region first, which is the very function sought (conjecturally unreachable).

    The central open problem of the variant -- \emph{is the once-primitive $L$-reachable at all?} -- survives Proposition~\ref{prop:del-leftmost} in sharpened form. The earlier negative searches now read correctly: the witness needs depth $5$, beyond the exhaustive ternary search of depth $3$, and the randomized search over $4$--$7$-pass pipelines sampled $0.08\%$ of the depth-$5$ space; the exhaustive meet-in-the-middle search at that depth finds $W$ -- which also explains those searches' closest misses, differing at exactly one position: the junction where the parity residue must land. What remains is twofold. Over $|\Sigma| \geq 3$ the constant needle is open, with a precise obstruction: the parity-to-residue conversion needs gaps of a single character, and every repair scheme searched -- front-marks, migration swaps, sentinel sandwiches, per-letter deletions, comma-coded worlds (cascade-shaped vocabularies to depth $6$, suffix repairs to depth $3$, $1.6$ million pre/post wraps) -- re-creates the first-site selection it was meant to remove. And over every alphabet the \emph{computed} needle is untouched: Proposition~\ref{prop:unary-once} shows computed needles do the job on anchored domains, and no search has found one in general position.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Positional Addressing}

The positional family replaces the scan by an \emph{address}: write at index $i$, or replace the $k$-th occurrence.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Positional Primitives})
    \label{def:pos}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{setAt}(i, c, S) = S[{:}i]\cdot c\cdot S[i{+}1{:}]$ if $0 \leq i < |S|$, and $S$ otherwise: an out-of-range write is the identity. (As in the baseline, totality; and no length smuggling -- an extending write would let a constant index create unbounded length.)
        \item $\mathrm{occ}(S, B)$ is the list of start positions of the greedy leftmost-first, non-overlapping occurrences of $B$ in $S$ -- exactly the scan of Definition~\ref{def:subst} stopped at each hit.
        \item $\mathtt{repOcc}(k, B, A, S)$: with $O = \mathrm{occ}(S, B)$ (undefined for $B = \epsilon$), replace $S[O[k]{:}O[k]{+}|B|]$ by $A$ if $|O| > k$, and return $S$ otherwise. The rank counts in scan order and is re-indexed after each edit; $\mathtt{repOcc}(0, B, A)$ is the once-primitive.
    \end{enumerate}
    The \emph{positional calculus} $\mathrm{POS}$ has terms over variables, constants, $\mathtt{cat}$, $\mathtt{setAt}$ and $\mathtt{repOcc}$, with literal or computed patterns, replacements and characters, and indices that are either literals or the \emph{length} of a subterm's value -- length being the only number a string calculus has.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Positional Toolkit})
    \label{prop:pos-toolkit}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{tail}(X) = \mathtt{repOcc}(0, c, \epsilon, \mathtt{setAt}(0, c, X))$: mark position $0$ with any character $c$, then delete the first $c$. Two literal nodes, total on every alphabet including $|\Sigma| = 1$.
        \item with $P_2 = \mathtt{tail}(\mathtt{tail}(X\,c^3))$,
        \begin{align*}
            \mathtt{head}(X) \;=\; \mathtt{repOcc}\big(0, P_2, \epsilon, \mathtt{repOcc}(0, \mathtt{tail}(X\,c^3), \epsilon, X\,c^3)\big),
        \end{align*}
        total on every alphabet (the scaffold $c^3$ pads the $\epsilon$-edge, where the pattern would otherwise be empty).
        \item consequently $\mathtt{char}_j = \mathtt{head} \circ \mathtt{tail}^j$ and $\mathtt{prefix}_j$ are positional.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    (i) After the write, position $0$ holds $c$ and is necessarily the first $c$; deleting it leaves $X[1:]$; for $X = \epsilon$ both nodes are inert. (ii) Let $V = Xc^3$, so $|V| \geq 3$. The pattern $\mathtt{tail}(V)$ occurs in $V$ at position $1$; it can occur at position $0$ only if $V$ is uniform, and then deleting $c^{k-1}$ from $c^k$ still leaves one $c$: in both cases the inner node computes $\mathtt{head}(V)$, which is $X[0]$ if $X \neq \epsilon$ and $c$ if $X = \epsilon$. The cleanup pattern $P_2$ has length $|X|+1 \geq 2 > 1$ when $X \neq \epsilon$ (inert), and is exactly $c$ when $X = \epsilon$, deleting the residue to $\epsilon$. (Verified: all strings of length $\leq 6$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$, $\leq 5$ over $|\Sigma| = 3$ and $\leq 8$ over $|\Sigma| = 1$ -- and, for the head, $\leq 5$ over $|\Sigma| = 4$ -- every choice of $c$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Positional Invariants})
    \label{thm:pos-invariants}
    Let $E$ be a positional term, $c \in \Sigma$, and let $C_E(c)$ be the number of $c$'s in the constant leaves of $E$.
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item \emph{(single-site alphabet bound)} on inputs whose components all avoid $c$:
        \begin{align*}
            \#c\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec X)\big) \;\leq\; C_E(c),
        \end{align*}
        in every fragment, up to computed indices.
        \item \emph{(linear growth)} there are $V_E, K_E$ with $\big|\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec X)\big| \leq V_E \cdot M + K_E$, where $M = \sum_i |X_i|$.
        \item for terms with \emph{literal} patterns there is $D_E$ with $\big|\llbracket E \rrbracket(X)\big| \geq |X| - D_E$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Each node edits at most one contiguous site, and every subterm value is used exactly once, as a tree -- nothing is duplicated -- and an index value contributes only through its \emph{length}, which is then discarded. (i): a $\mathtt{setAt}$ writes one character; a $\mathtt{repOcc}$ deletes a nonempty block (deletion cannot add $c$'s) and inserts the replacement, whose $c$'s are budgeted by induction; $\mathtt{cat}$ adds the budgets. (ii): the site edit changes the length by at most the replacement's budget, $\mathtt{setAt}$ preserves length, $\mathtt{cat}$ adds. (iii): a literal-pattern $\mathtt{repOcc}$ deletes at most the maximal literal pattern length; $\mathtt{setAt}$ preserves length; $\mathtt{cat}$ adds the deficits.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    \label{cor:pos-sep}
    $L \not\sqsubseteq \mathrm{POS}$, on every alphabet and in every fragment: letter-changing replace-all $[\,b/a\,]$ and $\mathtt{enc}$ violate (i) for $|\Sigma| \geq 2$ (on $a^n$, respectively $b^n$: $\#b = n$, respectively $\#x = n$, against the constant $C_E$), and $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ violates (ii) on every alphabet. Moreover $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{truncate}_j$ and $\mathtt{dec}$ are not reachable with literal patterns, by (iii): each must delete $n - O(1)$ characters on $a^n$, respectively $(xb)^n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Unary Dichotomy})
    \label{thm:unary}
    Over $\Sigma = \{a\}$:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item every positional length function $n \mapsto \big|\llbracket E \rrbracket(a^n)\big|$ is piecewise $\mathbb{Z}$-affine with finitely many pieces; hence halving $[\,a/a^2\,]$ -- a single $L$ node -- is not positional.
        \item for every $j \geq 2$, the term
        \begin{align}
            \mathtt{repOcc}\big(0,\; a,\; \underbrace{\mathtt{tail}(X)\cdots\mathtt{tail}(X)}_{j-1 \text{ factors}}\cdot a^j,\; X\big)
        \end{align}
        maps $a^n$ to $a^{jn}$: replace-all \emph{with growth} is positional.
    \end{enumerate}
    So replace-all dies in the positional calculus exactly when a second letter exists to count.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    (i) On a unary alphabet every string is its length: $\mathrm{occ}(a^q, a^p)$ has $q-p+1$ elements; $\mathtt{repOcc}(k, a^p, a^r, a^q)$ is $a^{q-p+r}$ if $q \geq p+k$ and $a^q$ otherwise; $\mathtt{setAt}$ writes $a$ over $a$; computed indices are themselves piecewise-affine lengths. Inductively each subterm's length is piecewise $\mathbb{Z}$-affine in $n$, with at most one new breakpoint per node, where a fire-condition flips. Halving has slope $1/2$ on infinitely many points. (ii) The replacement has length $(j-1)(n-1)+j$; replacing the first $a$ leaves $(n-1) + (j-1)(n-1) + j = jn$ characters, and on unary strings content is determined by length. (Verified: $j \leq 5$, $n \leq 24$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{The Once Hinge, Positionally})
    \label{thm:pos-hinge}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{setAt}(i, c)$ is $L$-reachable:
        \begin{align}
            \mathtt{setAt}(i,c)(X) \;=\; \mathtt{if}\Big(&\mathtt{eq}\big(\mathtt{tail}^i X, \epsilon\big),\; X,\notag\\
            &\mathtt{cat}\big(\mathtt{cat}(\mathtt{prefix}_i X, c), \mathtt{tail}^{i+1} X\big)\Big),
        \end{align}
        with $\mathtt{prefix}_i$ the $i$-fold product of heads ($i$ fixed, as the index is).
        \item given a marker constant $M$ fresh for $B$, $A$ and the input, $\mathtt{repOcc}(k, B, A) = [\,M/B\,]_1^k\,[\,A/B\,]_1\,[\,B/M\,]_1^k$.
        \item Hence $\mathrm{POS} \sqsubseteq L$ iff the once-primitive is $L$-reachable: (i) and (ii) simulate every positional constructor, and $\mathtt{repOcc}(0, B, A)$ \emph{is} the once-primitive.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    (i) $i \geq |X|$ iff $\mathtt{tail}^i X = \epsilon$, in which case the write is out of range and the identity applies; otherwise the $\mathtt{cat}$ reassembles $X[{:}i]\cdot c\cdot X[i{+}1{:}]$. (ii) Each factor $[\,M/B\,]_1$ replaces the current leftmost $B$ by a marker block; the blocks are never re-matched and no new $B$ can appear at or across one (disjoint alphabets, as in the Independent Substitution lemma), so $k$ factors convert the occurrences $o_0, \dots, o_{k-1}$ into marker blocks, the middle factor addresses $o_k$, and the last $k$ restore. If there are fewer than $k+1$ occurrences, the marking stops early, the middle factor is inert, and the unmarking restores everything -- the identity, matching the out-of-range clause. (iii) is then immediate. (Verified: (i) for $i \leq 3$ on all strings of length $\leq 5$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$, composed from the Section-2 constructions; (ii) for $k \leq 3$ on the same inputs.)
\end{proof}

Equality testing, by contrast, is conjecturally not positional in any fragment: $\mathtt{eq}$ must broadcast a global comparison into a two-character output, while every positional detection mechanism is local -- fire or not, at one site; all searches to depth $3$ over the computed-index node space fail.

The positional constructors address by index and the once-primitive freezes multiplicity at one; between the two sits a family with a node per address. Let $[\,A/B\,]_k$ replace the $k$-th occurrence of $B$ in the left-to-right, non-overlapping enumeration that the full pass itself uses ($k = 1$ is the once-primitive), and let $\mathrm{L}_k$ be the calculus built on the node. Its nodes splice at most once, so the once-bookkeeping survives; what the family adds is a mosaic of incomparable fragments rather than a hierarchy.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{The $k$-th-occurrence family})
    \label{prop:kth}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item \emph{(Invariants transfer.)} The four invariants of Theorem~\ref{thm:once-invariants} hold verbatim for every $\mathrm{L}_k$: the node case of the induction is a computation about a single splice, and a $k$-th-occurrence node performs exactly one. Consequently $L \not\sqsubseteq \mathrm{L}_k$ for every $k$: the witnesses of Corollary~\ref{cor:once-sep} are unreachable exactly as there. (Verified on a $2{,}500$-expression corpus over the mixed single-site family -- anchored, once, and $k$-th-occurrence nodes together, depth $\leq 3$: the generalized two-sided measure bound in $135{,}136$ checks and the four classical invariants, max-run multiplicities included, with zero violations.)
        \item \emph{(A mosaic, not a hierarchy.)} Over the binary alphabet, at bounded depth the constant-pattern fragments are pairwise incomparable and shrink with the rank: the depth-$3$ spaces on the $63$ strings of length $\leq 5$ hold $38{,}959$, $28{,}090$ and $2{,}307$ distinct functions at $k = 1, 2, 3$, and the addressing nodes sit on the diagonal -- $[\,a/b\,]_j$ is reachable in $\mathrm{L}_j$ and in no other $\mathrm{L}_k$, $[\,aa/b\,]_2$ likewise -- while a higher-rank pass fires less often, so addressing power buys no breadth.
        \item \emph{(Versus once: marker freshness.)} By Theorem~\ref{thm:pos-hinge}(ii), $\mathtt{repOcc}(k, B, A) = [\,M/B\,]_1^k\,[\,A/B\,]_1\,[\,B/M\,]_1^k$ whenever a marker letter $M$ is fresh for $B$, $A$ and the input ($18{,}522$ exact agreements over $B \in \{a,b\}^{\leq 3}$, $|A| \leq 2$, $|S| \leq 5$, $k \leq 2$, the marker $c$ over the ternary alphabet). So wherever a letter is spare the question is the once-hinge itself, and no alphabet is tighter than the binary one: there, for inputs using both letters, no marker exists, and the functions $[\,a/b\,]_2$, $[\,a/ab\,]_2$, $[\,aa/b\,]_2$ are absent from the once constant-pattern space of depth $\leq 4$ ($969{,}321$ distinct functions -- the once node $[\,a/b\,]_1$ itself is present at depth $1$), $[\,a/b\,]_2$ and $[\,a/ab\,]_2$ likewise from the once variable-pattern space of depth $\leq 3$ ($3{,}773$). The $\mathrm{ONCE}$-versus-$\mathrm{L}_2$ question thus reduces to marker freshness -- the alphabet-sensitivity of the once-hinge, reappearing on the multiplicity axis.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The multiplicity axis is thus controlled, like the direction axis, by a single reachability question -- but unlike the direction axis it fans out, over the binary alphabet, into a family of pairwise incomparable fragments rather than a single mirror class.

\subsection{The Other Direction}
\label{subsec:direction}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Rightmost Substitution})
    \label{def:r2l}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$ and $C = XBY$ with $|Y|$ \emph{minimal},
    \begin{align}
        [\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} C \;\triangleq\; \big([\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} X\big)\cdot A\cdot Y .
    \end{align}
    The scan proceeds right to left, takes the rightmost occurrence, and resumes to the left of the inserted text; $[\,A/\epsilon\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ is undefined. The resulting calculus is denoted $\mathrm{R}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Rev Duality})
    \label{thm:rev-duality}
    For all $A$, $B \neq \epsilon$, $C$:
    \begin{align}
        [\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} C \;=\; \mathrm{rev}\big([\,\mathrm{rev}A/\mathrm{rev}B\,]\,(\mathrm{rev}C)\big).
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $|C|$. Occurrences of $B$ in $C$ at position $s$ correspond bijectively to occurrences of $\mathrm{rev}B$ in $\mathrm{rev}C$ at position $|C|-|B|-s$, so the rightmost occurrence of $B$ corresponds to the leftmost of $\mathrm{rev}B$. Write $C = XBY$ with $|Y|$ minimal; then $\mathrm{rev}C = \mathrm{rev}Y\cdot\mathrm{rev}B\cdot\mathrm{rev}X$ exhibits the leftmost occurrence of $\mathrm{rev}B$, and Definition~\ref{def:subst} gives $[\,\mathrm{rev}A/\mathrm{rev}B\,](\mathrm{rev}C) = \mathrm{rev}Y\cdot\mathrm{rev}A\cdot[\,\mathrm{rev}A/\mathrm{rev}B\,](\mathrm{rev}X)$. Applying $\mathrm{rev}$ and the induction hypothesis ($|X| < |C|$) gives $([\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}}X)\cdot A\cdot Y$. If $B \not\subset C$ then $\mathrm{rev}B \not\subset \mathrm{rev}C$ and both sides are $C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Conjugation})
    \label{thm:conjugation}
    For an expression $E$ let $\overline{E}$ be its rev-conjugate: every constant $W$ replaced by $\mathrm{rev}W$, every node $[\,R/P\,]$ by $[\,\overline{R}/\overline{P}\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$, the children of every concatenation swapped. Then
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket \overline{E} \rrbracket^{\mathrm{R}}(\mathrm{rev}S_1, \dots, \mathrm{rev}S_n) \;=\; \mathrm{rev}\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(S_1, \dots, S_n)\big),
    \end{align}
    both sides being undefined together. Consequently $\mathrm{rev}$ is $L$-reachable iff it is $\mathrm{R}$-reachable; and if $\mathrm{rev}$ is reachable in either calculus, then $L$ and $\mathrm{R}$ have exactly the same reachable functions.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$: the node case is Theorem~\ref{thm:rev-duality}, the concatenation case is $\mathrm{rev}(XY) = \mathrm{rev}Y\,\mathrm{rev}X$, and undefinedness is preserved because $\mathrm{rev}\,\epsilon = \epsilon$. For the consequences: if $\llbracket E \rrbracket = \mathrm{rev}$, then $\llbracket \overline{E} \rrbracket^{\mathrm{R}}(\mathrm{rev}S) = \mathrm{rev}(\mathrm{rev}S) = S$, so $\overline{E}$ defines $\mathrm{rev}$ in $\mathrm{R}$; the converse is the mirror. If $\mathrm{rev} \in L$, every $\mathrm{R}$-node is $L$-expressible by Theorem~\ref{thm:rev-duality} and closure under composition (Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}), and concatenation is eliminable in both calculi (Theorem~\ref{thm:core}; for $\mathrm{R}$, see Theorem~\ref{thm:r2l-toolkit}), so $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$; the other inclusion uses $\mathrm{rev} \in \mathrm{R}$, equivalent to the first.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{Inclusion Symmetry})
    \label{cor:incl-symmetry}
    $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$ iff $L \sqsubseteq \mathrm{R}$; either is equivalent to $L = \mathrm{R}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Write $m(f)$ for the mirror $\vec S \mapsto \mathrm{rev}\big(f(\mathrm{rev}S_1, \dots, \mathrm{rev}S_n)\big)$. The conjugation of Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation} is a bijection between the two expression sets, so the $\mathrm{R}$-reachable class is exactly the $m$-image of the $L$-reachable class; $m$ is an involution on functions ($m(m(f)) = f$, undefinedness together, since $\mathrm{rev}$ is total and $\mathrm{rev}\,\mathrm{rev} = \mathrm{id}$). Applying $m$ to the inclusion $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$ therefore yields $L \sqsubseteq \mathrm{R}$ -- $m$ carries each class onto the other -- and the converse is the same argument mirrored. Either inclusion says the $L$-reachable class is closed under $m$, which is exactly $L = \mathrm{R}$.
\end{proof}

So the second open problem of Section~4 -- is reversal reachable? -- is direction-independent, and it decides whether $L = \mathrm{R}$. The two inclusions of the landscape's right-to-left row are not two questions but one (Corollary~\ref{cor:incl-symmetry}); what reversal would decide is that one question's \emph{answer}. The question also has a quantitative face, the crossing ladder of Remark~\ref{rem:price}.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Agreement})
    \label{prop:r2l-agree}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$ over an alphabet of at least two letters: $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} = [\,A/B\,]$ for all $A$ and $C$ iff $B$ is unbordered. In particular a single-character pattern never distinguishes the directions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    ($\Leftarrow$) Two overlapping occurrences of $B$ at distance $d < |B|$ would give $B$ a border of length $|B|-d$; unborderedness excludes this, so all occurrences in any $C$ are pairwise disjoint. Neither scan can see a match touching inserted text -- the left-to-right pass resumes at the end of its replacement, the right-to-left pass at its start -- so both select and replace the same disjoint occurrence set, and disjoint local edits commute whatever their order. Induction on $|C|$.
    ($\Rightarrow$) Let $B$ be bordered with smallest period $p$ and set $C = B \cdot B[|B|{-}p:]$, of length $|B| + p$. The occurrences of $B$ in $C$ are exactly $\{0, p\}$: an occurrence at $q$ with $0 < q < p$ would make $q$ a smaller period, and no occurrence fits beyond $p$. The two overlap, so the left-to-right scan takes $0$ and the right-to-left takes $p$, whence for any character $a$,
    \begin{align*}
        [\,a/B\,]\,C \;=\; a \cdot B[|B|{-}p:], \qquad [\,a/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} C \;=\; B[:p] \cdot a,
    \end{align*}
    which differ whenever $a \neq B[0]$. (Over the unary alphabet no disagreement exists at all: all passes commute there, and $L = \mathrm{R} = L{+}\mathrm{R}$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:minwitness}
    Disagreement therefore requires $|B| \geq 2$ and $|C| \geq |B|+1$, and the unique minimal shape up to renaming is $(A, B, C) = (d, c^2, c^3)$ with $d \neq c$:
    \begin{align*}
        [\,b/aa\,]\,\mathtt{aaa} = \mathtt{ba} \qquad\text{versus}\qquad [\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}} \mathtt{aaa} = \mathtt{ab}.
    \end{align*}
    (Exhaustively confirmed: $116$ disagreement triples over $\{a,b\}$ with $|A| \leq 2$, $|B| \leq 3$, $|C| \leq 5$, none smaller; $912$ over three letters, with minimal set exactly $\{(d, c^2, c^3) : d \neq c\}$. For $(B, C) = (c^2, c^3)$ the two values are $A \cdot c$ and $c \cdot A$, which disagree iff $A \notin c^*$.)
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Direction-Robust Toolkit})
    \label{thm:r2l-toolkit}
    The constructions of Section~2 -- $\mathtt{enc}$, $\mathtt{dec}$, $\mathtt{cat}$, $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{eq}$, $\mathtt{if}$ -- compute the same functions under rightmost-first semantics. In particular $\mathtt{enc}^{\mathrm{R}} = \mathtt{enc}$ exactly, and concatenation is eliminable in the right-to-left calculus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Every pass occurring in these constructions is direction-robust in one of three ways: (a) its pattern is a single character (Proposition~\ref{prop:r2l-agree}) -- this covers $\mathtt{enc}$'s pattern $b$ and the $[\,\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top\,]$ pass of $\mathtt{if}$; (b) its pattern is a fixed unbordered constant, of the form $xb^k$ with $x \neq b$ -- covering $\mathtt{dec}$ and the marker passes of $\mathtt{cat}$; (c) its pattern has at most one occurrence in every string arising at that point of the pipeline -- which is exactly what the occurrence analyses of Section~2 establish for the remaining passes of $\mathtt{cat}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{eq}$ and $\mathtt{if}$ (e.g.\ by Border Injectivity, $b\,\mathtt{enc}(Y)$ occurs in $b\,\mathtt{enc}(X)$ iff $X = Y$, and then as the whole string). Note that the variable patterns of (c) need not be unbordered -- $b\,\mathtt{enc}(\epsilon) = xbbxbb$ is bordered -- so it is the occurrence analysis, not the pattern's shape, that carries them; and that analysis is direction-free. A direction-robust pass computes the same function under both primitives; compose pass by pass. (Verified for $|\Sigma| = 2, 3, 4$, under both the left-to-right and right-to-left primitives: all inputs of length $\leq 5$, and of length $\leq 4$ for the $\mathtt{eq}$ and $\mathtt{if}$ scrutinees.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{The Construction is Not Direction-Robust})
    \label{thm:r2l-rep}
    Read with rightmost-first passes, the construction of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} does not compute the rightmost-freezing $\mathtt{rep}$. With $b = \sigma_1$, $x = \sigma_2$, $S = \sigma_2\sigma_1$, $X_1 = \sigma_2$, $Y_1 = \epsilon$, the rightmost-freezing semantics gives $\sigma_1$; the construction gives $\sigma_2\sigma_1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The encoded input is $\mathtt{enc}^2(\sigma_2\sigma_1) = \sigma_2\sigma_2\cdot\sigma_2\sigma_1$, and $E = \mathtt{enc}^2(\sigma_2) = \sigma_2\sigma_2$ occurs in it at position $0$ -- comma-aligned, genuine -- and at position $1$, misaligned, starting at the data character $\sigma_2$. Under leftmost-first the aligned occurrence is taken first and the misaligned one is shadowed; this is the $(\beta)$ case of the phase-locking proof, and the shadowing is a \emph{leftmost} phenomenon. Under rightmost-first the misaligned occurrence is taken first; it overlaps the genuine one, which is therefore never frozen, and the repair stage then restores the text as if nothing had happened. (Over $|\Sigma| = 2$, $n = 1$, all $|S| \leq 5$, $|X_1| \leq 2$, $|Y_1| \leq 2$, both choices of $(b,x)$: 708 disagreements in 5292 instances.)
\end{proof}

The repair is to conjugate the whole construction:

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Mirrored Construction})
    \label{thm:r2l-mirror}
    Replace every constant of the construction by its reversal -- the encoder becomes ``every $c$ \emph{followed} by its comma'', the markers become $b^{i+1}x$, the repair pass becomes $[\,b\,E'_i/b^{\,i+2}x\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$, and so on -- and run it right-to-left. It then computes the rightmost-freezing $\mathtt{rep}_n$, with no restriction on the patterns.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Conjugate Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} by $\mathrm{rev}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation}): the conjugated construction computes
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{rev}\big(\mathtt{rep}_n(\mathrm{rev}S;\,\mathrm{rev}X_i, \mathrm{rev}Y_i)\big),
    \end{align}
    and leftmost-greedy freezing of $\mathrm{rev}X_i$ in $\mathrm{rev}S$ is rightmost-greedy freezing of $X_i$ in $S$. Verified on 16{,}140 instances, with both choices of $(b,x)$ throughout: $n = 1$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$ with all patterns and replacements of length $\leq 2$; $n = 2$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$ with all patterns of length $\leq 2$ and replacements of length $\leq 1$; and $n = 1$ over $|\Sigma| = 3$ with replacements of length $\leq 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Original Constants, Stronger Hypothesis})
    \label{thm:r2l-ends}
    If every $X_i$ ends with a character outside $\{b, x\}$ -- possible iff $|\Sigma| \geq 3$ -- then the construction of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} with its own constants, read right-to-left, computes the rightmost-freezing $\mathtt{rep}_n$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The three spurious classes of the phase-locking proof are excluded by the hypothesis: an $(\alpha)$-spurious match running into a marker needs the last data character of $E_i$ to be $b$; a $(\beta)$-match needs $X_i = x^k$; the $(\gamma)$ case needs $X_i = b$. So every $E_i$-match is genuine, and since for disjoint occurrence sets the two greedy directions select the same set, the rightmost-first scan takes exactly the rightmost-freezing set of round $i$. The repair pass is inert -- its pattern arises only at spurious junctions -- and instantiation replaces whole markers, by the Comma Code Lemma. Verified on 23{,}996 instances over $|\Sigma| = 3$: $n = 1$ with all patterns of length $\leq 2$ ending outside $\{b,x\}$ and all replacements of length $\leq 2$, all three choices of $(b,x)$; $n = 2$ with replacements of length $\leq 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Escape is Direction-Locked})
    \label{prop:escape-lock}
    The pair scheme of Theorem~\ref{thm:escape} must match the scan direction. With the pre-fix pairs $u \mapsto u_1f(u)$ the round trip fails under rightmost-freezing: $U = \{b, x\}$, $f(b) = b$, $f(x) = c$, $u_1 = b$, $S = bx$ gives $\mathtt{escape}(S) = bbbc$ and the round trip returns $bbc$. With the post-fix pairs $u \mapsto f(u)u_1$ it fails under leftmost-freezing: $f(b) = b$, $f(x) = x$, $u_1 = b$, $S = xb$ gives $\mathtt{escape}(S) = xbbb$ and the round trip returns $xbb$. The matched pairings round-trip.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    In each failing case the round-$1$ pattern has an occurrence straddling two pairs that \emph{starts later} than the genuine one; the unmatched direction takes the straddler first, freezing positions that block the genuine pair. The matched pairings are Theorem~\ref{thm:escape} and its rev-conjugate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Constant Fragments are Incomparable})
    \label{thm:subsequential}
    Every constant-pattern core $L$-pipeline over one variable is left-subsequential \cite{berstel79,elgot65}: computed by a deterministic finite-state transducer reading left to right and emitting as it goes, with a final output. The single pass $[\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ is not left-subsequential. Hence $[\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ is computed by no constant-pattern core $L$-pipeline, and, by mirror, $[\,b/aa\,]$ by no constant-pattern core $\mathrm{R}$-pipeline: the two constant fragments are incomparable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    A single pass is realized by the machine whose state is the longest suffix of the text read so far that is a proper prefix of $B$; it emits the finalized part and resets on a match -- this is exactly the scan of Definition~\ref{def:subst}, the classical pattern-matching automaton \cite{knuth77,aho75}. The class is closed under composition: feed the first machine's online output into the second. For $[\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$: on $a^n$ the output is $b^{n/2}$ for even $n$ and $a\cdot b^{(n-1)/2}$ for odd $n$, so the first output character depends on the parity of an unbounded run. A deterministic streaming machine on $a^{\infty}$ either never emits -- its total output is then a bounded final output, contradicting the unbounded lengths -- or makes its first emission at some fixed step $k$ with fixed content; but then on $a^m$ and $a^{m+1}$ with $m \geq k$ the first emission is the same character, while the two outputs begin with different characters.
\end{proof}

Nothing about the direction breaks the complexity theory: the length bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:length} holds with the same degree (the greedy matches are disjoint either way), single passes run in linear time, and the polynomial-time soundness of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} transfers verbatim. Likewise $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ is $\mathrm{R}$-reachable (single-character patterns throughout) and $X \mapsto X^{2^{|X|}}$ is not. What \emph{is} direction-sensitive is the fine structure: the multiple-substitution construction needs its constants mirrored (Theorems~\ref{thm:r2l-rep} and~\ref{thm:r2l-mirror}, or Theorem~\ref{thm:r2l-ends} under a stronger hypothesis), the escape scheme needs its pairs on the correct side (Proposition~\ref{prop:escape-lock}), and the constant fragments are provably incomparable (Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}).

\subsection{Re-entering Inserted Text}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Unsafe Substitution})
    \label{def:rescan}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$, $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{u}} C$ is the process maintaining a frozen prefix $O$ and a work string $T$, initialized $\epsilon$ and $C$: while $B \subset T$, freeze $T[{:}q]$ into $O$ -- where $q$ is the leftmost occurrence of $B$ in $T$ -- and set $T := A\cdot T[q{+}|B|{:}]$. The value is $O\cdot T$. This is Definition~\ref{def:subst} with the single clause ``the scanning never restarts inside inserted text'' \emph{deleted}: the scan re-enters $A$ at its first character. The process may diverge.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Totality})
    \label{thm:rescan-total}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$ is total iff $B \not\subset A$; its divergence domain is exactly $\{C : B \subset A \wedge B \subset C\}$.
        \item When $B \not\subset A$, the number of matches is at most $|C|-|B|+1$, and $\big|[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{u}}C\big| \leq |C|\,(1+|A|)$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    If $B \subset A$ and $B \subset C$, every subsequent work string begins with $A \supseteq B$ and the loop never exits. If $B \not\subset A$: for $k \geq 1$ the work string $T_k$ begins with $A$, and a match at $q_k$ with $q_k + |B| \leq |A|$ would exhibit an occurrence of $B$ inside $A$ -- excluded; so $|T_{k+1}| = |A| + |T_k| - q_k - |B| \leq |T_k| - 1$, while $|T_k| \geq |A|$ always. After the first match there is room for at most $|T_1| - |A| \leq |C| - |B|$ further steps, giving the match bound; the net length change per match is $|A|-|B|$, giving the length bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Exact Agreement})
    \label{thm:rescan-agree}
    $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{u}} = [\,A/B\,]$, as functions of $C$, if and only if $B \not\subset A$ and no nonempty suffix of $A$ -- $A$ itself included -- is a proper prefix of $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    ($\Leftarrow$) Under the two conditions no occurrence of $B$ can start inside an inserted copy of $A$: one lying entirely inside gives $B \subset A$; one starting at the copy's first character and extending past it makes $A$ a proper prefix of $B$; one straddling the copy's end makes a nonempty suffix of $A$ a proper prefix of $B$. So after every insertion the first possible match start is exactly where the safe scan resumes, and induction over match events shows the two processes freeze identical prefixes. ($\Rightarrow$) If $B \subset A$, take $C = B$: the work string begins with $A \supsetneq B$ forever. Otherwise, negating the second condition, some nonempty suffix of $A$ is a proper prefix of $B$; let $i < |A|$ be minimal with $A[i{:}]$ a proper prefix of $B$, and put $\beta = B[|A|-i{:}]$, $C = B\cdot\beta$. The safe pass gives $A\cdot\beta$, the residue $\beta$ being shorter than $B$. The unsafe pass gives the work string $T_1 = A\cdot\beta$, whose leftmost $B$ sits at position $i$ -- none earlier: an occurrence at $j < i$ with $j+|B| \leq |A|$ would exhibit $B \subset A$, and one straddling the splice makes $A[j{:}]$ a proper prefix of $B$, contradicting the minimality of $i$. The match at $i$ consumes $A[i{:}]\cdot\beta = B$, which is exactly the suffix of $T_1$ from position $i$ on, since $i+|B| = |A|+|\beta|$; so the pass freezes $A[{:}i]$ and leaves the work string $T_2 = A$, which is $B$-free by hypothesis -- the process halts, with value $A[{:}i]\cdot A$. If $A[{:}i]\cdot A \neq A\cdot\beta$, the values differ. If they are equal, then $|\beta| = i$ and $\beta$ is the last $i$ characters of $A$, and comparing the middles forces $A$ to have period $i$ \cite{lothaire97}; then $B = A[i{:}]\cdot\beta = A$, contradicting $B \not\subset A$. (Verified as a biconditional: all $A, B, C$ with $|A| \leq 4$, $|B| \leq 3$, $|C| \leq 6$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$, and smaller domains over $|\Sigma| = 3$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{The Encoder is the Load-Bearing Violation})
    \label{cor:rescan-collapse}
    The encoder $\mathtt{enc} = [\,xb/b\,]$ has $B = b \subset xb = A$: under the unsafe primitive it diverges on every $b$-containing input, and likewise every escape pass $[\,xc/c\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$ of the comma code on every $c$-containing one. The whole of Section~2 routes through the encoder, and read with unsafe passes every one of $\mathtt{cat}$, $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{eq}$, $\mathtt{if}$, $\mathtt{rep}_n$ and the escape round trip diverges or miscomputes. The syntactic totality discipline fails as well: $[\,a/a\,]^{\mathrm{u}}X_1$ satisfies $\mathrm{Safe}$ (Definition~\ref{def:safe}) and diverges on every $a$-containing input.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{No Escape from Constant Passes})
    \label{thm:no-escape}
    Every total constant-pattern unsafe pipeline is non-injective. In particular, no per-character encoding $c \mapsto w_c$ is computed by a total constant-pattern unsafe pipeline.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Let the innermost pass be $[\,W_1/P_1\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$ on the scrutinee. Totality forces $P_1 \not\subset W_1$, since the input $P_1$ otherwise diverges by Theorem~\ref{thm:rescan-total}(i). But then the pass maps $P_1$ to $W_1$ -- one insertion, and $W_1$ is $P_1$-free, so the process halts -- and maps $W_1$ to $W_1$, with $P_1 \neq W_1$: two scrutinee values on which the suffix pipeline sees the same string.
\end{proof}

This is the precise content of the non-restart clause: it is exactly what makes an encoder -- a pass whose \emph{replacement contains its pattern} -- possible.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{What Survives})
    \label{prop:rescan-survives}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item The total fragment keeps the complexity theory: by Theorem~\ref{thm:rescan-total}(ii) each node performs at most linearly many matches under the same per-pass length bound, so the degree induction of Lemma~\ref{lem:length} and the polynomial-time soundness of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} carry over verbatim. In particular $X \mapsto X^{2^{|X|}}$ is not unsafe-reachable.
        \item \emph{Run-collapse} -- the function mapping every maximal $a$-run to a single $a$, other characters untouched -- is $L$-reachable by three constant passes,
        \begin{align}
            \mathtt{collapse}(S) \;\triangleq\; [\,\epsilon/ab\,][\,\epsilon/aba\,][\,aab/a\,]\,S,
        \end{align}
        and unsafe-reachable by the single node $[\,a/aa\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    For (ii): the first pass maps each $a$ to $aab$, so each maximal $a$-run becomes $(aab)^n$ and every $aba$ in the image sits at offset $1 \bmod 3$ within a run image -- the only junctions with the $b$-blocks spell $abb$ or $baa$. The greedy deletions of the second pass therefore consume one block at a time inside a run image and cannot cross run boundaries, collapsing $(aab)^n$ to $aab$; the third deletes the residual $ab$, leaving one $a$ per run. The mechanism -- a \emph{deletion} pass resuming at the deletion point and re-exposing junctions -- is available in the safe semantics of Definition~\ref{def:subst} and goes unused in Section~2. For the unsafe node: matches of $aa$ occur only inside $a$-runs, each match shortens the current run by one, and the frozen prefix carries the collapsed part. (Verified: all strings of length $\leq 12$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$ and $\leq 9$ over $|\Sigma| = 3$, 3000 random strings of length up to 60, and the three unary shapes $a^m$, $ba^m$, $a^mb$ up to $m = 300$; all strings of length $\leq 10$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$ for the unsafe node.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    The two calculi are conjecturally \emph{incomparable}. In one direction, $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ is $L$-reachable, but its construction's instantiation pass $[\,X_1/\sigma\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$ diverges exactly when $\sigma \subset X_1$, and no unsafe construction is known (bounded searches fail). In the other direction, the \emph{a-flood} $[\,a/ab\,]^{\mathrm{u}}$ -- a single node, mapping $b^i a^j W \mapsto b^i a^{\#a(W)}$ -- is absent from every constant-pattern $L$-pipeline of depth $\leq 4$ on the same finite domains. Both directions are open.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Restarting from Zero}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Markov Substitution})
    \label{def:markov}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$, $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{m}} C$ is the trajectory $s_0 = C$, $s_{k+1} = s_k[{:}i]\cdot A\cdot s_k[i{+}|B|{:}]$ with $i$ the leftmost occurrence of $B$ in $s_k$: the value is the first $B$-free $s_k$; if there is none, the expression diverges. This is the leftmost-strategy normalization of the single rewrite rule $B \to A$ -- a one-rule Markov algorithm \cite{markov54}, the semantics the closing remark of Section~4 contrasted with bounded pipelines.
\end{definition}

Fixpoints are $B$-free -- the process stops exactly when $B$ no longer occurs -- whereas baseline outputs may contain their pattern ($[\,ab/b\,]$ applied to $b$ gives $ab$): the root of every difference below.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Termination Classification})
    \label{thm:termination}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $|A| < |B|$: total, in at most $|C|$ steps.
        \item $|A| = |B|$ and $A \neq B$: total.
        \item $A = B$ or $B \subset A$: diverges on every $B$-containing input.
        \item So for $|A| \leq |B|$: total iff $A \neq B$.
        \item For $|A| > |B|$ with $B \not\subset A$ there is no known criterion. This is the termination problem for the single rule $B \to A$ under the leftmost strategy; its unrestricted-strategy form -- the one-rule semi-Thue termination problem -- is a long-standing open problem \cite{geser01}, undecidable already for systems with three rules \cite{matiyasevich05}.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    (i) Each step shortens the string. (ii) Let $i_1$ be the first position where $A$ and $B$ differ; assign digit $0$ to $A[i_1]$, digit $1$ to $B[i_1]$, digit $0$ to every other character, and let $V(s) = \sum_j \mathrm{digit}(s[j])\,2^{-j}$. A step at position $i$ changes the digit at $i + i_1$ from $1$ to $0$ -- a drop of exactly $2^{-(i+i_1)}$ -- while the positions after $i+i_1$, inside or beyond the replaced block, can rise by a total of at most $\sum_{j > i+i_1} 2^{-j} < 2^{-(i+i_1)}$. So $V$ strictly decreases along the trajectory and takes only finitely many values on strings of the fixed length. (iii) The replacement re-contains the pattern at the same site. The arguments for (i) and (ii) nowhere use that the leftmost occurrence is chosen -- they bound the effect of a step at any position -- and in (iii) a redex persists under every strategy, so items (i)--(iv) hold verbatim for unrestricted derivations of the one-rule system as well; only (v) is a genuinely strategic question. (v) The divergent rules of this class with $|A|, |B| \leq 4$ are, up to renaming, $[\,aabb/ba\,]$ and $[\,abba/bab\,]$ -- the four rules $[\,aabb/ba\,]$, $[\,bbaa/ab\,]$, $[\,abba/bab\,]$, $[\,baab/aba\,]$ -- all with $|A| = 4$; e.g.\ $[\,aabb/ba\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ on $baa$ goes $baa \to aabba \to aabaabba \to \cdots$, each splice -- a prefix ending $aab$ against the replacement $aabb$ -- re-creating a junction $ba$. Among the 930 binary rules with $|A|, |B| \leq 4$, run on all inputs of length $\leq 9$, exactly 166 diverge under leftmost: the 162 with $B \subset A$, plus these four; the census methodology follows Kurth \cite{kurth90,kurth96}. The count is step-cap-sensitive, a small methodological finding of its own: the near-miss families $[\,aaab/ba\,]$ and $[\,abbb/ba\,]$ with their reversals terminate on every input of length $\leq 9$ -- certified with a step cap of $10^6$, the longest run being $3{,}280$ leftmost steps on $\mathtt{bbbbbbbba}$ -- so a cap of a few thousand misreads them as divergent. The strategy restriction costs nothing on this domain: an infinite leftmost trajectory is already an infinite derivation, so leftmost can only be \emph{better}-defined than unrestricted rewriting, and no rule was found in the other direction -- the four cap-sensitive families have finite unrestricted closures on every input of length $\leq 7$, so all their derivations terminate under every strategy.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Agreement})
    \label{prop:restart-agree}
    If $A$ and $B$ share no character and both are nonempty, then $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{m}} = [\,A/B\,]$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    No occurrence of $B$ can touch an inserted copy of $A$ -- it would have to share a character -- so each step destroys the leftmost occurrence and creates none; after the step, the next leftmost occurrence starts exactly where the greedy scan resumes. The number of occurrences strictly decreases, and induction gives identical outputs.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Amplifier})
    \label{thm:amplifier}
    For every binary string $S$,
    \begin{align}
        [\,baa/ab\,]^{\mathrm{m}}(S) \;=\; b^{\#b(S)}\cdot a^{v(S)},
    \end{align}
    where $v$ is the Horner evaluation of $S$ read left to right, $a$ adding $1$ and $b$ doubling: $v(\epsilon) = 0$, $v(Ta) = v(T) + 1$, $v(Tb) = 2\,v(T)$. The trajectory has exactly $v(S) - \#a(S)$ steps; on input $ab^{\,n-1}$ the output has length $2^{n-1} + n - 1$ and the process runs for exactly $2^{n-1} - 1$ steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Track $\#b$, $v$ and $\#a$ along the trajectory. Replacing the leftmost $ab$ by $baa$ preserves $\#b$ (one $b$ either way) and $v$: the single $a$ of $ab$ has $1+k$ $b$'s to its right, where $k$ is the number of $b$'s after the site, and contributes $2^{1+k}$; the two $a$'s of $baa$ each see $k$ and contribute $2\cdot 2^{k}$. It increases $\#a$ by exactly one. Since $v \geq \#a$ always, the trajectory has exactly $v(S) - \#a(S)$ steps and terminates at an $ab$-free string with $\#a = v(S)$ and $\#b = \#b(S)$ -- necessarily $b^{\#b}a^{v}$. For $ab^{\,n-1}$, $v = 2^{n-1}$. (Verified on all binary strings of length $\leq 10$; step counts on all of length $\leq 9$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{Complexity Collapse})
    \label{cor:towers}
    The length bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:length} and the polynomial-time soundness of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} fail for the Markov calculus: a single node has exponential output growth, in exponentially many steps. Worse, the two-node block $[\,baa/ab\,]^{\mathrm{m}}\circ[\,ab/aa\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ maps $b^m a^K$ to
    \begin{align}
        b^{\,m+\lfloor K/2\rfloor}\;a^{\,2^{\lfloor K/2\rfloor+1}-2+(K \bmod 2)},
    \end{align}
    so $2t-1$ constant-pattern nodes produce output of tower height $t$ in the input length.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    The first node maps $b^m a^K$ to $b^m\,(ab)^{\lfloor K/2\rfloor}\,a^{K \bmod 2}$: each step turns the leftmost $aa$ into $ab$, an $ab$ contains no $aa$, and the $b$-prefix is untouched, so after $\lfloor K/2 \rfloor$ steps the $a$-run is exhausted. The second node is the amplifier of Theorem~\ref{thm:amplifier}: on that string the $a$'s are those of $(ab)^j$ with $j = \lfloor K/2 \rfloor$, the $i$-th seeing $j-i$ $b$'s, plus the trailing $a$ if $K$ is odd, so $v = 2^{\,j+1} - 2 + (K \bmod 2)$ and $\#b = m + j$. Iterating the block composes $K \mapsto 2^{\lfloor K/2 \rfloor}$, so $2t-1$ nodes turn length $n$ into a tower of height $t$. (Verified: the block formula for $m \leq 3$, $K \leq 8$, and the height-$2$ composition on $ab^{\,n-1}$ for $n \leq 5$.)
\end{proof}

This makes the closing remark of Section~4 quantitative: unbounded iteration of substitution is what lifts the system beyond polynomial time -- a single node suffices -- while every bounded pipeline of passes stays inside it. Each node of the tower terminates individually -- both are rules of the family $0^p1^q \to 1^r0^s$, whose termination Zantema and Geser completely characterized \cite{zantema00} -- so the towers are a statement about \emph{composing} one-rule normalizers, one level beyond the derivational complexity of any single one-rule system \cite{kobayashi01}.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{No Injective Nodes})
    \label{thm:no-injective}
    A total restart node $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ satisfies $B \not\subset A$ and maps both $A$ and $B$ to $A$, with $A \neq B$: no total restart node is injective. Consequently every total constant-pattern core Markov expression with at least one node is non-injective, while the single $L$-node $[\,aa/a\,]$ -- doubling the $a$'s -- is total and injective: the constant-pattern core fragments of $L$ and of the Markov calculus are incomparable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Totality forces $B \not\subset A$, by Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(iii). Then $A$ contains no $B$, so the trajectory from $A$ stops immediately; the trajectory from $B$ stops after one step, at $A$; and $A = B$ would give $B \subset A$. For the second claim, write the expression as a pipeline over an atomic scrutinee (Proposition~\ref{prop:pipeline}): its innermost pass is total, hence identifies two values of the scrutinee, and the suffix pipeline cannot separate them.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}
    (\emph{IC})
    \label{conj:ic}
    No one-variable core Markov expression -- variable patterns allowed, no concatenation -- computes a total, injective, somewhere-growing function. If true, then $\mathtt{enc}$ is not Markov-core-reachable, and with it the whole architecture of Section~2, which relocates variable text and so needs exactly such a function. Concatenation alone does restore injective growth ($X \mapsto XX$), so the conjecture is genuinely about the core.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Toolkit with Black-Box Encoders})
    \label{prop:restart-toolkit}
    Take $\mathtt{enc}^2$ and $\mathtt{dec}^2$ as black boxes and read every other pass of the Section-2 constructions as a restart node. Then:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $\mathtt{head}$, $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{eq}$, $\mathtt{cat}$ and $\mathtt{bdec}$ transfer verbatim: every pass performs exactly one effective operation and is then inert, the patterns containing $bb$ and the images not.
        \item $\mathtt{if}$ needs a redesign -- the node $[\,\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ diverges whenever $\top \subset \mathtt{enc}(X)$ -- and has a deletion-based one: put the control character \emph{inside} the patterns, so that each pass deletes exactly its branch.
        \item the construction of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution} computes the freezing semantics if and only if no $X_i = b$. The renaming pass $[\,m_i/E_{X_i}\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ diverges exactly when $E_{X_i} \subset m_i$, i.e.\ when $X_i = b$ (then $E_{X_i} = xb$, the one image that fits inside $m_i = xb^{\,i+1}$); the repair and instantiation passes never diverge (their patterns have $b$-runs of length at least $3$ and $2$, their replacements at most $2$ and $1$); and, with no $X_i$ equal to $b$, each restart node performs exactly its left-to-right pass, because no replacement creates or destroys an occurrence of its own pattern: an $E_i$ can neither lie inside the inserted marker (its even positions are $x$'s, the marker's interior is $b$'s) nor straddle it, and likewise for the repair and instantiation patterns. Verified on 8370 instances with no $X_i = b$, both choices of $(b,x)$ over $|\Sigma| = 2$: $n = 1$ with all patterns and replacements of length $\leq 2$, and $n = 2$ with replacements of length $\leq 1$. Unconditionally -- the $n = 1$ domain without the restriction -- 364 of 2604 instances fail, exactly those with $X_1 = b$ and $b$ in $S$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
    Single restart nodes already compute nontrivial total functions: $[\,ba/ab\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ sorts its input to $b^*a^*$, and $[\,a/aa\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ collapses $a$-runs -- one node, where the baseline needs the three-pass pipeline of Proposition~\ref{prop:rescan-survives}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Converging to the Fixed Point}
\label{ssec:lim}

Restart iterates a \emph{rule}; we now iterate an \emph{expression}. The
variant of this subsection adds one node to the grammar: apply the
unary expression to its own output until nothing changes. Where
Definition~\ref{def:markov} normalizes one rewrite rule
$B \to A$ under the leftmost strategy, the new node normalizes a whole
pipeline under the strategy the calculus already has -- the sweep -- and
the two normalizers of the same rule will turn out to be genuinely
different functions (Remark~\ref{rem:limstrategies} below). The reward for the
difference is a characterization the restart calculus does not have:
with this one node the flat calculus computes \emph{exactly} the partial
computable functions, and the entire distance between the bounded
calculus of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} and universality turns out to be one
iteration node (Corollary~\ref{cor:limclass}).

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Fixed-Point Iteration})
    \label{def:lim}
    Extend the grammar of Definition~\ref{def:exp} by the rule
    \begin{mathpar}
        \inferrule{E \in \mathrm{Exp}_1, \; E_0 \in \mathrm{Exp}_n}{\mathrm{lim}(E)[E_0] \in \mathrm{Exp}_n}
    \end{mathpar}
    where $X_1$ is \emph{bound} in $E$ by the node; the free variables of
    $\mathrm{lim}(E)[E_0]$ are those of $E_0$. The \emph{orbit} of
    $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_1$ on $w$ is $s_0 = w$ and
    $s_{k+1} = \llbracket E \rrbracket(s_k)$, and
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket \mathrm{lim}(E)[E_0] \rrbracket(\vec S) \;=\; s_K
        \quad \text{for the least $K$ with } s_{K+1} = s_K\,,
    \end{align}
    with $s_0 = \llbracket E_0 \rrbracket(\vec S)$: the first state the
    iterand does not change. If some $s_{k+1}$ is undefined, or no such
    $K$ exists, the node is undefined (in the second case it
    \emph{diverges}). Substitution acts on the start expression,
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{lim}(E)[E_0]\big[F_1/X_1, \dots, F_n/X_n\big]
        \;=\; \mathrm{lim}(E)\big[\,E_0[F_1/X_1, \dots, F_n/X_n]\,\big],
    \end{align}
    capture-free, since only $X_1$ is bound and only in $E$ -- so
    Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} survives verbatim for the extended grammar.
\end{definition}

An $L{+}\mathrm{lim}$ expression is \emph{flat} if its iterands contain no
$\mathrm{lim}$ nodes; a single node $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ iterates one
constant pass, the \emph{sweep} $w \mapsto [\,A/B\,]w$. The preceding
remark's sort has its sweep twin: $\mathrm{lim}([\,ba/ab\,])$ also maps
every input to $b^{\#b}a^{\#a}$ (verified on all binary inputs of length
$\leq 12$ and $100$ random ones of length $13$--$16$).

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{The Fixed Points of a Pass})
    \label{lem:fpsweep}
    For $B \neq \epsilon$ and $A \neq B$:
    \begin{align}
        [\,A/B\,]w = w \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B \not\subset w .
    \end{align}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    If $B$ does not occur, the scan never fires. If it does, the sweep
    performs $m \geq 1$ non-overlapping replacements, changing the length
    by $m(|A| - |B|)$ -- so $w$ is changed whenever $|A| \neq |B|$ -- and
    when $|A| = |B|$ (with $A \neq B$) the first replacement spells $A$
    where $w$ spells $B$ at the same position, with the prefix before it
    untouched.
\end{proof}

So for $A \neq B$ the sweep's fixed points are exactly the $B$-free
strings -- the same stop set as the restart process of
Definition~\ref{def:markov}, which stops at the first $B$-free
trajectory term. Lemma~\ref{lem:fpsweep} is also a complete decision
procedure for the \emph{occurrence} of a constant, and at one stroke a
subroutine of the compilation below: a pass with a one-character
replacement tests containment,
\begin{align}
    \mathtt{contains}_B(E) \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{eq}\big([\,c/B\,]E,\; E\big),
    \qquad c \text{ any character with } \{c\} \neq B,
    \label{eq:contains}
\end{align}
because $[\,c/B\,]E = E$ iff $B \not\subset E$.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{One-Rule Totality})
    \label{prop:limtotal}
    Let $B \neq \epsilon$.
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $|A| \leq |B|$ and $A \neq B$: $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ is total.
        \item $B \subset A$: $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ diverges on every $B$-containing input.
        \item $A = B$: $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ is the identity.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    The proofs of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(i) and (ii) bound a
    replacement at \emph{any} position -- length in (i), the potential
    $V$ in (ii) -- so each sweep with at least one replacement strictly
    decreases a measure that takes finitely many values, and the orbit
    of sweeps reaches a $B$-free string, a fixed point by
    Lemma~\ref{lem:fpsweep}: (i). For (ii), every replaced site
    re-contains $B$, so after every sweep the string still contains $B$
    and is not a fixed point; for (iii) the pass is the identity, so the
    first sweep already is a fixed point. Note the one divergence from
    the restart column: at $A = B$ the restart process still hunts a
    redex and diverges (Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(iii)), while the
    sweep stops -- the two stop rules agree exactly when $A \neq B$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{Two Normalizers, Neither Canonical})
    \label{rem:limstrategies}
    $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ and $[\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{m}}$ are two
    deterministic normalizers of the one-rule system $B \to A$, and
    one-rule systems are not confluent: they disagree. On the census
    domain of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v) -- the $930$ binary rules
    with $|A|, |B| \leq 4$ on all inputs of length $\leq 9$, restart at
    a $3 \cdot 10^5$ step cap -- the two disagree on \emph{value} on
    $3{,}306$ pairs and on \emph{termination} on $290$ pairs, the latter
    exactly the two rules $[\,abba/bab\,]$ and $[\,baab/aba\,]$, all in
    the direction restart-diverges, sweep-converges, and \emph{none} in
    the reverse direction; re-verified on all inputs of length $\leq 6$
    at a $2{,}000$ step cap ($126$ value, $20$ termination disagreements,
    $0$ reverse). Witnesses, at a $10^5$ step restart cap:
    $[\,ab/bb\,]$ on $bbbb$ gives $\mathtt{abab}$ (one sweep replaces
    the two disjoint occurrences) against restart's $\mathtt{aaab}$
    (three sequential leftmost splices, each shifting the next site);
    $[\,ab/abba\,]$ on $abbababba$ gives $abb$ in two sweeps against
    restart's $abbba$; and $[\,baab/aba\,]$ on $aaaba$ goes
    $aaaba \to aabaab \to abaabab \to baabbaabb$ -- three sweeps, done --
    where restart diverges, each leftmost splice re-creating an $aba$ at
    the junction, the census mechanism of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v). The sweep is thus
    \emph{sometimes} the better-defined of the two strategies, and
    sometimes not definable from it at all: neither normalizer computes
    the other's values on a non-confluent rule.
\end{remark}

The rest of this subsection shows that this modest operator -- iterate a
flat expression to its fixed point -- is exactly the missing piece
between the polynomial calculus of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} and universality.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Compiling Two-Counter Machines Flat})
    \label{thm:lim2cm}
    Fix $\Sigma$ with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$ and two characters
    $\sigma \neq \tau \in \Sigma$. For every two-counter machine $M$ with
    instructions $\mathrm{inc}(r, j)$, $\mathrm{decjz}(r, j_0, j_1)$ and
    halt (counters $r \in \{1, 2\}$), there are flat expressions
    $\mathrm{INIT}, \mathrm{step}, \mathrm{OUT} \in \mathrm{Exp}_1$ such
    that
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{MAIN} \;\triangleq\; \mathrm{OUT}\big[\,F/X_1\,\big],
        \qquad F \;\triangleq\; \mathrm{lim}(\mathrm{step})\big[\,\mathrm{INIT}\,\big],
    \end{align}
    has exactly one $\mathrm{lim}$ node and computes the partial
    function of $M$ on tallies: $\mathrm{MAIN}(\tau^n)$ is the
    counter-2 tally $\tau^{f(n)}$ when $M$ halts on input $n$ with
    counter 2 equal to $f(n)$ and counter 1 empty, and is undefined
    exactly when $M$ does not halt. The iterand $\mathrm{step}$ is an
    if-chain of the Section~\ref{sec:toolkit} constructions over
    constant occurrence tests~(\ref{eq:contains}), all of whose branches
    are constant passes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof (construction)]
    Three compile-time transformations of $M$ come first, each
    preserving its partial function: (N1) a $\mathrm{decjz}$ whose
    zero-branch jumps to itself would leave the configuration
    untouched -- a non-halting fixed point of the compiled step -- so it
    is replaced by a fresh self-looping increment (both diverge);
    (N2) the output is read from counter 2 (swap the counters if $M$
    leaves its result in counter 1); (N3) jumps to the halt
    instruction are retargeted through a fresh draining instruction
    $\mathrm{decjz}(1, \mathrm{halt}, \mathrm{drain})$, so counter 1 is
    empty at halt.

    \emph{Frames.} Write
    $V(k) = \sigma^2(\tau\sigma)^{k-1}\tau\sigma^3$ for $k \geq 1$:
    $k$ isolated $\tau$'s, head run $\sigma^2$, interior $\sigma$-runs
    of length $1$, tail run $\sigma^3$, length $2k+4$. The compiler
    assigns each \emph{frame constant} a distinct $V(k)$ with $k \geq 2$:
    delimiters $D_0, D_1, D_2, D_3$, instruction codes
    $P_1, \dots, P_s, P_H$ for the instructions of the normalized
    machine, and the wrap marker $M$.

    \emph{Configurations.} For $i \in \{1, \dots, s\}$ let $\Pi(i)$ be
    the program list $P_1 \cdots P_s P_H$ with the $i$-th code wrapped,
    and $\Pi(0) = P_1 \cdots P_s\, M P_H M$ the halted list. The
    configuration with instruction counter $i$ and counter values $x, y$
    is
    \begin{align}
        C(i, x, y) \;=\; D_0\;\Pi(i)\;D_1\;\tau^{x}\;D_2\;\tau^{y}\;D_3 ,
    \end{align}
    an empty tally meaning adjacent delimiters.

    \emph{The step.} On a configuration the current instruction is the
    wrapped one, so dispatch is the occurrence test of
    $M P_i M$~(\ref{eq:contains}): $\mathrm{step}$ is the if-chain
    $\mathtt{if}(\mathtt{contains}_{M P_i M}, \mathrm{body}_i,
    \cdots)$ over $i = 1, \dots, s$, with the identity as the final
    else -- the halted configuration is a fixed point. Each
    $\mathrm{body}_i$ is three constant passes:
    \begin{enumerate}[(a)]
        \item \emph{operate}: for $\mathrm{inc}(r, j)$, the pass
        $[\,D_r\tau/D_r\,]$ (a new tally character at the left edge of
        counter $r$'s run); for $\mathrm{decjz}(r, j_0, j_1)$, the test
        $\mathtt{contains}_{D_r D_{r'}}$ -- adjacent delimiters, i.e.\
        counter $r$ empty (Lemma~\ref{lem:occurrence}(iii)) -- selects
        between the jump $j_0$ and $[\,D_r/D_r\tau\,]$ followed by the
        jump $j_1$ (the decrement deletes the first tally character,
        pinned by Lemma~\ref{lem:occurrence}(iv));
        \item \emph{unwrap}: $[\,P_i/M P_i M\,]$;
        \item \emph{wrap}: $[\,M P_j M/P_j\,]$ for the jump target $j$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Unwrap before wrap: on a self-jump ($j = i$) the wrap pattern
    $P_j$ would otherwise occur inside the block being unwrapped, and
    double-wrap. Every pattern is a nonempty constant, so every branch
    is total on every input, and an unselected branch's passes are
    merely \emph{discarded}: nothing needs the lazy gate of
    Section~\ref{sec:recursion}, because a flat branch has no
    undefined part to guard. (For the doubling machine:
    $\mathrm{step}$ is $298$ nodes, $83$ passes, of which $75$
    constant-pattern; $\mathrm{MAIN}$ is $310$ nodes.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Occurrence})
    \label{lem:occurrence}
    Let the frame constants be assigned pairwise distinct family
    indices $k \geq 2$. Then for every instruction counter
    $i \in \{0, \dots, s\}$ and every $x, y \geq 0$, in $C(i, x, y)$:
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item every frame constant occurs exactly at its slots ($M$
        twice, at the two wrap positions; every other constant once);
        \item $M P_i M$ occurs iff $i$ is the current instruction
        ($i \geq 1$), and $M P_H M$ iff $i = 0$; in either case exactly
        once;
        \item $D_r D_{r'}$ occurs iff counter $r$ is zero, exactly once;
        \item $D_r\tau$ occurs iff counter $r$ is nonzero, exactly
        once, ending at the \emph{first} tally character;
        \item the output prefix $D_0\,\Pi(0)\,D_1 D_2$ occurs iff
        $i = 0$ and $x = 0$, exactly once, at position $0$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    The proof is a \emph{gap calculus}. For a string $W$ containing at
    least one $\tau$ and no $\tau\tau$, let its \emph{gap sequence} be
    $(h;\, g_1, \dots, g_{m-1};\, t)$: the number of $\sigma$'s before
    the first $\tau$, between consecutive $\tau$'s, and after the last.

    \emph{Step 1 (configurations).} Every frame constant has gap
    sequence $(2;\, 1^{k-1};\, 3)$. Concatenating two constants merges
    the trailing $\sigma^3$ of the first with the leading $\sigma^2$ of
    the second: an interior gap of $5$. A tally $\tau^{n}$, $n \geq 1$,
    between its delimiters contributes $3\,0^{\,n-1}\,2$; an empty tally
    makes them touch, a gap of $5$. So a configuration's gaps are: head
    $2$, then the constants' interior $1$-blocks interleaved with
    junction gaps of $5$, the two tally junctions $3\,0^{x-1}\,2$ or
    $5$, tail $3$.

    \emph{Step 2 (patterns cannot cross tallies).} Every pattern of
    the construction has at least two $\tau$'s, no $\tau\tau$, head
    exactly $2$, tail in $\{0, 3\}$, and interior gaps in $\{1, 3, 5\}$
    only -- never $0$ and never $2$. If a pattern $W$ occurs in
    $C(i,x,y)$, its $\tau$'s map to \emph{consecutive} $\tau$'s of the
    configuration (a skipped $\tau$ would have to appear among $W$'s
    $\sigma$'s), so every interior gap of $W$ equals the corresponding
    gap of the configuration. Gaps of $0$ arise only inside tallies of
    length $\geq 2$, so $W$ covers no two consecutive tally characters;
    a gap of $2$ arises only after the last character of a tally, so
    $W$'s $\tau$-sequence cannot cross a tally's right edge; and if
    $W$'s first $\tau$ were a tally character, its next gap would have
    to be $0$ or $2$ -- both impossible. The only pattern that can meet
    a tally at all is the decrement pattern $D_r\tau$, whose last gap
    $3$ pins its last $\tau$ to the tally's \emph{first} character.
    Every other pattern avoids tallies entirely.

    \emph{Step 3 (pinning).} Let $W$'s leading segment (up to its
    first gap of $3$ or $5$) contain $k$ $\tau$'s, all separated by
    gaps of $1$. Its first $\tau$ must be a constant's \emph{first}
    $\tau$: a middle $\tau$ of a constant has one $\sigma$ before it
    inside the constant, and $W$'s head $\sigma^2$ must fit in the
    preceding $\sigma$-run. If that constant had more than $k$ $\tau$'s,
    the $(k{+}1)$-st would follow a gap of $1$ where $W$ has its seam
    $3$ or $5$: impossible; so the constant has exactly $k$ $\tau$'s,
    and by the distinctness of family indices it is the unique slot
    holding that string. The same argument applies constant-by-constant
    along $W$'s seams (a seam of $5$ matches a junction, so the
    constants on both sides occupy adjacent slots). Assembling:
    $M P_i M$ can only chain the $M$-slot, a junction, the $P_i$-slot,
    a junction, the $M$-slot, and the two $M$-slots of a configuration
    bracket exactly one code -- the wrapped one: (ii). $D_r D_{r'}$
    chains $D_r$'s slot to the next only through a gap of $5$, which
    after $D_r$'s slot arises exactly when the tally is empty: (iii).
    The output prefix is pinned at $D_0$'s slot and then slot-by-slot
    through the program zone (matching $\Pi(0)$ iff $i = 0$), then
    across the first tally junction ($5$ iff $x = 0$): (v). Finally a
    single frame constant $V(k)$ is itself such a pattern, pinned by
    Step 3 to a slot holding that string: the two $M$-slots are the two
    wrap positions, every other constant has one slot: (i).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lim2cm} (correctness)]
    $\mathrm{INIT} = D_0\,\Pi(1)\,D_1\cdot[\,\tau/\sigma\,]X_1\cdot D_2 D_3$
    maps any $w$ over $\{\sigma, \tau\}$ to $C(1, |w|, 0)$: one constant
    pass turns the input into a $\tau$-tally of its length, and the
    frame constants sandwich it. By Lemma~\ref{lem:occurrence} the
    dispatch test of the current instruction is the only one that
    fires, the operation pass acts on exactly the intended tally, and
    unwrap-then-wrap moves the wrap to exactly the target code -- so an
    induction along the run of $M$ gives
    $\mathrm{step}(C(i, x, y)) = C(i', x', y')$ for the machine's step,
    and the halted configuration -- where every dispatch test fails and
    the identity branch runs -- is a fixed point. It is the unique
    fixed point among configurations: every non-halted configuration
    moves, since its instruction either edits a tally or jumps (the
    zero-branch self-jump that would not move is excluded by (N1)).
    The orbit of $\mathrm{lim}(\mathrm{step})$ on a configuration is
    therefore exactly the run of $M$, sweep for step: it converges iff
    $M$ halts, in as many sweeps as the machine takes steps. At halt,
    (N2) puts the result in counter 2 and (N3) empties counter 1, so
    Lemma~\ref{lem:occurrence}(v) applies to the configuration
    $C(0, 0, y)$: $\mathrm{OUT} =
    [\,\epsilon/D_0\Pi(0)D_1D_2\,][\,\epsilon/D_3\,]$ deletes the
    constant prefix (which occurs exactly once, at position $0$) and
    the closing delimiter, leaving $\tau^{y}$. Both deletion passes sit
    outside the iterand, so $\mathrm{step}$ itself is total.

    Verified end to end (the scripts and full domains are in the
    research log, research/scratch/lim/): the compiled doubling machine
    on $n \leq 12$ ($\tau^{2n}$ exact), the adder on all $25$ input
    pairs $n, m \leq 4$ plus a $7 \times 7$ border grid, a
    counter-draining machine on a $5 \times 5$ grid, and the
    normalization pipeline (N1)--(N3) demonstrated on a machine
    needing all three, with the raw defects shown (a non-halting fixed
    point, a counter-1 output, a dirty halt) and repaired; divergence
    verified on a self-looping and a two-cycling machine, the orbit
    matching the simulated run on every point; $\mathrm{step}$'s
    totality on $400$ random large strings and on every branch; the
    occurrence census of Lemma~\ref{lem:occurrence} at the edge
    lengths $x, y \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ for every instruction counter,
    systematically for $x, y \leq 40$, and on $400$ random
    configurations up to $1000$, at three constant assignments (a
    fourth, violating the distinctness hypothesis, run as a stress
    test); and the whole of it re-run under both swaps of the
    Section-2 toolkit roles.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{The Compiled Program Computes the Machine})
    \label{thm:limpartial}
    Over any alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$, the flat calculus with one
    $\mathrm{lim}$ node computes exactly the partial computable
    functions $\Sigma^* \rightharpoonup \Sigma^*$: it computes all of
    them (Theorem~\ref{thm:lim2cm} and Minsky's two-counter machines
    \cite{minsky67}, arbitrary inputs brought to tallies by the
    two-letter prefix-code reduction of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}),
    and it computes nothing else: a converging evaluation is a finite
    sequence of sweeps, each a primitive-recursive pass, halted by a
    decidable fixed-point test, so every denotation is partial
    computable.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{One Node Is Enough: the Driver})
    \label{prop:limcontain}
    Every $L{+}\mathrm{lim}$ expression is computed by a lazy-pass
    recursive $L$ expression of the same size up to a constant factor:
    the unary function $\mathrm{lim}(E)$ over a flat iterand $E$ is the
    guarded definition
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{RUN}(X) \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{eq}(E(X),\, X),\;\; X,\;\; \mathrm{RUN}(E(X))\big),
    \end{align}
    with the recursive call in the \emph{dead} branch's replacement --
    the pattern-gated position of Remark~\ref{rem:ifgate}, where the
    two-way gate of Theorem~\ref{thm:gate} makes $\mathtt{if}$ sound
    both ways.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on the expression, replacing each $\mathrm{lim}$ node by
    its driver bottom-up: the iterand $E$ is then $\mathrm{lim}$-free
    by the induction hypothesis, and the node
    $\mathrm{lim}(E)[E_0]$ is the driver applied to the start,
    $\mathrm{RUN}[E_0/X]$. The driver unrolls the orbit exactly: its
    guard tests $s_{k+1} = s_k$, and its else-branch forces $E(X)$ --
    one sweep -- before recurring, so the recursion halts on the least
    $K$ with $s_{K+1} = s_K$ and diverges with the orbit; and if
    $E(X)$ is undefined at any point, the guard is undefined and so is
    the driver, exactly as the orbit's undefinedness makes the node
    undefined (Definition~\ref{def:lim}) -- the two are undefined
    together, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}'s caveat. What the eager
    calculus could not do -- evaluate a branch that may diverge -- is
    not asked of it: the dead branch's round is a discarded
    replacement (Remark~\ref{rem:ifgate}'s minimal repair), and the
    two-way gate of Theorem~\ref{thm:gate} is what makes that sound.
    Verified: the driver against the direct orbit for eight rules
    (convergent and divergent, census witnesses included) on $19$
    inputs each under both choices of the toolkit roles -- $135$
    convergent orbits value-exact, $29$ divergent -- plus the compiled
    iterands of Theorem~\ref{thm:lim2cm} (the drivers of the doubling
    and adder machines match $\mathrm{MAIN}$ exactly on $n \leq 5$) and
    a self-looping machine diverging on both sides.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{One Node Is the Whole Gap})
    \label{cor:limclass}
    Over any alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$:
    \begin{align}
        \text{flat } L + \text{ one } \mathrm{lim} \text{ node}
        \;&=\; \text{lazy-pass recursive } L \notag \\
        \;&=\; \text{the partial computable functions.}
    \end{align}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Left to right by Proposition~\ref{prop:limcontain} (one driver per
    node; the calculus underneath is untouched), right to left by
    Theorem~\ref{thm:limpartial} together with the soundness direction
    of Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}.
\end{proof}

This is the inertness story of Section~\ref{sec:recursion}, sharpened
to a single quantifier. The bounded calculus is polynomial
(Theorem~\ref{thm:fp}); recursion under eager evaluation changes
nothing (Theorem~\ref{thm:eager}), and neither do lazy arguments
(Theorem~\ref{thm:lazyargs}); the two-way gate plus recursion is
universal (Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}). The gate's condition --
evaluate the replacement only when the pattern fires -- is exactly the
fixed-point test of Definition~\ref{def:lim}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:limcontain} says the translation is an
equality of classes, not an embedding of one into a richer one: the
power was never in the recursion depth, it is unbounded guarded
iteration, and one iteration node over a flat pipeline already has
all of it.

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{Undecidability})
    \label{cor:limundec}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item Given a flat $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_1$ and $w$, whether
        $\mathrm{lim}(E)(w)$ is defined is $\Sigma^0_1$-complete.
        \item Given a flat $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_1$, whether
        $\mathrm{lim}(E)$ is total is $\Pi^0_2$-complete.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Both problems are in their classes because ``the orbit converges
    within $k$ sweeps'' is decidable, each sweep a primitive-recursive
    pass. Hardness: $\mathrm{MAIN}(\tau^{n})$ is defined iff $M$ halts
    on $n$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:lim2cm}), a $\Sigma^0_1$-hard relation,
    and $\mathrm{MAIN}$ is total iff $M$ halts on \emph{every} input
    (every $n$ is $\mathrm{INIT}$'s image of some string -- every
    length is hit), a $\Pi^0_2$-hard one. The undecidability of
    totality is the sibling of Corollary~\ref{cor:barriers}(4) for the
    recursive calculus.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Growth})
    \label{prop:limgrowth}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item (\emph{exponential output, one node}) Writing $v$ for the
        Horner value of Theorem~\ref{thm:amplifier} ($a$ adds $1$, $b$
        doubles),
        \begin{align}
            \mathrm{lim}([\,baa/ab\,])(w) \;=\; b^{\,\#b(w)}\, a^{\,v(w)}
        \end{align}
        for every $w$, in at most $v(w) - \#a(w)$ sweeps; in particular
        \begin{align}
            \mathrm{lim}([\,baa/ab\,])(a\,b^{\,n-1}) \;=\;
            b^{\,n-1} a^{\,2^{\,n-1}} \text{ of length } 2^{\,n-1} + n - 1 .
        \end{align}
        One constant pass under one $\mathrm{lim}$ node has exponential
        output: the length bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:length} fails at
        depth one.
        \item (\emph{towers, nested nodes}) The block
        $\mathrm{lim}([\,baa/ab\,]) \circ \mathrm{lim}([\,ab/aa\,])$
        maps $b^{m} a^{K}$ to
        $b^{\,m + \lfloor K/2 \rfloor}\,
        a^{\,2^{\lfloor K/2 \rfloor + 1} - 2 + (K \bmod 2)}$ -- the
        closed form of Corollary~\ref{cor:towers}'s two restart nodes,
        now for sweep normalizers -- so $2t - 1$ constant-pattern
        $\mathrm{lim}$ nodes produce output of tower height $t$ in the
        input length, and $\mathrm{lim}(L) \sqsubseteq L$ fails: three
        nodes already outgrow Lemma~\ref{lem:length}'s polynomial
        bound.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    (i) Each replacement $ab \to baa$ preserves $\#b$ and $v$:
    reading $v$ left to right, $v(x\,ab) = (v(x)+1) \cdot 2 =
    v(x\,baa)$, and appending the same suffix multiplies both equally
    -- formally $v(uv) = v(u) \cdot 2^{\#b(v)} + v(v)$. So the sweep,
    which performs several such replacements at disjoint sites,
    preserves them too. The potential $\Phi(w) = v(w) - \#a(w)$ is
    nonnegative -- $v$ sums $2^{\#b(\text{after})}$ over the $a$'s of
    $w$ -- drops by exactly $1$ per replacement ($\#a$ grows, $v$
    does not), and is $0$ exactly when no $a$ has a $b$ after it, i.e.\
    on the $ab$-free strings, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:fpsweep} are the
    sweep's fixed points. The orbit therefore reaches an $ab$-free
    string after at most $\Phi(w)$ replacements -- hence at most that
    many sweeps -- and an $ab$-free string with the same $\#b$ and $v$
    is $b^{\#b} a^{v}$, both invariants forcing the exponents. The
    potential also re-proves the termination of the restart process of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:amplifier}, and the two normalizers of this rule
    agree on every input. (Verified: all binary inputs of length
    $\leq 12$, $200$ random of length $13$--$16$, the invariants per
    sweep on $50$ traces, and the tally witness for $n \leq 14$.)
    (ii) The half node maps $b^{m} a^{K}$ to
    $b^{m} (ab)^{\lfloor K/2 \rfloor} a^{K \bmod 2}$ in \emph{one}
    sweep -- the greedy pairing of the $a$-run -- and is then inert
    ($ab$ contains no $aa$); composing with (i) and counting the
    $b$'s after each $a$ of $(ab)^{j}$ gives the displayed form, the
    Horner computation of Corollary~\ref{cor:towers}. (Verified
    against the closed forms for $t \leq 3$ -- $5$ nodes, output
    length $65{,}556$ at $n = 4$ -- and the block on the grid
    $m \leq 3$, $K \leq 14$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{Divergent Orbits Grow Like Fibonacci})
    \label{rem:fiborbit}
    The census's divergent rule $[\,aabb/ba\,]$ diverges under the sweep
    too, but with a law: on $aabbaabb$ the orbit lengths are
    $2F_{k+1} + 2k + 6$ for the Fibonacci numbers $F$, i.e.\ $8 \to 10
    \to 14 \to 18 \to \cdots \to 204$ in ten sweeps $\to \cdots \to
    1{,}028{,}520$ in twenty-eight -- growth by the golden ratio per
    sweep, exponential in the iteration count; on $bbaa$ the law is
    $2F_{k+1} + 2k + 2$, and the mirror rule $[\,bbaa/ab\,]$ runs the
    reversed orbits (verified exactly through $k = 28$, the sweep
    strings cross-checked against the evaluator through $k = 16$). The
    mechanism is the census splice of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v) -- a prefix ending $aab$ against
    the replacement $aabb$, re-creating a junction $ba$ -- compounding:
    a junction in the context $(b, a)$ doubles per sweep, a junction in
    $(a, a)$ or $(b, b)$ persists, one in $(a, b)$ dies, and the two
    persistent species follow the Fibonacci recurrence. So the growth
    of the iterated calculus lives in the \emph{iteration}, not the
    nesting: Corollary~\ref{cor:towers} needs $2t-1$ nested nodes for
    tower height $t$, but one node already produces exponential output
    (Proposition~\ref{prop:limgrowth}(i)) and Fibonacci divergent
    orbits -- while divergent restart rules were the \emph{only}
    obstacle left in the one-rule census, here divergence is the
    engine.
\end{remark}

The landscape closes one row and opens a question. $L \sqsubseteq \mathrm{lim}(L)$ trivially, the grammar growing
by one node kind; $\mathrm{lim}(L) \sqsubseteq L$ refuted by
Proposition~\ref{prop:limgrowth} (one node, exponential output); the
toolkit carried verbatim -- the compilation of
Theorem~\ref{thm:lim2cm} needs no recursion and no nesting, an if-chain
of Section~\ref{sec:toolkit} passes; growth unbounded, the class being
the partial computable functions. Restart and $\mathrm{lim}$ are the
two iteration operators over the same grammar -- one rule iterated
against one expression iterated -- and both escape $L$ on growth
(Corollary~\ref{cor:towers}, Proposition~\ref{prop:limgrowth}), but
$\mathrm{lim}$'s class is exactly characterized
(Corollary~\ref{cor:limclass}) where the Markov row's entries carry
Conjecture~\ref{conj:ic}. What the characterization does \emph{not}
settle is the one-rule fragment: is convergence of
$\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ decidable? Proposition~\ref{prop:limtotal}
settles everything outside the strategic case $|A| > |B|$ with
$B \not\subset A$, and Remark~\ref{rem:limstrategies} shows the sweep
and leftmost versions of that case are genuinely different problems --
the sweep sibling of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v)'s strategic
termination problem, whose unrestricted form is the long-standing open
one-rule termination problem \cite{geser01}.  The affine potentials of
Theorem~\ref{thm:affine} are a decidable sufficient condition for the
strategic case -- one product equation and one linear equation -- and
they cover every growing census rule of the exponential tier. And the hierarchy inside the
operator: one $\mathrm{lim}$ node over a \emph{single constant pass}
reaches exponential output and Fibonacci orbits
(Proposition~\ref{prop:limgrowth}, Remark~\ref{rem:fiborbit}) -- and, we
conjecture, nothing beyond. The exponential is never a fire blowup:
every occurrence of $B$ in $s_{k+1}$ starts within $|A|{+}|B|{-}1$ of
the start of some emission of sweep $k$ -- clear of every emission it
would sit in surviving text and have fired then -- so the fires obey
$r_{k+1} \le (|A|{+}|B|{-}1)\,r_{k}$, and over the iteration census
($930$ rules, $203{,}126$ convergent orbits, $0$ failures of the
freshness, branching, and drift bounds) they never exceed $9$ per
sweep with no strictly growing run outlasting $4$ sweeps: the output
is few fires sustained exponentially long. Four rule shapes realize
\emph{every} exponential base $m$ exactly,
$\mathrm{lim}([\,ba^{m}/ab\,])(a^{i}b^{j}) = b^{j}a^{\,i\,m^{j}}$ and
its three siblings, all degenerating at $m = 1$ to insertion sort
($1{,}169$ grid cells exact), and they make the single-exponential law
$|\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])(w)| \le (|A|{-}|B|{+}1)^{|w|} + |w|$ on
convergent orbits tight in its base -- verified on all $148$ growing
rules over inputs $\leq 9$, and the family attains $m^{n-1}$ on
length-$n$ inputs, so no smaller base serves. If true it separates the
one-rule fragment from the universality of Corollary~\ref{cor:limclass}:
a $\mathrm{lim}$ node over a pipeline computes every partial computable
function, the tower map of Corollary~\ref{cor:towers} among them,
which no single-exponential rule can match. And the sweep half of the
conjecture is no longer missing for the exponential tier: a potential
argument proves it outright.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Affine potentials})
    \label{thm:affine}
    Fix a direction and let each letter act on a state as
    $t \mapsto \mathrm{mult}[x]\cdot t + \mathrm{off}[x]$ with
    $\mathrm{mult}[x] \ge 1$ and $\mathrm{off}[x] \ge 0$, folding a
    string to $v(s)$, and set $\Phi(s) = v(s) - \#c(s)$.  The data
    $(\mathrm{mult}, \mathrm{off}, c)$ is an \emph{affine potential} for
    $[\,A/B\,]$ when
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item the word maps of $A$ and $B$ coincide: $v(xAy) = v(xBy)$
        for all contexts $x, y$;
        \item $\delta \triangleq \#c(A) - \#c(B) \ge 1$;
        \item $\mathrm{off}[c] \ge 1$.
    \end{enumerate}
    Then every sweep-orbit of $\mathrm{lim}([\,A/B\,])$ converges, with
    sweeps bounded by the initial potential:
    \begin{align}
        K \;\le\; \Phi(s_0)/\delta \;\le\; n\cdot\mathrm{maxoff}\cdot
        \mathrm{maxmult}^{\,n}.
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    A fire replaces an occurrence $xBy$ by $xAy$, preserving $v$ by (i),
    and the fires of one sweep apply sequentially at disjoint positions,
    so $v$ survives the whole sweep; each fire adds $\delta$ to $\#c$, so
    $\Phi$ drops by $\delta \ge 1$ per fire.  Positivity:
    $v(s) = \sum_i \mathrm{off}[s_i]\cdot\prod_{j>i}\mathrm{mult}[s_j]
    \ge \#c(s)$ by (iii).  $\Phi(B) = \Phi(A) + \delta \ge 1$ by (i) and
    (ii); and writing $M(u) = \prod_{x \in u}\mathrm{mult}[x] \ge 1$,
    $\Phi(uv) = M(v)\,\Phi(u) + (M(v){-}1)\,\#c(u) + \Phi(v) \ge
    \Phi(u)$, so any string containing $B$ has $\Phi \ge 1$.  The orbit
    can therefore neither stop while $B$ occurs -- a sweep finding an
    occurrence fires at the leftmost one -- nor fire more than
    $\Phi(s_0)/\delta$ times, and when $B$ is finally absent the orbit is
    at a fixed point (Lemma~\ref{lem:fpsweep}).
\end{proof}

The census splits exactly at the tier boundary.  Of the $144$
convergent growing rules, $114$ admit an affine potential -- every rule
that reaches the exponential tier -- and the theorem covers them; of the
$30$ remaining, all slow ($K \le 14$ at $n = 9$, against the admitters'
$2{,}194$), $18$ fall to counting potentials $\Phi = \#g_1 +
w\,\#g_2$ over $k$-grams of $B$ ($[\,aba/aa\,]$ by the overlapping
count of $aa$, giving $K \le n^{k}$), and the last $12$ -- a precisely
delimited family with $[\,aaab/baa\,]$ representative -- admit
neither: for $[\,aaab/baa\,]$ the map equality (i) forces
$\mathrm{mult}[a] = 1$ and then $\mathrm{off}[a] = 0$, killing the only
letter with $\delta \ge 1$.  Their measured sweeps are linear
nonetheless ($K \le 2n$, the worst $14$ at $n = 9$) -- the data sits
nowhere near the conjecture's limit; what fails is the method.  The
conjecture therefore stands proved up to a polynomial factor on every
exponential-tier rule, and open exactly on the $12$-rule family --
where it is expected to be loose -- and on the rules beyond the census.
(Verified: the existence search is exact, the product equation forcing
$\mathrm{mult}$ and the offset equation being linear; the theorem
per-orbit on all $114$ admitting rules over inputs $\le 7$ ($29{,}070$
orbits: $v$ invariant every sweep, $\Phi \ge 0$, $K\delta \le
\Phi_0$, the limit $B$-free); no divergent rule admits a potential, a
consistency check; and on the four base-$m$ shapes the potential counts
the remaining fires exactly, $\Phi_0 = i\,m^{j} - i$ and $R =
\Phi_0/(m{-}1)$, all $16$ instances at $m = 2..5$.)

\subsection{Both Directions at Once}
\label{ssec:union}

The variants so far reinterpret the node; the \emph{union} $L{+}\mathrm{R}$ extends the grammar, admitting both node kinds $[\,R/P\,]$ and $[\,R/P\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ in one expression. It is the weakest calculus that edits from both ends in a single pipeline, and its placement reduces to one function. Write
\begin{align*}
    \mathrm{r}(A, B, C) \;=\; [\,A/B\,]^{\mathrm{R}} C
\end{align*}
for the right pass as a three-argument function -- the denotation of the one-node expression $[\,X_1/X_2\,]^{\mathrm{R}} X_3$, undefined exactly when $B = \epsilon$ -- and $\bar{\mathrm{r}}$ for its mirror $m(\mathrm{r})$, the left pass as a three-argument function.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Collapse Criterion})
    \label{thm:union-collapse}
    The following are equivalent: $L{+}\mathrm{R} = L$; $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$; $\mathrm{r} \in L$. Each is equivalent to $L{+}\mathrm{R} = \mathrm{R}$ and to $\bar{\mathrm{r}} \in \mathrm{R}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    If $\llbracket E_{\mathrm{r}} \rrbracket = \mathrm{r}$, translate a mixed expression bottom-up, replacing every $\mathrm{R}$-node $[\,R/P\,]^{\mathrm{R}} E$ with $E_{\mathrm{r}}[\,t(R)/X_1,\ t(P)/X_2,\ t(E)/X_3\,]$: by Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} and induction the translation denotes the same partial function. On defined values this is the node's semantics. On undefinedness: $\mathrm{r}$ depends on each of its three arguments (fix two, vary the third), an expression cannot depend on a variable it does not contain, so all three variables occur in $E_{\mathrm{r}}$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}'s clause applies; and $\mathrm{r}$ is undefined exactly at $B = \epsilon$, which is the node's condition too. Conversely $[\,X_1/X_2\,]^{\mathrm{R}} X_3$ is itself a mixed expression, giving the reverse implication and the restriction to pure-$\mathrm{R}$ expressions. The final clause is the mirror $m$ of Corollary~\ref{cor:incl-symmetry}: $m$ preserves the union's reachable class, carries the $L$-class onto the $\mathrm{R}$-class, and $m(\bar{\mathrm{r}}) = \mathrm{r}$.
\end{proof}

So the union collapses onto $L$ exactly when the right pass, as a function of all three of its arguments, is itself an $L$-expression -- and reversal's reachability (Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation}) is sufficient for this but, by the criterion, not necessary.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Disjoint Commutation})
    \label{lem:disjoint-comm}
    Passes $[\,A/B\,]^{d_1}$ and $[\,C/D\,]^{d_2}$ -- either direction pairing -- commute whenever $\mathrm{alph}(B) \cap \mathrm{alph}(D) = \emptyset$, $A \neq \epsilon$ with $\mathrm{alph}(A) \cap \mathrm{alph}(D) = \emptyset$, and $C \neq \epsilon$ with $\mathrm{alph}(C) \cap \mathrm{alph}(B) = \emptyset$. Every hypothesis is necessary.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    A $B$-match and a $D$-match sharing a position would place a letter of $\mathrm{alph}(B) \cap \mathrm{alph}(D)$ there; the hypotheses exclude this, so the two patterns' matches in any string are pairwise disjoint windows. Replacing a $B$-window by $A$ creates no $D$-match -- a straddling match would need a $D$-letter at an inserted position, and $A$ carries none -- and destroys none, since no $D$-window met a $B$-window; symmetrically for the second pass. Both compositions therefore compute the same simultaneous replacement. For necessity: $[\,\epsilon/c\,]$ before $[\,d/ab\,]$ on $\mathtt{acb}$ yields $\mathtt{ab}$, the reverse order $\mathtt{d}$ -- deletion splices fresh matches, which is why the replacements must be nonempty -- and the disjointness clauses admit two-letter counterexamples of the same size. (Verified: $1{,}677{,}312$ checks -- $1{,}152$ rule pairs $\times$ four direction pairings $\times$ $364$ inputs, $|A|, \dots, |D| \leq 2$ over three letters.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{No Two-Block Normal Form})
    \label{prop:no-2block}
    Over the four cross-letter halvings $\{[\,a/bb\,], [\,b/aa\,]\} \times \{L, \mathrm{R}\}$: the four sibling pipelines $[\,a/bb\,] \cdot [\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}} \cdot [\,a/bb\,]$, its mirror, and their two renamings are equivalent to no two-block composite $L^j \circ \mathrm{R}^k$ or $\mathrm{R}^k \circ L^j$ \emph{at any depth} -- and of the $64$ mixed words of three passes, exactly these four are not two-block composites. The siblings fix every alternating string, both patterns being absent from it.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    Closure computation by breadth-first search over function tables with exact dedup, on all $255$ binary strings of length $\leq 7$: the pure-$L$ and pure-$\mathrm{R}$ fragments saturate at $15$ tables each -- which makes the enumeration a proof over compositions of every depth, not a bounded budget -- and the two-block composites close at $69$ tables, among which the siblings' tables do not appear. The census is the same enumeration restricted to words of at most three passes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Orientation})
    \label{lem:orientation}
    On a two-letter alphabet, the halving passes lay their output down in opposite orders: on a maximal run of length $n$, bare or sentinel-delimited, $[\,a/bb\,]$ produces the block $a^{\lfloor n/2 \rfloor}$ \emph{before} the residue $b^{\,n \bmod 2}$, and $[\,a/bb\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ the residue \emph{before} the block.
\end{lemma}

The growth theory transfers unchanged: an $\mathrm{R}$-pass obeys the length bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:length} (its matches are disjoint either way), so the degree recursion and Theorem~\ref{thm:fp} carry over verbatim, and every $L{+}\mathrm{R}$-reachable function is polynomial-time with degree bounded by its expression (verified on $20{,}000$ random mixed pipelines). Concatenation is eliminable in the union as in $L$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:core}, whose induction is direction-agnostic), and by Lemma~\ref{lem:disjoint-comm} a mixed pipeline whose patterns are pairwise alphabet-disjoint and met only by nonempty disjoint replacements is equivalent to a pure-$L$ one -- interleaving, in other words, \emph{requires} overlapping, hence bordered, patterns (Proposition~\ref{prop:r2l-agree}).

\begin{conjecture}
    (\emph{Strictness of the Union})
    \label{conj:union}
    $L{+}\mathrm{R} \supsetneq L$ -- equivalently $\mathrm{r} \notin L$, $\bar{\mathrm{r}} \notin \mathrm{R}$, $L \neq \mathrm{R}$. In particular it would follow that reversal is not $L$-reachable.
\end{conjecture}

The evidence is fourfold. (i) The constant fragments are provably incomparable (Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}). (ii) No witness exists in any searched space: none for $\mathrm{r}$ among $1{,}841{,}923$ depth-$3$ pipeline tables nor $300{,}000$ randomized depth-$5$ pipelines; none for the minimal hard case $[\,A/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ -- a bordered pattern, by Proposition~\ref{prop:r2l-agree} the first that can disagree -- among $592{,}447$ tables, $1{,}574{,}748$ more with anchored variable patterns, and $74{,}525$ with computed pattern sources; none for $\mathrm{rev}$ in the union at exhaustive depth $2$, at $400{,}000$ randomized depth $6$, nor in a $400 \times 400$ genetic search to depth $11$ whose best programs score exactly the palindrome inputs and fail all others. (iii) The obstruction has a mechanism (Lemma~\ref{lem:orientation}): an $\mathrm{R}$-halving leaves its residue at the run \emph{front} while every $L$ mechanism computes per-run residues only at run \emph{ends}, and moving a marker from where it is computed to where it is needed is right-to-left information flow, which an $L$-pass's frontier cannot perform; three concrete constructions -- pad-then-pair, comma-code parity marking, offset-$1$ pairing -- each fail with their own counterexample. (iv) The boundary of the easy invariants is located: five output-shaped formalizations of right-to-left routing are each falsified by plain $L$-constructions, and the strongest -- a first-difference offset -- separates the identity and the toolkit (relative offset $\geq 0.5$) from $[\,a/bb\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ ($0.14$) and the sibling $W$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:no-2block} ($0.00$), falling only to deletion under an \emph{input-computed} pattern, the $\mathtt{rep}$ escape hatch of Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution}. A proof of Conjecture~\ref{conj:union}, if one exists, must be dependence-structural rather than output-shaped. The searches cover pipeline-shaped expressions with single-pass pattern sources; anchored junction patterns -- the shape of Proposition~\ref{prop:last} -- lie outside them, and are the natural first place to look for a collapse witness.

\subsection{The Landscape}

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l|llll}
    variant & $L \sqsubseteq V$ & $V \sqsubseteq L$ & toolkit & growth \\
    \hline
    once & refuted & open & rebuilt & linear \\
    positional & refuted & iff once $\in L$ & $\mathtt{tail}$, $\mathtt{head}$ & linear \\
    $k$-th occurrence $\mathrm{L}_k$ & refuted & open & untested & linear \\
    $\mathrm{lim}$ & trivially & refuted & verbatim & unbounded \\
    right-to-left & open & open & verbatim & polynomial \\
    unsafe & open & open & collapses & poly.\ fragment \\
    Markov & conj.\ no & refuted & black-box $\mathtt{enc}$ & towers \\
    union ($L{+}\mathrm{R}$) & trivially & conj.\ no & verbatim & polynomial
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The right-to-left row's two ``open'' cells and the union row's one are a single question: either inclusion implies the other (Corollary~\ref{cor:incl-symmetry}), and $L{+}\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$ iff $\mathrm{R} \sqsubseteq L$ iff $\mathrm{r} \in L$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:union-collapse}) -- Conjecture~\ref{conj:union} predicts the negative answer for all three. Both inclusions hold if $\mathrm{rev}$ is reachable in either calculus, and no other route between $L$ and $\mathrm{R}$ is known (Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential} separates only the constant fragments). The two ``refuted'' entries of the once and positional rows are Corollaries~\ref{cor:once-sep} and~\ref{cor:pos-sep}, and the $k$-th-occurrence row's is Proposition~\ref{prop:kth}(i); the Markov row's is Corollary~\ref{cor:towers}. The $k$-th-occurrence row's ``open'' reduces, wherever a marker letter is fresh, to the once-hinge by Proposition~\ref{prop:kth}(iii). The $\mathrm{lim}$ row is the only one whose class is exactly characterized (Corollary~\ref{cor:limclass}) -- its ``refuted'' is Proposition~\ref{prop:limgrowth}, one constant pass under one iteration node already outgrowing Lemma~\ref{lem:length} -- where the Markov row's entries carry Conjecture~\ref{conj:ic}; restart and $\mathrm{lim}$ are the two iteration operators over the same grammar, one rule against one expression, and both escape $L$ on growth, but only $\mathrm{lim}$'s escape is total. The unsafe row's two ``open''s carry the conjectures after Proposition~\ref{prop:rescan-survives}; the Markov row's ``conj.\ no'' is Conjecture~\ref{conj:ic}, with the doubling node of Theorem~\ref{thm:no-injective} the concrete obstacle.

The web hangs on two ropes, and both are reachability questions about the baseline itself:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Is the once-primitive $L$-reachable?} By Theorem~\ref{thm:pos-hinge} this decides $\mathrm{POS} \sqsubseteq L$, and with it the whole once-family's relation to $L$. The probe is affirmative over two letters (Proposition~\ref{prop:del-leftmost}); over $|\Sigma| \geq 3$ the constant needle resists with a precise obstruction, and the computed needle is open over every alphabet. The $k$-th-occurrence family rides on the same hinge wherever a marker letter is spare (Proposition~\ref{prop:kth}(iii)); over the binary alphabet its $k \geq 2$ nodes are absent from every once-space searched. The hinge itself is one question, not one per alphabet: the once node as a three-argument function is coding-equivariant (Corollary~\ref{cor:uniform}), so an answer over any one alphabet of at least two letters is the answer over all -- the alphabet-sensitivity above is about native conjugates, not the hinge.
    \item \emph{Is reversal $L$-reachable?} This decides whether $L = \mathrm{R}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugation}), and by the mirror symmetry of the once-calculi it is also the hinge between once-left and once-right. (Section~\ref{sec:recursion} settles the recursive version: reversal is computable there by a three-line guarded recursion; and the bounded calculus can already read and edit its right end, Proposition~\ref{prop:last} -- the question is now sharp: the bounded calculus, or nothing.) The union calculus (Section~\ref{ssec:union}) refines the stakes: $\mathrm{rev} \in L$ is sufficient but not necessary for $L = \mathrm{R}$ -- the exact criterion is $\mathrm{r} \in L$, and every search for it has failed (Conjecture~\ref{conj:union}). Section~\ref{sec:alphabet} makes the question alphabet-uniform: $\mathrm{rev} \in L$ over one alphabet of at least two letters iff over every (Theorem~\ref{thm:rev-uniform}) -- as with the once hinge, a single negative answer, proved over any one alphabet, would settle all.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{The Price of Reordering})
    \label{rem:price}
    The second open problem -- is reversal $L$-reachable? -- has a quantitative form. For a function $f$ and an input $w$, let $w^{p}$ be $w$ with its $p$-th character flipped, and let
    \[
        D_f(w,p) \;\triangleq\; \{\, i : f(w)_i \neq f(w^{p})_i \,\},
    \]
    discarding the flips that change the output length. Where the surviving rows are singletons meeting distinct columns, they induce a partial injective map $\pi_w$ from input positions to output positions, and
    \[
        \chi_f(w) \;\triangleq\; \#\{(p < p') : \pi_w(p) > \pi_w(p')\}
    \]
    counts its \emph{crossings}: the disorder of $f$'s output routing. Reading the right end is free -- $\mathtt{last}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:last}) routes its single far cell with zero crossings -- and so is dropping the final character ($\mathtt{init}$, the same proposition). What costs crossings is \emph{moving} characters, and the costs of the functions the calculus demonstrably \emph{can} build form a ladder (all rows at $|w| = 14$, worst input; the $k$-th rung family $\mathrm{revlast}_k(w) \triangleq w_{n-1} w_{n-2} \cdots w_{n-k} \cdot w[:n{-}k]$ is $L$-expressible for every fixed $k$, by $k$ anchored right-end reads $\mathtt{last} \circ \mathtt{init}^{j}$ ($j < k$) and the remaining head $\mathtt{init}^{k}$, glued with $\mathtt{cat}$):

    \begin{center}
        \small
        \begin{tabular}{llll}
            \hline
            $f$ & in $L$? & $\chi_f$ & closed form \\
            \hline
            $\mathtt{init}$ & yes & $0$ & order-preserving \\
            $\mathtt{last}$ & yes & $0$ & reads the far end \\
            $\mathrm{rotR} = \mathtt{cat}(\mathtt{last},\mathtt{init})$ & yes & $13$ & $n-1$ \\
            swap-first-last & yes & $25$ & $2(n{-}2)+1$ \\
            $\mathrm{revlast}_k$, $k=1,2,3,4$ & yes, fixed $k$ & $13,\,25,\,36,\,46$ & $\binom{n}{2}-\binom{n-k}{2}$ \\
            $\mathrm{rev}$ & \emph{open} & $91$ & $\binom{n}{2}$ \\
            \hline
        \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    The ladder is exact: $\mathrm{revlast}_k$ reverses a $k$-character tail block and moves it to the front, so its crossings are the block's internal reversals $\binom{k}{2}$ plus its crossings of the untouched head $k(n-k)$ -- which sum to $\binom{n}{2} - \binom{n-k}{2}$, the number of pairs involving at least one moved character. Each fixed $k$ is affordable; reversal is the $k = n$ rung, and it is the only row whose crossing count is quadratic in $n$. Every $L$-expression measured so far -- the built rungs of the table, the rotations $\mathrm{rotR}_k$ for $k \leq 4$, the doubling $\mathtt{cat}(X,X)$, a thousand random pipelines -- pays $O(n)$ crossings, suggesting the quantitative form of the hoped negative answer: there is a constant $c(E)$, depending only on the expression, with $\chi_{\llbracket E \rrbracket}(w) \leq c(E)\cdot|w|$ for all $w$; reversal needs $|w|(|w|{-}1)/2$. (Verified: the built rungs compute their functions exactly on all binary inputs of length $\leq 10$ for $k \leq 2$ and $\leq 8$ for $k \leq 4$, and their measured crossings at $|w| = 14$ -- worst of $56$ inputs -- agree with the closed forms; likewise the rotations, with closed form $k(n{-}k)$; swap-first-last exactly on all binary inputs of lengths $2$ to $11$; the random corpus is a thousand expressions of depth $\leq 4$ at $|w| = 12$, none paying more than $3$ crossings, the five heaviest re-measured at $|w| = 8, 10, 14$ and exceeding $3$ at no length.)

    Three delimitations keep the suggestion honest. \emph{(i) Pair counts are the wrong measure.} The doubling $\mathtt{cat}(X,X)$ repeats the input, and under the natural correspondence sending each input position to its two output copies, $\binom{n}{2}$ pairs come out inverted -- for input positions $i < j$, the first copy of $j$ lands before the second copy of $i$. The doubling is as cheap as an expression gets, so any obstruction that counts inverted pairs of a natural input--output correspondence is falsified on the spot; the crossing count survives it because it is computed flip by flip, and the doubling's flips each move the two copies of one character together -- non-singleton rows, discarded by construction. \emph{(ii) Content is relabelable.} Label each character of $\mathrm{rev}(w)$ by the input position it comes from, consistently (same character, distinct positions); there is such a labeling increasing within each character class -- each class of $\mathrm{rev}(w)$ is a class of $w$ -- and a strictly decreasing subsequence of it uses at most one position per class, at most $|\Sigma|$ in all. So no invariant computable from output content alone can distinguish $\mathrm{rev}$ from an order-preserving function; a proof must constrain which input characters the calculus delivers where. (The within-class-increasing labeling has width at most the number of distinct characters on all inputs of length $\leq 14$, and brute force over \emph{all} consistent labelings confirms it optimal on all inputs of length $\leq 6$.) \emph{(iii) Tracing is fakeable.} The anchored constructions of the ladder rebuild their outputs from constant characters -- Proposition~\ref{prop:last} \emph{emits} the right end rather than moving it -- so an invariant that demands output characters be moved input characters excludes none of the anchored rungs and constrains a hypothetical reversal witness only on its verbatim routes. The crossing count is defined through output influence, which constant reconstruction cannot fake; that is the sense in which it carries the suggestion above.

    Finally, the obstruction the ladder measures is \emph{reversal-specific}. The canonical functions of the two sibling questions -- the longest $b$-free prefix (truncation at the leftmost $b$, the left end of the once-problem's probe, ``delete the leftmost $b$'') and the right-to-left pass $[\,b/aa\,]^{\mathrm{R}}$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}'s separating witness) -- have $\chi = 0$ on \emph{every} binary input of length $\leq 12$ ($8{,}191$ inputs each): a prefix cut and a local halving reorder nothing globally. A crossing-count proof that $\mathrm{rev} \notin L$ therefore cannot double for either sibling; unbounded reordering, the computed needle, and residue routing are three obstructions, and the ladder prices only the first.
\end{remark}

Beyond the two hinges:
\begin{enumerate} \setcounter{enumi}{2}
    \item Conjecture~\ref{conj:ic} (IC): no total injective growing core Markov expression. Either answer reorganizes the Markov row.
    \item The two incomparability witnesses for the unsafe calculus: is $X \mapsto X^{|X|}$ unsafe-reachable? Is the a-flood $L$-reachable?
    \item Is one-rule termination under the leftmost strategy decidable (Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v))? The known decidable classes for the unrestricted problem -- length-decreasing, length-preserving, left-hand sides of the shape $a^*b^*$ \cite{senizergues96}, inhibitors \cite{mcnaughton01}, grid \cite{geser02}, one-overlap-pair \cite{geser03}, single-threaded \cite{moczydlowski05} -- decide a different question, and the unrestricted problem itself is open \cite{geser01}.
    \item Is there a growth dichotomy for total restart rules? Among the 162 total binary rules with $|A|, |B| \leq 3$, 140 have linearly bounded output, 18 grow superlinearly but no faster than $3n$, and exactly 4 grow exponentially -- the amplifier $[\,baa/ab\,]$, its reversal, and their two renamings; nothing in between was found. Growth of one-rule systems is the classical subject of derivational complexity \cite{kobayashi01}.
    \item The exact characterization of the reachable class for each variant -- open problem 1 of Section~4, now sevenfold.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
    \emph{Methodology.} The computational statements in this section were verified by exhaustive enumeration over the finite domains stated where they occur: all strings up to the given lengths over the stated alphabets, all patterns and replacements up to the given lengths, both choices of the encoding characters where they matter. The negative claims rest on breadth-first search in function space over bounded expression depths, with every candidate found re-verified on strictly larger domains before being believed -- a necessary discipline, since short-domain artifacts are real: three halvings $[\,a/aa\,]$ collapse exactly the $a$-runs of length at most $8$ and fail at length $9$, and an iterated positional node computes $\mathtt{dec}$ exactly on the even lengths and fails on the odd ones. Every enumeration and search reported here is reproducible from the stated domains in at most hours on a single core.
\end{remark}

\section{Alphabet Invariance}
\label{sec:alphabet}

Every construction so far has fixed its alphabet, and two letters always sufficed. This section addresses the fourth open problem of Section~\ref{sec:limits} head on: \emph{which functions are reachable over every alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$, and does a larger alphabet ever add power?} Write $f \in L_\Sigma$ for: $f$ is reachable by an expression over $\Sigma$ -- the landscape's notation. Up to a canonical conjugation, the class does not depend on the alphabet at all: for any two alphabets of at least two letters and any \emph{good coding} between them, $f \in L_\Sigma$ iff its conjugate $f^c \in L_\Gamma$. The boundary of the invariance is exactly $|\Sigma| = 1$: the conjugation exists one way and cannot exist the other, for a structural reason. As corollaries, the two hinges of the landscape are each a single question rather than one per alphabet: the once node and the right pass are coding-equivariant, and reversal over one alphabet of at least two letters is reachable iff over every.

\subsection{Comma-Free Codings}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Good Codings})
    \label{def:code}
    A \emph{coding} is an injective monoid homomorphism $c : \Sigma^* \rightarrow \Gamma^*$ with $D_c \triangleq c(\Sigma)$ of uniform length $\ell \geq 1$ (a \emph{dictionary}); write $c(S)$ for the letter-by-letter extension. The coding is \emph{good} if $D_c$ is \emph{comma-free} (Golomb's condition \cite{golomb58}; the theory of codes treats it under synchronizing codes \cite{berstel85,lothaire97}): no $w \in D_c$ occurs in $u \cdot v$ at any of the junction-straddling positions $1, \dots, \ell-1$ ($u, v \in D_c$).
\end{definition}

Comma-freeness is the Golomb analogue of the Comma Code Lemma for $\mathtt{enc}^2_{b,x}$ (blocks $xc$): it is exactly the self-synchronization that makes a coding usable inside a scanning calculus. Without it the conjugacy class of a function depends on the coding, and the notion degenerates: a free assignment with $\ell(SS) = \ell(S)^2$, say, makes $X \mapsto XX$ conjugate to the unary squaring map. The good codings are the ones on which the conjugation is canonical.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Existence: the Double-$a$ Family})
    \label{lem:dict}
    Let $\Gamma$ have two distinct letters $a \ne b$ and $\ell \geq 3$. Then
    \begin{align}
        D_\ell \;\triangleq\; \{\, a\,a\,m\,b \;:\; m \in \Gamma^{\ell-3},\ aa \not\le m,\ m[0] \ne a \,\}
    \end{align}
    is comma-free, of uniform length $\ell$; over the binary alphabet $|D_\ell| = \mathrm{Fib}(\ell-2)$ (Fibonacci numbers, $\mathrm{Fib}(1) = \mathrm{Fib}(2) = 1$). So good codings exist between any two alphabets with at least two letters, in both directions.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Every codeword starts with $aa$, contains $aa$ only at position $0$ (the interior $m$ avoids $aa$ and, for $\ell > 3$, does not start with $a$), and ends with $b$. In $u \cdot v$ the factor $aa$ therefore occurs only at $0$ and $\ell$ (the junction reads $b \cdot a$), and any occurrence of a codeword, starting as it does with $aa$, starts at $0$ or $\ell$ -- aligned. The count: $m$ is $b$ followed by an $aa$-free word of length $\ell-4$, counted by $\mathrm{Fib}(\ell-2)$. (Verified: comma-freeness of all $21$ dictionaries in the family, binary/ternary/quaternary, $\ell = 3..9$; sizes at $\ell = 3..12$ match $\mathrm{Fib}(\ell-2)$.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Self-Synchronization})
    \label{lem:selfsync}
    Let $c$ be good and $B \ne \epsilon$. The occurrences of $c(B)$ in $c(C)$ are exactly $\{\, \ell \cdot s \;:\; s$ an occurrence of $B$ in $C \,\}$; in particular every occurrence of a codeword inside a codeword-sequence starts at a multiple of $\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Uniform lengths first: an occurrence of $w \in D_c$ (length $\ell$) lying wholly inside a single codeword (length $\ell$) of the surrounding sequence starts at offset $0$. Otherwise the occurrence straddles at least one junction $j\ell$ with $p < j\ell < p + \ell$, and its restrictions to the two flanking codewords exhibit $w$ in their product at the junction-straddling position $p - (j{-}1)\ell \in [1, \ell-1]$ -- against comma-freeness. For the block $c(B)$: its first codeword $c(B[0]) \in D_c$ occurs at a multiple of $\ell$ by the first part, the block then covers whole codewords of $c(C)$, and these spell $B$; the converse is immediate. (Verified: all $B \in \{a,b,c\}^{\le 3} \setminus \{\epsilon\}$, $C \in \{a,b,c\}^{\le 5}$ -- $14{,}196$ cases, zero exceptions.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Pass Transfer})
    \label{lem:pass-transfer}
    Let $c$ be good. For all $A, C \in \Sigma^*$ and $B \in \Sigma^* \setminus \{\epsilon\}$,
    \begin{align}
        [\,c(A)/c(B)\,]\; c(C) \;=\; c\big([\,A/B\,]C\big),
    \end{align}
    and the same identity holds for the once, rightmost, and $k$-th occurrence primitives (Definitions~\ref{def:once} and~\ref{def:r2l}, Proposition~\ref{prop:kth}): the greedy occurrence lists correspond order-isomorphically under $s \mapsto \ell s$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Induction along the scan. By Self-Synchronization the occurrence lists correspond, and the correspondence preserves order and disjointness (positions scale by $\ell$), so leftmost, $k$-th and rightmost all transfer; it is direction-free, so the right-to-left variant transfers too. For the full pass, write $C = X B Y$ with $|X|$ minimal: the first occurrence of $c(B)$ in $c(X)\,c(B)\,c(Y)$ is the aligned one at $\ell|X|$ (any earlier one would be an aligned occurrence inside $c(X)$, i.e.\ an occurrence of $B$ in $X$), the scan resumes at $\ell(|X|{+}|B|)$ -- aligned again, at the start of $c(Y)$ -- and the inserted $c(A)$ is never re-entered, matching the never-rescan clause of Definition~\ref{def:subst}. Both sides now split as $c([A/B]X) \cdot c(A) \cdot c([A/B]Y)$ with $|X|, |Y| < |C|$. (Verified: $A, C \in \{a,b,c\}^{\le 3}$, $B \in \{a,b,c\}^{1..3}$ -- $188{,}760$ leftmost cases; once, rightmost and $k$-th ($k \le 3$) on the smaller box -- $31{,}200$ cases; zero mismatches.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Transfer Theorem}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Conjugate})
    \label{def:conjugate}
    For $f$ an $n$-ary partial function on $\Sigma^*$ and $c : \Sigma \rightarrow \Gamma^\ell$ a good coding, the \emph{conjugate} $f^c : c(\Sigma^*)^n \rightharpoonup c(\Sigma^*)$ is $c \circ f \circ c^{-1}$ -- a partial function in the sense of the restriction clause of Definition~\ref{def:reachable}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Direction 1})
    \label{thm:transfer-forward}
    Let $E \in \mathrm{Exp}_n$ over $\Sigma$ and $c$ good. Define $E^c$ by: constants $W \mapsto c(W)$, variables and both constructors unchanged. Then
    \begin{align}
        \llbracket E^c \rrbracket\big(c(\vec S)\big) \;=\; c\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(\vec S)\big)
    \end{align}
    whenever the right-hand side is defined, and the two sides are undefined together (the pattern is $\epsilon$ on one side iff on the other). Moreover $|E^c| = |E|$ (node count; constant symbols grow $\ell$-fold in word length), $\deg(E^c) = \deg(E)$, and $\mathrm{Safe}(E) \Rightarrow \mathrm{Safe}(E^c)$. Finally, if $\llbracket E \rrbracket$ is total, a \emph{total} $\Gamma$-expression agreeing with $E^c$ on $c(\Sigma^*)^n$ exists: replace, simultaneously, every pattern sub-expression $P^c$ of $E^c$ by $\gamma(P^c)$, where $\gamma(X) \triangleq \mathtt{if}(\mathtt{eq}(X, \epsilon), b, X)$ for a fixed letter $b \in \Gamma$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Induction on $E$. Variables and constants are immediate; concatenation commutes with $c$ because $c$ is a homomorphism. For the pass node the patterns and replacements are the $c$-images of the sub-values, and Pass Transfer applies at every value of the sub-expressions; the definedness clause is the $\epsilon$-condition, and $c(B) = \epsilon$ iff $B = \epsilon$. Safety and degree are structural ($c(W)$ is a nonempty constant iff $W$ is, so $\mathrm{Anch}$ transfers; $\deg$ sums over the scrutinee and replacement of a pass, and each slot's transfer preserves it by induction, constants having degree $0$ on both sides). For the total extension: on inputs from $c(\Sigma^*)^n$ every sub-value of $E^c$ is itself a $c$-image (apply the equation to each sub-expression), and every pattern sub-value is nonempty -- a total $\llbracket E \rrbracket$ forces nonempty pattern values on \emph{every} input, by strictness -- so $\gamma$ acts as the identity exactly on those, and the wrapped expression computes $E^c$'s values on the image; and a $\gamma$-output is never empty while every other node type is total, so the wrapped expression is total everywhere. The guard sits on the pattern, as in the total variant of $\mathrm{rep}_n$ (Section~\ref{sec:toolkit}). (Verified: all $2{,}954$ depth-$\le 1$ expressions over $\{a,b,c\}$ with constants $\le 2$, on all $121$ inputs of length $\le 4$ -- $357{,}434$ evaluations, $0$ mismatches, $23{,}912$ undefined on both sides; $48{,}000$ further evaluations of random depth-$\le 3$ expressions; the same under once and rightmost semantics, $118{,}160$ evaluations each; degree and safety over the full space, $2{,}954$ expressions, $0$ violations; node count equal in every case.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Composition})
    \label{lem:compose}
    If $c : \Sigma \rightarrow \Gamma^{\ell_c}$ and $d : \Gamma \rightarrow \Sigma^{\ell_d}$ are good, then $d \circ c$ is good: $\{d(w) : w \in D_c\}$ is comma-free over $\Sigma$, of uniform length $\ell_c\ell_d$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    An occurrence of $d(w)$ inside a sequence of $d$-images of $D_c$-codewords begins with $d(w[0]) \in D_d$, which by Self-Synchronization over $D_d$ starts on the $D_d$-grid; the whole block $d(w)$ then covers $D_d$-codewords spelling $d(w[0]) \cdots d(w[\ell_c{-}1])$, so by injectivity of $d$ the word $w$ occurs among the pre-image codewords, and if the block did not start on the outer ($\ell_c\ell_d$-) grid, $w$ would occur in a product of two $D_c$-codewords at a junction-straddling position -- against the comma-freeness of $D_c$. (Verified: $\{d(c(\sigma))\}$ for $c : \{a,b,c\} \rightarrow \{x,y\}^6$ and $d : \{x,y\} \rightarrow \{a,b,c\}^4$, length-$24$ dictionary, comma-free.)
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Retraction})
    \label{lem:retract}
    Let $h = d \circ c : \Sigma \rightarrow \Sigma^{\ell_c\ell_d}$ with $c, d$ good. Then $h$ is good, and both $h$ and a decoder $H_h$ with $H_h \circ h = \mathrm{id}$ are \emph{total} $L$-reachable: $h$ is $\mathrm{rep}_{|\Sigma|}$ with the single characters as patterns and the $h$-codewords as replacements, $H_h$ the same with the roles exchanged -- $\mathrm{rep}_n$ places no restriction on its patterns (Theorem~\ref{lem:multiple substitution}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    Comma-freeness is Lemma~\ref{lem:compose}; on an $h$-image the codeword occurrences are aligned (Self-Synchronization), so the freezing semantics of $\mathrm{rep}_n$ tiles the text exactly, replacing each $h(\sigma)$ by $\sigma$. Both $\mathrm{rep}_n$ have constant, nonempty patterns, hence are total. (Verified: the $h$- and $H_h$-expressions against their intended semantics on all $40$ inputs of length $\le 3$ over $\{a,b,c\}$; and as part of the roundtrip below.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Transfer})
    \label{thm:transfer}
    For all finite $\Sigma, \Gamma$ with $|\Sigma|, |\Gamma| \geq 2$, every good coding $c$, and every partial function $f$ on $\Sigma^*$:
    \begin{align}
        f \in L_\Sigma \;\iff\; f^c \in L_\Gamma,
    \end{align}
    and when $f$ is total the $\Gamma$-witness of the forward direction may be taken total (Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer-forward}). The $\Sigma$-witness of the backward direction is
    \begin{align}
        H_h\big(E'_d(h(X_1), \dots, h(X_n))\big),
    \end{align}
    where $E'$ is the given $\Gamma$-witness of $f^c$, $E'_d = (E')^d$, $d$ is any good coding $\Gamma \rightarrow \Sigma^{\ell_d}$, and $h = d \circ c$; its node count is $|E'|$ plus a constant depending only on the two alphabets.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Direction 1 is Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer-forward}. For Direction 2 let $E'$ over $\Gamma$ witness $f^c$. Every $h(X_i) = d(c(X_i))$ lies in $\mathrm{image}(d)$, where $E'_d$ computes $d$ of the $E'$-value (Direction 1, for the coding $d$); so
    \begin{align*}
        \llbracket E'_d \rrbracket\big(h(\vec X)\big)
        \;=\; d\big(\llbracket E' \rrbracket(c(\vec X))\big)
        \;=\; d\big(c(f(\vec X))\big) \;=\; h\big(f(\vec X)\big),
    \end{align*}
    and $H_h$ returns $f(\vec X)$ by the Retraction lemma. Definedness tracks $f$'s: $h$ and $H_h$ are total, and $E'_d$ is defined at the $h$-images exactly when $E'$ is at the $c(\vec X)$ -- strictness carries $E'$'s undefinedness through the total wrappers unchanged. The node-count claim follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer-forward} and the constant patterns of the two $\mathrm{rep}_n$. (Verified: the full roundtrip $E_0 \mapsto E' = E_0^c \mapsto E_{\mathrm{final}}$ on all $155$ depth-$\le 1$ expressions with constants $\le 1$, on all $40$ inputs of length $\le 3$ -- $6{,}200$ evaluations, $0$ mismatches, $1{,}025$ undefined on both sides; $\deg E_{\mathrm{final}} = \deg E_0$ in all $155$ cases, node overhead $528$ for the $(3,2)$-alphabet pair.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{Alphabet-Uniform Questions})
    \label{cor:uniform}
    The right pass $\mathrm{r}$ (the collapse criterion of Theorem~\ref{thm:union-collapse}), the once node $[\,X_1/X_2\,]_1 X_3$ read as a three-argument function, and every $k$-th-occurrence node are \emph{coding-equivariant} -- each commutes with every good coding, by Lemma~\ref{lem:pass-transfer} -- so each of these reachability questions is a single question, not one per alphabet: the answer over one alphabet with at least two letters is the answer over every. The same holds for $\mathrm{rev}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rev-uniform} below. Consequently every negative search of Sections~\ref{sec:calculus}--\ref{sec:variants} run over one alphabet excludes the conjugate over \emph{every} alphabet of at least two letters, and Conjecture~\ref{conj:union}, if true over one alphabet, is true over every.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
    Equivariance plus Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer}: if $F$ commutes with the good coding $e$, then a $\Sigma$-witness for $F$ conjugates along $c$ to a $\Gamma$-function which, composed with the retraction $q = c \circ e$, computes the native $F$ over $\Gamma$ -- the identical construction as in Theorem~\ref{thm:rev-uniform}, with the equivariance of Lemma~\ref{lem:pass-transfer} in place of the palindrome clause. Concretely, for $e : \Gamma \rightarrow \Sigma^k$ good and $q = c \circ e$:
    \begin{align*}
        \llbracket E^c \rrbracket\big(q(\vec T)\big) \;=\; c\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket(e(\vec T))\big) \;=\; c\big(F(e(\vec T))\big) \;=\; c\big(e(F(\vec T))\big) \;=\; q\big(F(\vec T)\big),
    \end{align*}
    and $H_q$ returns $F(\vec T)$. (Verified end to end: the pass node over $\{a,b,c\}$ transferred to $\{x,y\}$ and retracted -- a full binary expression in the leftmost calculus, $294$ evaluations, $0$ mismatches; once, rightmost and second occurrence as semantic compositions, $882$ evaluations, $0$ mismatches; equivariance of all four passes under the two retraction codings, $2{,}352$ cases, $0$ mismatches; the doubling function as a further end-to-end witness, $31$ inputs.)
\end{proof}

\subsection{Reversal}

Reversal is the one equivariance that codings do not enjoy for free: a letter-by-letter reversal reverses \emph{each codeword individually}, so $c(\mathrm{rev}\,S) = \mathrm{rev}(c(S))$ holds iff every codeword is a palindrome -- a false claim in an earlier draft of this section, caught by the verification battery ($60$ failures on $31$ inputs). The naive ``rev on the code image'' is not the reversal of the image; the repair is to retract along a coding whose outer half is palindromic.

\begin{lemma}
    (\emph{Palindromic Dictionaries})
    \label{lem:palindrome}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $c(\mathrm{rev}\,S) = \mathrm{rev}(c(S))$ for all $S$ iff every codeword of $c$ is a palindrome; then $e(\mathrm{rev}\,T) = \mathrm{rev}(e(T))$ for every such $e$.
        \item (\emph{Middle-marker family.}) For $\mu \in \Sigma$ and $m \geq 1$, $\{\, \alpha\,\mu\,\mathrm{rev}(\alpha) : \alpha \in (\Sigma \setminus \mu)^m \,\}$ is comma-free and palindromic, of size $(|\Sigma|-1)^m$ -- unbounded for $|\Sigma| \geq 3$.
        \item Over the binary alphabet the family degenerates (size 1), but size-3 palindromic comma-free dictionaries exist: $\{\mathtt{aabaa}, \mathtt{ababa}, \mathtt{abbba}\}$ and $\{\mathtt{aabaa}, \mathtt{babab}, \mathtt{bbabb}\}$.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
    (i) is immediate on one-letter words. (ii) $\mu$ occurs in each codeword only at the exact middle, and in $u \cdot v$ only at the two middles; an occurrence of a codeword has its $\mu$ at $p + m$, hence $p \in \{0, 2m+1\}$ -- aligned. (iii) by inspection. (Verified: (ii) $20$ dictionaries, ternary and quaternary, $m \le 5$, all comma-free with the stated sizes; (iii) both dictionaries comma-free; exact maxima of palindromic comma-free sets over binary, by branch and bound: $1, 1, 3, 2, 7, 14$ at $\ell = 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9$ -- data, not a formula.)
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Reversal Uniformity})
    \label{thm:rev-uniform}
    $\mathrm{rev} \in L$ over one alphabet with at least two letters iff over every: given a witness $E$ over $\Sigma$, a good $c : \Sigma \rightarrow \Gamma^\ell$, and a good \emph{palindromic} $e : \Gamma \rightarrow \Sigma^k$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:palindrome}), $q \triangleq c \circ e$ gives
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{rev}_\Gamma \;=\; H_q \circ \llbracket E^c \rrbracket \circ q .
    \end{align}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    $q(T) = c(e(T))$, so $\llbracket E^c \rrbracket(q(T)) = c(\mathrm{rev}\, e(T))$ by Direction 1; the palindrome clause gives $\mathrm{rev}\, e(T) = e(\mathrm{rev}\, T)$, hence $\llbracket E^c \rrbracket(q(T)) = c(e(\mathrm{rev}\,T)) = q(\mathrm{rev}\,T)$, and $H_q$ returns $\mathrm{rev}\,T$. The needed dictionaries exist for every pair of alphabets: from a source of at least three letters use the middle-marker family for $e$; from a binary source, first move to a ternary alphabet along the size-3 binary dictionary of Lemma~\ref{lem:palindrome}(iii) -- a coding of three letters needs three codewords, and three exist -- and then proceed as before; $c$ itself is never required to be palindromic (Lemma~\ref{lem:dict} suffices), and $D_q = \{c(e(\gamma))\}$ is comma-free by Lemma~\ref{lem:compose}. (Verified: $e = \{\mathtt{x} \mapsto \mathtt{bab}, \mathtt{y} \mapsto \mathtt{cac}\}$ over $\Sigma = \{\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b},\mathtt{c}\}$, $c$ the length-6 double-$a$ coding to $\{\mathtt{x},\mathtt{y}\}$; $q$ and $H_q$ as \emph{expressions}, with the oracle $D = c \circ \mathrm{rev} \circ c^{-1}$ standing in for $\llbracket E^c \rrbracket$ ($\mathrm{rev}$-reachability being the hypothesis): $H_q(D(q(T))) = \mathrm{rev}\,T$ on all $63$ inputs of length $\le 5$, zero failures.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{No Contradiction with the Native Results})
    \label{rem:native}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} conjugates; Propositions~\ref{prop:del-leftmost} and~\ref{prop:kth} speak of \emph{native} functions, and the two live in different conjugacy classes. The binary witness $W$ of the leftmost deletion transfers to a ternary alphabet as the \emph{block-level} function $[\,\epsilon/c(b)\,]_1$ on the image of $c$ -- it deletes an $\ell$-block, not a character, and is not the native ternary $[\,\epsilon/\gamma\,]_1$; the negative ternary searches could not have seen the transferred witness in any case, its depth after transfer and retraction being far beyond the exhaustive depth-3 and randomized $4$--$7$-pass spaces. Nor is the $\ell = 1$ coding a loop-hole: an injective character map is (trivially) good, and conjugating the binary once-deletion along it yields the native ternary function \emph{restricted to a two-letter sub-alphabet} -- reachable as a partial function, exactly the anchored-domain face of the phenomenon, and consistent with the resistance being about general position over three letters. Finally, the once-calculus -- and the rightmost and $k$-th-occurrence calculi -- have transfer theorems of their own: Lemma~\ref{lem:pass-transfer} is scan-direction- and multiplicity-agnostic, the first coded occurrence being the coding of the first native one. The marker-freshness obstruction of Proposition~\ref{prop:kth}(iii) is a statement about native simulation within one alphabet and is untouched.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Unary Edge}

Section~\ref{sec:variants} tracks the unary case wherever it differs; the transfer theorem now locates its boundary exactly.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{The Boundary $|\Sigma| = 1$})
    \label{prop:unary-edge}
    \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
        \item $c(\mathtt{a}) = \mathtt{ab}$ is good ($\{w\}$ is comma-free iff $w$ is primitive), and every unary-reachable function is the restriction of a binary-reachable one.
        \item There is no injective monoid homomorphism $\{\mathtt{a}, \mathtt{b}\}^* \rightarrow \{\mathtt{a}\}^*$: any $h$ has $h(\mathtt{ab}) = h(\mathtt{a})h(\mathtt{b}) = h(\mathtt{b})h(\mathtt{a}) = h(\mathtt{ba})$ while $\mathtt{ab} \ne \mathtt{ba}$. So no coding-conjugation crosses the $|\Sigma| = 1$ boundary from above, and the hypothesis $|\Sigma|, |\Gamma| \geq 2$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} cannot be dropped on that side.
        \item Every unary $L$-expression computes a length map in the closure of $\{n \mapsto n,\ n \mapsto k\}$ under pointwise sum (concatenation) and $(g,r,p) \mapsto \big(n \mapsto r(n) \lfloor g(n)/p(n) \rfloor + g(n) \bmod p(n)\big)$ (the pass; the constant case is $n \mapsto i\lfloor n/j \rfloor + n \bmod j$, and $[\,X_1/a\,]X_1$ squares). Every such map is eventually polynomially bounded, so $n \mapsto 2^n$ is unreachable at any size -- the unary face of Theorem~\ref{thm:fp}'s growth bound. The floor-halving $n \mapsto \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$ -- the needle $H$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:unary-once} -- is absent from every unary expression of size $\le 7$ ($36{,}978$ expressions, $2{,}091$ distinct length maps on $n \le 24$, plus $3{,}000$ random ones of depth $\le 4$): every single unary pass leaks its residue $n \bmod j$, and subtraction is not among the closures.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
    (i) is Direction 1 for the coding $c$ (verified: all $258$ depth-$\le 1$ unary expressions on $a^0..a^{24}$, $6{,}450$ evaluations, $0$ mismatches, plus $12{,}000$ deeper-random; alignment and the pass law, $566$ cases). (ii) is the displayed computation. (iii): the pass law is the greedy scan of a unary string (verified: $20$ $(i,j)$ pairs); the growth bound is an induction -- a pass outputs at most $|R| \cdot \lfloor |E|/|P| \rfloor \cdot |P| + |E| \le |R||E| + |E|$ characters; the halving search is exhaustive at size $\le 7$ ($\lceil n/2 \rceil$ and $n \bmod 2$ are present at size 4, $n^4$ at size 10 via $[\,[X/a]X / a\,]([X/a]X)$, verified). Of the total binary length profiles $n \mapsto |\llbracket E \rrbracket(a^n)|$ with $n \le 12$ arising from the depth-$\le 1$ binary space and $2{,}000$ random depth-$\le 3$ expressions ($480$ in the reference run; an independent resampling agrees), exactly two are realized by no size-$\le 7$ unary expression nor any constant shift of one -- bounded-search evidence for the strictness of (i)'s converse.
\end{proof}

\section{Recursive Definitions}
\label{sec:recursion}

The \texttt{meow} language recurses: a macro body may call macros, itself included, and the calls may be mutual. This section adds exactly that to the calculus and asks what it buys. The primitive stays raw -- the pass of Definition~\ref{def:subst} over the toolkit of Section~\ref{sec:toolkit}, itself substitution-expressible, with no new constructs -- and everything that varies is the \emph{runtime}: when a sub-expression's value is demanded. Four runtimes span the space between \emph{nothing new} and \emph{everything}, and the pivot turns out to be a clause of Definition~\ref{def:subst} that has been there all along.

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Recursive programs})
    \label{def:recprog}
    A \emph{program} is a finite list of distinct names $f_1, \dots, f_k$, the $j$-th with arity $m_j$ and body $B_j$ built as in Definition~\ref{def:exp} from $f_j$'s parameters and the constants, extended by one node: the call $f_{j'}(E_1, \dots, E_{m_{j'}})$. Its \emph{call graph} has the edge $j \to j'$ when $B_j$ mentions $f_{j'}$; the program is \emph{recursive} when the graph has a directed cycle, and $C$ is the set of indices that can reach one.
\end{definition}

Four runtimes, in order of increasing laziness:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{Eager.} Call by value: the denotation is the least fixpoint of $\Phi(\rho)_j \triangleq \llbracket B_j \rrbracket_\rho$ (call nodes read through $\rho$), over tuples of partial functions ordered by graph inclusion.
    \item \emph{Lazy arguments.} Call by need: arguments are thunks, forced on demand; every constructor of Definition~\ref{def:den} remains strict in all its parts.
    \item \emph{Lazy passes.} Call by need, plus the one non-strictness the operator itself already possesses, made official: in $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ the pattern and the scrutinee are forced, but $R$ only if $P$ occurs in the value of $E$ -- justified by the operator equation $[\,A/B\,]S = S$ when $B \not\subset S$, \emph{regardless of $A$} (Definition~\ref{def:subst}).
    \item \emph{Streams.} Values are finite or infinite strings, and the pass is the causal process of Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}; termination gives way to productivity (Section~\ref{sec:rec-streams}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{The runtimes as dynamics})
    \label{def:dynamics}
    The first three runtimes are structural dynamics: rewrite relations on
    closed expressions, the strings being the values.  Write $B_j[X_i := E_i]$
    for the simultaneous substitution of Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}.  The axiom
    schemes, all closed under the runtime's evaluation contexts:
    \begin{mathpar}
        (\mathrm{cat})\;\; \frac{}{W_1\,W_2 \;\to\; W_1W_2}
        \and
        (\mathrm{fire})\;\; \frac{W_P \neq \epsilon \quad W_P \subseteq W_S}
        {[\,W_A/W_P\,]\,W_S \;\to\; [\,W_A/W_P\,](W_S)}
        \and
        (\mathrm{inert}_{=})\;\; \frac{W_P \neq \epsilon \quad W_P \not\subset W_S}
        {[\,W_A/W_P\,]\,W_S \;\to\; W_S}
        \and
        (\mathrm{inert}_{\epsilon})\;\; \frac{W_P \neq \epsilon \quad W_P \not\subset W_S}
        {[\,R/W_P\,]\,W_S \;\to\; W_S}
        \and
        (\mathrm{call}_{v})\;\; \frac{}{f_j(W_1, \dots, W_{m_j}) \;\to\; B_j[X_i := W_i]}
        \and
        (\mathrm{call}_{n})\;\; \frac{}{f_j(E_1, \dots, E_{m_j}) \;\to\; B_j[X_i := E_i]}
    \end{mathpar}
    Concatenation and firing are shared; $\mathrm{inert}_{=}$ (all three
    slots values -- the pass strict in every part) and $\mathrm{call}_{v}$
    (call by value) are the \emph{eager} rules; $\mathrm{inert}_{=}$ and
    $\mathrm{call}_{n}$ the \emph{lazy-argument} rules;
    $\mathrm{inert}_{\epsilon}$ -- the replacement an arbitrary,
    discarded expression, which is the operator equation
    $[\,A/B\,]S = S$ of Definition~\ref{def:subst} made operational --
    and $\mathrm{call}_{n}$ the \emph{lazy-pass} rules.  The evaluation
    contexts are the whole difference.  \emph{Eager}: the hole may sit in
    any child of a concatenation, in any slot of a pass, in any argument
    of a call.  \emph{Lazy arguments}: any child of a concatenation and
    any slot of a pass -- a call's arguments are not contexts, they
    travel as thunks.  \emph{Lazy passes}: any child of a concatenation,
    the pattern or scrutinee slot of a pass, and the replacement slot
    only under the \emph{guard} that the other two slots are values and
    the pattern occurs in the scrutinee's value.  A run halting at a
    string denotes it; a closed non-value admitting no step is
    \emph{stuck} (an empty pattern, or a forced stuck sub-expression);
    otherwise it diverges.  The runtime's partial function is defined
    exactly on the halting runs.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
    \label{rem:dynamics}
    The dynamics are the three runtimes of the list, made precise: the
    eager relation computes the least-fixpoint denotation of
    Theorem~\ref{thm:eager}, the lazy-argument relation the liveness
    characterization of Theorem~\ref{thm:lazyargs} (the call is by name;
    the runtime's shared thunks force each live argument at most once,
    and by the liveness characterization the forced positions -- hence
    the values and the defined inputs -- are the same), and the
    lazy-pass relation is the machine of Proposition~\ref{prop:lpcons}.
    The rules fix \emph{what} is forced, not the order: the value and
    the defined inputs are order-independent in all three runtimes, while
    the split of the undefined runs into stuck and divergent is not
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:eager} records this for eager).  The replacement
    slot's guard is a neededness criterion in the sense of Huet and
    L\'evy \cite{huetlevy79}: the replacement of $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ is needed
    exactly when $P$ occurs.  (Verified: the nine canonical programs --
    the gate $[\,g(X)/b\,]X$ open and closed, the dead-argument
    separator of Theorem~\ref{thm:lazyargs} under all three runtimes,
    the mutual ping-pong, the empty pattern, the constant-scrutinee fire
    -- machine-run against hand-computed outcomes by an implementation of
    these rules, all correct; the implementation, with the rules as its
    only specification, is \texttt{research/scratch/rec/dynamics.cpp}.)
\end{remark}

\subsection{Eager and Lazy Arguments: Recursion Is Inert}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Eager inertness})
    \label{thm:eager}
    Over any finite alphabet: if $j \in C$ then $\llbracket f_j \rrbracket$ is undefined on every input, whatever the bodies contain, and no run of $f_j$ ever halts with a value (an error, or an infinite descent; both modes occur). If $j \notin C$ then no run of $f_j$ ever diverges -- every input ends in a value or an error -- and $\llbracket f_j \rrbracket = \llbracket E_j \rrbracket$ for a plain expression $E_j \in \mathrm{Exp}_{m_j}$, the strict inlining of the acyclic calls. The eager recursive calculus denotes exactly the partial functions of $L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Every constructor of Definition~\ref{def:den} is strict in all its sub-values, so undefinedness at any evaluated position propagates to the root. If $j \in C$, some call node of $B_j$ reaches a definition of $C$, and a Kleene induction gives $\varphi^n_j = \bot$ for every $n$: the callee's approximant is everywhere $\bot$, and strictness carries $\bot$ from the node through the body -- for every choice of bodies and every input. (The denotational and operational readings agree throughout; the Err/Div split is evaluation-order-dependent, the Val/never-Val split is not.) If $j \notin C$, the region of the call graph below $j$ is a DAG of depth $D(j)$, and an induction on call-depth shows that no evaluation with fuel $\geq D(j)$ is ever cut short: no input diverges. Were some input to diverge, the tree of body entries would be infinite and finitely branching, so K\"onig's lemma would give an infinite branch -- an infinite walk in the finite call graph, which revisits a node, a cycle reachable from $j$. The inlining: unfold the DAG in topological order, each call node replaced by the callee's body with the arguments substituted. Lemma~\ref{lem:beta}'s occurrence clause is what makes the substitution delicate -- plain $\beta$-inlining is call-by-name flavored, while the eager call is strict in \emph{all} arguments, the dead ones included -- so each discarded argument is forced through the identity passes of Corollary~\ref{cor:closure}'s proof before it is dropped. Conversely $L$ is contained: the one-definition program with body $E$. (Verified: three independent evaluators -- a memoized Kleene chain, fuel-capped operational runs, and evaluation of the syntactic inlining -- agree on all hand-written and $450$ random programs: $31{,}995$ checks, no disagreement.)
\end{proof}

Two consequences deserve the record. Since every eager denotation is an $L$ denotation, the whole theory of Sections~\ref{sec:calculus}--\ref{sec:limits} applies verbatim -- safety, pipeline normal form, the polynomial-time bound; and safety can be read off the program directly ($j \notin C$, safe bodies). And the failure modes are now separated by a graph property: membership in $C$ is reachability, decidable in linear time -- a definition in $C$ is incapacitated \emph{by the recursion itself}, on every input and for every body, while outside $C$ undefinedness is the inlining's own, data-dependent. (The inlining can be exponentially larger than the program: expressiveness is unaffected, but the naive interpreter need not run in the polynomial time of the function it computes; memoization restores it.)

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Lazy-argument inertness})
    \label{thm:lazyargs}
    Under lazy arguments, a thunk is forced exactly when its parameter is \emph{live}: liveness is the least fixpoint $S^*$ of the operator $(f,i) \in F(S)$ iff $X_i$ occurs in $B_f$ at an $S$-evaluated position -- a position reached from the root by descending through all constructor children and through exactly the argument slots with $(g,\cdot) \in S$. Least, not greatest: for $f(X) = g(X)$, $g(Y) = f(Y)$ nothing is ever forced -- each body is a single call whose argument is never demanded -- while the greatest fixpoint would force both. $S^*$ is syntactic, computable in polynomial time. Termination is input-independent, all-or-nothing: the run of a definition halts on every input or on none, according to whether the \emph{live dependency graph} has a cycle reachable from an evaluated position of the main body. When it does not, the live unfolding is finite and $\llbracket f_j \rrbracket = \llbracket E'_j \rrbracket$ for the plain expression obtained by pruning the dead calls and inlining the live ones. The lazy-argument recursive calculus denotes exactly the partial functions of $L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    In raw $L$ every occurrence of a variable forces its whole value -- patterns, replacements, scrutinees, concatenands all need complete strings -- so ``evaluated at all'' coincides with ``evaluated unconditionally'', and liveness closes under occurrence through live call slots: a parameter passed into a never-evaluated argument slot is never forced, iteratively to any depth; that is $F$ iterated from $\emptyset$, which stabilizes within $\sum_j m_j$ rounds. A call at a live position receives the same argument values whatever the input, so the live unfolding is a syntax-determined tree, finite exactly when the live dependency graph is acyclic below the root. Pruning the never-forced subtrees and inlining the rest preserves the denotation by Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} on defined values, and preserves undefinedness too: the pruned positions are exactly the never-evaluated ones, so no data-dependent undefinedness hides in them. (Verified: a $253$-program corpus, $13{,}141$ halting runs, exact agreement with the pruned unfolding in all of them; forcing confined to the live positions, also on the divergent runs.)
\end{proof}

Operationally the two runtimes are far apart; denotationally they coincide, and the gap is exactly the one Lemma~\ref{lem:beta} leaves open:

\begin{example}
    (\emph{Separation})
    \label{ex:separation}
    $F(X) = G(X, F(\mathtt{tail}\,X))$ with $G(A,B) = A$: the recursive call sits in a dead argument, so lazily $F$ is the identity while eagerly it never returns. No single definition separates the two runtimes (provably); the smallest separators have four nodes, and the smallest \emph{weak} separation -- disagreement on some input only -- needs no recursion at all. (Exhaustive search over all $68{,}101$ two-definition programs of size $\leq 7$ and all $622$ one-definition ones.)
\end{example}

\subsection{Lazy Passes: Universality}
\label{sec:rec-lazy}

Widening the operator's own non-strictness changes everything. On call-free programs the lazy-pass machine is conservative:

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Conservativity})
    \label{prop:lpcons}
    On programs without calls, the lazy-pass machine computes the eager denotation wherever that is defined, and is strictly more defined only through discarded replacements. (Verified: $9{,}000$ random call-free expressions -- of the $3{,}525$ on which the eager denotation is undefined, the machine was undefined on $3{,}200$ and strictly more defined on $325$, every one of them through a discarded replacement; on the $5{,}475$ defined ones, exact agreement; no mismatch.)
\end{proposition}

The strictness that makes recursion inert is the strictness of the \emph{scrutinee}: a pass $[\,R/P\,]\,E$ needs $P$ and $E$, but needs $R$ only when it fires. Under lazy passes that is the whole story, and recursion through the replacement slot becomes conditional -- a gate. A gate that is sound both ways takes care:

\begin{definition}
    (\emph{Two-way gate})
    \label{def:gate}
    Let $b \neq x$ in $\Sigma$ and $\top, \bot$ be as in Selection, and fix the constants $\mathbf{p} = bbx$ and $\mathbf{q} = xbb$ (both contain $bb$; neither occurs in the other). In the variables $X_1, X_2, X_3$,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{sel}(C, u, v) \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{dec}^2_{x}\big([\mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(X_3)/\mathbf{q}]\,[\mathtt{enc}^2_{x}(X_2)/\mathbf{p}]\;\mathtt{if}(X_1, \mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q})\big).
    \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Gate})
    \label{thm:gate}
    For $C \in \{\top, \bot\}$ and any $u, v$: $\mathtt{sel}(\top, u, v) = u$ and $\mathtt{sel}(\bot, u, v) = v$, the taken branch's value verbatim; the machine forces the condition and exactly the taken branch, once.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    By Selection, $\mathtt{if}(X_1, \mathbf{p}, \mathbf{q})$ is $\mathbf{p}$ or $\mathbf{q}$ as $X_1$ is $\top$ or $\bot$. If $C = \top$: the pass $[\mathtt{enc}^2(X_2)/\mathbf{p}]$ sees its pattern as its scrutinee, so it fires -- forcing $X_2$ -- and replaces the whole text by $\mathtt{enc}^2(u)$; the outer pass has pattern $\mathbf{q} = xbb \ni bb$, and $\mathtt{enc}^2$-images contain no $bb$ (Comma Code Lemma), so it is inert and $X_3$ is never forced. If $C = \bot$, symmetrically: $\mathbf{p} = bbx$ does not occur in $\mathbf{q} = xbb$, the $\mathbf{p}$-pass is inert, and the $\mathbf{q}$-pass fires and forces $X_3$. Either way $\mathtt{dec}^2_x \circ \mathtt{enc}^2_x = \mathrm{id}$ (Comma Code Lemma (i)) returns the branch verbatim. (Verified with forcing probes in the branch slots.)
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{The Selection was already a gate})
    \label{rem:ifgate}
    Section~\ref{sec:toolkit}'s construction places the branches only in \emph{replacement} slots: in $\mathtt{if}(C,X,Y)$, both $X$ and $Y$ travel inside the replacements $[\mathtt{enc}(X)/\top]$ and $[\mathtt{enc}(Y)/bb]$. Under lazy passes, $\mathtt{if}$ is therefore itself a two-way gate, and no new machinery is needed. Writing $\mathtt{isne}(S) \triangleq \mathtt{if}(\mathtt{eq}(S,\epsilon), \bot, \top)$ for the emptiness test,
    \begin{align}
        \mathtt{rev}(X) \;=\; \mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{isne}\,X,\;\mathtt{cat}(\mathtt{rev}(\mathtt{tail}\,X), \mathtt{head}\,X),\;\epsilon\big)
    \end{align}
    reverses every string (all $127$ of length $\leq 6$). This is also the minimal repair of the eager calculus's one visible totality defect: the guard that Remark~\ref{rem:total-rep} must work around -- an $\mathtt{if}$ on $\mathtt{eq}(X_i, \epsilon)$ whose dead branch holds the round -- becomes sound as written, the round being a discarded replacement when the gate is closed.
\end{remark}

\begin{example}
    (\emph{A naive gate is unsound})
    \label{ex:naivegate}
    The definition
    \begin{align*}
        F(X) \;\triangleq\; [\,F(\mathtt{tail}\,X)/a\,]\;\mathtt{if}(\mathtt{isne}\,X, a, a)
    \end{align*}
    \emph{looks} guarded -- the recursive call is a replacement, demanded only when the pattern $a$ occurs -- but the closed-gate value $a$ contains the gate pattern itself, and the pass fires inside the base value: $F(\epsilon)$ forces $F(\epsilon)$, and the intended base case is a loop. The two-way gate is immune by construction: the gate values are the constants $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{q}$, and the branch values travel as $\mathtt{enc}^2$-images, which no pattern of the construction can meet.
\end{example}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Universality})
    \label{thm:universal}
    The lazy-pass recursive calculus over any alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$ computes exactly the partial computable functions $(\Sigma^*)^n \rightharpoonup \Sigma^*$. Self-recursion suffices; mutual recursion is definable from it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Soundness is effectivity: each machine rule is primitive recursive in its data, so every denotation is partial computable. For completeness, work over a two-letter subalphabet $\{\mathtt{a}, \mathtt{b}\}$ (a fixed two-letter prefix code -- constant passes both ways, as in Section~\ref{sec:toolkit} -- reduces the general case to it). Code strings into $a$-tallies by Horner with a sentinel bit, $V(\epsilon) = a$, $V(cT) = a^{2V(T) + \mathrm{idx}(c)}$: injective, and computed by a structural recursion with $\mathtt{cat}$ and $\mathtt{head}$. Its inverse $\mathrm{DIGITS}$ recurses structurally as well, reading the parity with a flip and halving with the raw-$L$ pipeline $[\,a/b\,][\,\epsilon/a\,][\,b/aa\,]$ -- the mirror of the once-calculus's $H$ (Section~\ref{sec:variants}) -- so no new primitive is involved anywhere (verified: $\mathrm{DIGITS}(V(X)) = X$ on all $63$ strings of length $\leq 5$). By Minsky's theorem \cite{minsky67} there is a two-counter machine with $V(X) \mapsto V(f(X))$, and counter machines compile into guarded definitions: a configuration is the string $b^i\,a^{x+1}\,b\,a^{y+1}\,b$, each instruction is a bounded pattern scan with $\mathtt{eq}$-tests on the instruction counter, and the driver
    \begin{align}
        \mathrm{RUN}(c) \;=\; \mathtt{sel}\big(\mathtt{eq}(\mathtt{PC}(c), \epsilon),\; \mathtt{out}(c),\; \mathrm{RUN}(\mathtt{step}(c))\big)
    \end{align}
    is the pattern-gated while loop -- the recursive call is forced exactly while the machine has not halted. Then $f = \mathrm{DIGITS} \circ \mathrm{RUN} \circ V$. Executed end to end: a $\mu$-search for integer square roots ($15$ squares exact, $5$ non-squares valueless), a compiled doubling machine on $n \leq 5$, a compiled adder on $20$ configurations. Mutual recursion from self-recursion: one definition over tagged pairs, the projections plain passes (verified on the even/odd pair over tallies, both readings). (Also verified: agreement of the small-step machine with a big-step demand semantics on $400$ random recursive programs.)
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
    (\emph{The two barriers dissolve})
    \label{cor:barriers}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \emph{Reversal is computable} -- the second hinge of Section~\ref{sec:variants}, open for $L$ itself, dissolves one level up: Remark~\ref{rem:ifgate}'s three-line recursion. The hinge question for $L$ -- whether reversal is reachable by a \emph{bounded pipeline} -- is what remains.
        \item $X \mapsto X^{2^{|X|}}$, unreachable in $L$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:length}, is the total structural recursion
        \begin{align}
            A(S, W) \;=\; \mathtt{sel}\big(\mathtt{isne}\,S,\; A(\mathtt{tail}\,S, \mathtt{cat}(W,W)),\; W\big)
        \end{align}
        with $\mathrm{EXP}(X) = A(X, X)$ (verified on all $31$ strings of length $\leq 4$: $|X| = 4$ gives output length $64$). The degree machinery of Section~\ref{sec:limits} is a statement about pipelines (Proposition~\ref{prop:pipeline}); recursion is not a pipeline.
        \item \emph{Totality does not tame it.} The two restart nodes of Corollary~\ref{cor:towers}'s tower, rebuilt as gated while loops -- the once-primitive itself now a structural recursion -- and iterated down the input by $\mathtt{tail}$, give a total $\mathrm{TWR}(S) = a^{K_{|S|}}$ with $K_0 = 4$ and $K_{n+1} = 2^{K_n/2+1}-2$: verified $4, 6, 14, 254$ on all fifteen strings of length $\leq 3$, $K_4 = 2^{128}-2$ forced by the recursion, and the gated restarts agree with the restart process of Theorem~\ref{thm:amplifier} on $80$ random strings. Tower growth -- Corollary~\ref{cor:towers} for Markov -- is thus available already \emph{inside the total recursive programs}, and the polynomial bound fails for total definitions, not merely divergent ones.
        \item \emph{Totality of a definition is undecidable} (a two-counter machine halts on its initial configuration iff the compiled definition is total; Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}).
    \end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

Universality needed the gate, and the gate needs the loop: without self-reference, even the operator's own non-strictness leaves the reachable class exactly where it was. Proposition~\ref{prop:lpcons} located the call-free machine's extra definedness -- every point of it comes from a discarded replacement -- but did not say whether that definedness is \emph{realizable}: whether the lazy-pass denotation of a call-free expression is the partial function of some plain expression. It is, by an explicit translation.

Two small constructions carry it. First, the \emph{coercion}, fixing the two distinct characters $b, x \in \Sigma$ of Definition~\ref{def:gate}: $\gamma(X) \triangleq \mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{eq}(X, \epsilon),\, b,\, X\big)$, total, with $\gamma(w) = w$ for $w \neq \epsilon$ and $\gamma(\epsilon) = b$ -- a pattern wrapped in $\gamma$ is never empty. Second, the \emph{occurrence test} for total values $U, V$:
\begin{align*}
    \mathtt{occ}(U,V) \;\triangleq\; \mathtt{if}\Big(&\mathtt{eq}(U, x),\;
        \mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{eq}([\,xx/x\,]V,\, V),\, \bot,\, \top\big),\\
        &\mathtt{if}\big(\mathtt{eq}([\,x/\gamma(U)\,]V,\, V),\, \bot,\, \top\big)\Big),
\end{align*}
total, with value $\top$ iff $U$ occurs in $V$ whenever $U \neq \epsilon$ (the value at $U = \epsilon$ is defined but never load-bearing). Soundness is the observation that a pass whose replacement differs from its pattern changes its scrutinee iff the pattern occurs -- if the pattern is absent the text stands, and if it is present the spliced span, or the length, must move -- and the replacement $x$ differs from the pattern $\gamma(U)$ on the second branch by the case split itself, while the first branch's $[\,xx/x\,]$ strictly grows on firing. No freshness assumption is needed: the one degeneracy, pattern $=$ replacement, is routed around by testing $U$ first. (Verified: $225$ value pairs, $|U|, |V| \leq 3$: zero undefined, zero wrong answers on the load-bearing branch.)

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Flat lazy passes denote $\mathrm{L}$.})
    \label{thm:flatlazy}
    For every call-free expression $E$ over any alphabet with $|\Sigma| \geq 2$ there is a plain expression $E^{+}$ with $\llbracket E^{+} \rrbracket = \llbracket E \rrbracket^{\mathrm{lp}}$ -- the same partial function, domain included -- and $E^{+}$ is polynomially larger than $E$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    Write $\llbracket E \rrbracket^{\mathrm{lp}}$ for the lazy-pass denotation of the call-free $E$. Two mutually recursive translations, by structural induction. The \emph{value part} $\Phi(E)$ replaces every pattern by its coerced version: $\Phi(X_i) = X_i$, $\Phi(W) = W$, $\Phi(E_1 E_2) = \Phi(E_1)\Phi(E_2)$, and
    \begin{align*}
        \Phi\big(\,[\,R/P\,]\,T\,\big) \;=\; \big[\,\Phi R\,/\,\gamma(\Phi P)\,\big]\,\Phi T .
    \end{align*}
    Every pattern in $\Phi(E)$ is a $\gamma$-image, never empty, and every other position is total by induction, so $\Phi(E)$ is total. On an input where the lazy-pass machine defines $E$, the patterns are forced and nonempty, so each $\gamma$ acts as the identity there; an inert pass contributes its scrutinee either way (its replacement, total, is discarded); and a firing pass forces $R$, where $\Phi R$ agrees with $R$ by induction. Hence $\Phi(E)$ agrees with $\llbracket E \rrbracket^{\mathrm{lp}}$ on its domain.

    The \emph{domain part} $F(E) \in \{\top, \bot\}$, total, indicates the domain: $F(X_i) = F(W) = \top$; $F(E_1 E_2) = F(E_1) \wedge F(E_2)$; and
    \begin{align*}
        F\big(\,[\,R/P\,]\,T\,\big) \;=\;
        F(T) \,\wedge\, F(P) \,\wedge\, \mathtt{isne}(\Phi P)
        \,\wedge\, \big(\neg\,\mathtt{occ}(\Phi P, \Phi T) \;\vee\; F(R)\big),
    \end{align*}
    with $\mathtt{isne}$ as in Remark~\ref{rem:ifgate} and the connectives $\mathtt{if}$-combinations of $\top$ and $\bot$, all total -- note the \emph{conjunction}: an empty forced pattern must falsify $F$ outright, not vacuously satisfy it. The sub-expressions of $F$ are total -- the scrutinee and pattern of a node enter through $F$ itself, their \emph{values} only through the total $\Phi$ images -- so $F$ is well defined, and $F(E) = \top$ exactly on $\mathrm{dom}\big(\llbracket E \rrbracket^{\mathrm{lp}}\big)$: the node is defined iff its scrutinee and pattern are, the pattern is nonempty, and either the pattern is absent or the forced replacement is defined.

    Finally the \emph{guard}: $E^{+} \triangleq [\,b/\,\mathtt{if}(F(E),\, b,\, \epsilon)\,]\;\Phi(E)$. On the domain the pass is $[\,b/b\,]$, the identity; off it the pattern is empty and the expression is undefined. The size is a \emph{sum over the nodes, not a product over the levels}: $\Phi(E)$ is linear in $|E|$, and $F$ at a node adds $F$ of the children plus one copy each of the $\Phi$-images of the node's own pattern and scrutinee, so $|E^{+}| = O(|E|^2)$.

    (Verified exhaustively on the depth-$\leq 2$ call-free space over $\{a,b\}$ -- $599{,}844$ expressions, where the lazy-pass machine has $193$ denotations that no depth-$2$ eager expression has: for the simplest witness of each, $E^{+}$ reproduces the lazy partial function on all $31$ inputs $|S| \leq 4$ with zero mismatches; likewise for $250$ random depth-$2$ and $120$ random depth-$3$ expressions, $|E^{+}|$ up to ${\sim}3.1 \times 10^4$ nodes; $25$ witnesses re-verified on all $127$ inputs $|S| \leq 6$; and $31$ expressions cross-checked against an independent evaluator of the eager runtime -- zero mismatches throughout, and $\Phi$ total throughout.)
\end{proof}

The theorem completes Proposition~\ref{prop:lpcons} and sharpens the border this section draws. Eager evaluation and lazy arguments leave the reachable class unchanged even with recursion (Theorems~\ref{thm:eager} and~\ref{thm:lazyargs}); lazy passes make recursion universal through the gate (Theorem~\ref{thm:universal}). Theorem~\ref{thm:flatlazy} shows that the evaluation order \emph{alone} -- the operator's own non-strictness, without a self-reference to guard -- never leaves $\mathrm{L}$: the gate needs the loop. Section~\ref{sec:variants}'s $\mathrm{lim}$ node is the same border seen from the far side: flat $\mathrm{L}$ plus one iteration node computes \emph{exactly} the partial computable functions (Corollary~\ref{cor:limclass}) -- the loop, with no recursion to unwind, is the whole of it. In the terms of Proposition~\ref{prop:lpcons}, every discarded replacement is not merely harmless but \emph{recoverable}: the definedness the eager reading would lose to it is exactly the definedness the guard re-inserts.

\subsection{Streams: Productivity}
\label{sec:rec-streams}

For the coinductive reading, take the fragment $F_1$ -- one unary definition, constants, concatenation, constant-pattern passes -- and let values be finite or infinite strings, the pass running as the causal process of Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential} (its state: the longest suffix of the text consumed so far that is a proper prefix of the pattern). A definition denotes the \emph{least} fixpoint over partial strings; the machine's verdicts are \textsc{live} (unboundedly many characters emitted), \textsc{term} (a complete finite output) and \textsc{stall} (blocked without completing). Causality has a price: on an open input no process can decide that no more output is coming, so finite completion and infinite extension are incomparable outcomes. These questions are the string-substitution analogue of the productivity of recursive stream definitions, undecidable in general and decidable only for restricted classes \cite{endrullis10}.

\begin{proposition}
    (\emph{Adequacy and conservativity})
    \label{prop:streamadeq}
    The causal machine and the least-fixpoint denotation agree on all three verdicts (a $21$-case zoo, infinite inputs included; $300$ random call-free fragment expressions agree with the paper's operator composed). On finite inputs with constant patterns, the stream semantics refines the flat lazy-pass semantics of Section~\ref{sec:rec-lazy}: \textsc{term} and completed-\textsc{live} agree, and the flat undefinedness splits into \textsc{stall} versus \textsc{live} ($146$ curated and $120$ random programs, no mismatch).
\end{proposition}

The central question is \emph{guardedness}: when is the junction-guarded cascade $v = [\,R/P\,](W \cdot v)$, the pass straddling the junction between the emitted $W$ and the recursive stream, productive? The answer is semantic through and through. Call $e(W)$ the \emph{pre-pull emission}: what the pass on $W$ emits from consuming $W$ alone, before first demanding the recursive value.

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Guardedness is semantic})
    \label{thm:guardedness}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item (Deadlock) $e(W) = \epsilon$ implies \textsc{stall} with empty output: every level's emission is caused by a deeper one, and none ever starts.
        \item (Growth) $e(W) \neq \epsilon$ with $|R| \geq |P|$ and $|W| \geq |P|$ implies \textsc{live}: each level emits at least as much as it consumes.
        \item (Relay) $e(W) \neq \epsilon$ with $|P| = 1$ implies \textsc{live}: a single-character pattern never buffers.
        \item (Freshness) $e(W) \neq \epsilon$ with a character of $e(W)$ outside the alphabet of $P$ implies \textsc{live} (verified $560/560$; the flow argument is only sketched).
    \end{enumerate}
    And no simpler proxy survives: $e(W) \neq \epsilon$ with $R \neq \epsilon$ does not imply \textsc{live} -- deletion passes eat the junction buffer ($[\,\epsilon/ab\,](X \cdot f(X))$ on $X = aab$ stalls after emitting $a$), and $|R| < |P|$ admits geometric decay, the cascade contracting by $|R|/|P|$ per level ($76$ stall configurations in that region) -- while a fresh character in $R$ \emph{alone} does not either ($4$ counterexamples: the fresh character never fires, freshness must be in the emission). (Grid: $1{,}890$ configurations, $|W|, |P| \leq 3$, $|R| \leq 2$, over two- and three-letter alphabets: no violation of any of the four conditions.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{Least versus productive})
    \label{rem:leastfix}
    The cascade equation $v = [\,R/P\,](W \cdot v)$ can have \emph{productive} solutions while the least fixpoint is partial: for $W = \mathtt{bab}$, $P = \mathtt{bab}$, $R = \mathtt{b}$, the value $b^{\omega}$ is a fixpoint -- so are $ab^{\omega}$, $bab^{\omega}$, \dots -- but the least is the partial single character $b$, and the machine stalls after emitting it. The semantics selects the least, so guardedness must guarantee productivity \emph{of the least} fixpoint -- strictly harder than the existence of a productive one.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}
    (\emph{Beyond finite state})
    \label{thm:beyondfinite}
    One recursive definition outsprints the entire finite-state world of Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}: on input $a$,
    \begin{align}
        f(X) \;=\; \mathtt{cat}\big(X,\; b,\; f(\mathtt{cat}(X, X))\big)
    \end{align}
    emits $a\,b\,aa\,b\,aaaa\,b\,a^8\,b \cdots$, and no constant-pattern pipeline computes that stream: on a finite input a pipeline halts, and read coinductively on an ultimately periodic input it is a deterministic finite-state process, which revisiting a state cycles forever -- while the $a$-runs here double without bound. (The counter $f(X) = \mathtt{cat}(X, b, f(\mathtt{cat}(X, a)))$ gives the same conclusion with merely growing runs: $1, 2, 3, \dots$)
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
    The $n$-th call sees $X = a^{2^n}$ and emits $a^{2^n} b$, by induction, each level forcing only the next; the run lengths $2^n$ have no eventual period. (Verified \textsc{live}, prefixes matching the closed form beyond $40$ characters, cross-checked against the least-fixpoint evaluator.)
\end{proof}

Two further phenomena delimit the fragment. Recursion in the \emph{replacement} slot itself -- $f(X) = [\,f(X)/a\,]\,X$ -- obeys a trichotomy: if $a$ does not occur in the input, the value is the scrutinee, the replacement never forced (exactly the flat gate of Section~\ref{sec:rec-lazy}); if $a$ is the first character, deadlock, the forced replacement being the value being defined, which is partial and truncates everything after it; otherwise \textsc{live}, with the periodic value $s^{\omega}$ where $s$ is the $a$-free prefix before the first match. And with \emph{variable} patterns the pass never over-forces: it demands at most $\ell + 1$ characters of the pattern's value, $\ell$ the length of the longest prefix occurring as a factor of the scrutinee -- so an infinite pattern never completes a match, and the pass degenerates to streaming through mismatches, identity or absorption according to whether the scrutinee's tails keep extending the pattern's prefixes.

\begin{remark}
    (\emph{The $\omega$-limit reading})
    \label{rem:omega}
    Definition~\ref{def:lim} iterates a pass, and a pass is a causal
    process (Theorem~\ref{thm:subsequential}), so the orbit extends
    verbatim to infinite strings: the fixpoint test $s_{K+1} = s_K$,
    however, asks for equality of two $\omega$-strings -- it is not a
    finitely observable event.  The amplifier
    $\mathrm{lim}([\,baa/ab\,])$ on $a\,b^{\omega}$ shows what replaces
    it: the front (leftmost $a$) never retreats and spends exactly
    $2^{v-1}+1$ sweeps at each position $v$, so position $j$ is
    permanently $b$ from sweep $2^{j}+j$ on -- the orbit converges
    \emph{pointwise} to $b^{\omega}$, itself a fixed point of the pass,
    while firing every sweep forever: the stepwise test never fires,
    and the convergence is not uniform in any effective sense, each
    position costing exponentially many sweeps
    (Theorem~\ref{thm:beyondfinite}).  Nor does the stepwise
    reading agree with the finite one:
    $\mathrm{lim}([\,a/aa\,])(a^{n}) = a$ for every $n \geq 1$, but one
    pass over $a^{\omega}$ fires at $0, 2, 4, \dots$ and returns
    $a^{\omega}$ -- a fixed point, so the coinductive reading of the
    same node is $a^{\omega}$: least (approximation) versus greatest
    (coinductive) fixpoint, the split of Remark~\ref{rem:leastfix}
    compressed into a single rule.  And an $\omega$-orbit can abandon
    infinite strings entirely: $[\,\epsilon/ab\,]$ erases $(ab)^{\omega}$
    in \emph{one} sweep -- a process \textsc{live} forever with finite
    total output, the edge case of Theorem~\ref{thm:guardedness}.
    (Verified: front dwell and stabilization laws exact for $j < 10$,
    slack-certified; the two readings machine-checked on all quoted
    domains.)
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Landscape}

\begin{center}
\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{l|l}
    runtime & denotes \\
    \hline
    eager & $L$ exactly -- recursion inert \\
    lazy arguments & $L$ exactly -- inert, operationally separated \\
    lazy passes & the partial computable functions \\
    streams ($F_1$ fragment) & strictly beyond finite-state emitters; characterization open
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The four rows share one primitive; the distance between the second and the third is a single evaluation rule. What remains open:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The borderline.} The replacement slot is the one non-strictness universality needs: eager and lazy arguments give nothing, it gives everything. Is there an intermediate discipline -- a graded forcing of the replacement, say -- with strictly intermediate power?
    \item \emph{The stream boundary.} In the region $e(W) \neq \epsilon$, $|R| < |P|$, $\mathrm{alphabet}(R) \subseteq \mathrm{alphabet}(P)$, both \textsc{live} and \textsc{stall} occur and no complete invariant is known; and whether the fragment can enumerate every computable $\omega$-word is open (a unary counter suggests it can).
    \item \emph{Structural recursion only.} The schemes of Corollary~\ref{cor:barriers}(ii)--(iii) are structural -- each call on $\mathtt{tail}\,S$ -- and it is the $\mu$-gates that carry the universality. Restricting to structural recursion plausibly yields a string analogue of a subrecursive hierarchy; unexplored.
    \item \emph{Gates per variant.} The once-calculus's Selection splices its branches into the \emph{scrutinee} (Theorem~\ref{thm:once-toolkit}(v)), so it is not a gate under lazy passes; whether the once-primitive admits any two-way gate -- whether once-recursion is inert or universal -- is open, the recursive sibling of Section~\ref{sec:variants}'s first hinge.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
    \emph{Methodology.} The four runtimes were each implemented and cross-checked against independent evaluators -- a memoized Kleene chain, a fuel-capped operational runner, a big-step demand semantics, and the least-fixpoint evaluator -- and every number above was produced by a fresh re-run of the accompanying scripts; the grids and searches are exhaustive over the domains stated in situ.
\end{remark}

\section{Conclusion}

This note took search-and-replace seriously as a model of computation, and found a small theory with classical echoes. The single pass $[A/B]$ -- leftmost-first, all occurrences, no re-entry into inserted text -- supports a complete toolkit: escaping, concatenation, head and tail, equality, selection, and simultaneous multiple substitution are each an explicit composition of passes with a correctness proof, over any alphabet with at least two characters. The calculus built on the primitive is structurally well-behaved: a syntactic safety condition implies totality, concatenation is eliminable, and every reachable function is computable in polynomial time, with the degree read off the expression -- while quadratic growth is reachable and exponential growth is not. Varying the three design choices one at a time separated the once-primitive from full passes, spread the $k$-th-occurrence family between them into a mosaic of incomparable fragments, tied the right-to-left fragment to the reachability of reversal, collapsed the toolkit under rescanning, let Markov iteration outgrow every polynomial -- at the price of re-entering the classical one-rule termination problem, where the census found the leftmost strategy to cost nothing -- and closed the variant story with an exact class: iterating an expression to its fixed point, one node over a flat pipeline already computes every partial computable function, and one constant pass under it already grows exponentially while its divergent orbits grow like Fibonacci, and, we conjecture, never beyond single-exponential -- proved for the entire exponential tier by affine potentials, each sweep draining a nonnegative potential by a fixed amount, with the base $|A| - |B| + 1$ made exact by four rule shapes. Admitting both directions at once reduced the union's collapse to a single three-argument function, the right pass itself -- present in no searched space, and carried by a mechanism, residue routing, that the left-to-right frontier cannot perform. Varying the alphabet changed none of this up to conjugation: a comma-free coding carries every reachable function to its conjugate over any other alphabet of at least two letters, both ways, transfers all four scanning primitives verbatim, and makes each hinge a single question across alphabets -- while the unary edge splits, reachable from below by coding and structurally blocked from above, its class eventually polynomially bounded. Letting definitions recurse, finally, split the runtimes rather than the primitive: eager and lazy-argument evaluation buy nothing -- every recursive definition either inlines to a plain expression or never returns -- while the operator's one non-strictness, the replacement of a pass evaluated only when the pattern occurs, carries the calculus to universality -- though, without recursion, it buys definedness, not power -- and the coinductive reading on infinite strings beyond every finite-state emitter.  Read on streams, the iteration operator keeps its own two fixpoint readings -- the stepwise limit of finite approximations and the pointwise $\omega$-limit -- provably apart, and a single sweep can erase an $\omega$-word entire.

Two questions about the baseline itself carry the structure of the whole landscape: whether the once-primitive is $L$-reachable, and whether reversal is. The first is affirmative at its probe over two letters (Proposition~\ref{prop:del-leftmost}) and now rests on the computed needle and on alphabets of three or more letters; the second is sharpened by the union's criterion, which asks only whether the right pass as a function is reachable, and every search for which has failed, and priced by the crossing ladder of Remark~\ref{rem:price}. Both are single questions, not one per alphabet: the once node and the right pass are coding-equivariant, and reversal over one alphabet of at least two letters is reachable iff over every (Section~\ref{sec:alphabet}) -- a negative proof needs only one alphabet. Around them, the exact characterization of each variant's reachable class, the growth dichotomy for total restart rules, the strategy question of Theorem~\ref{thm:termination}(v), and the guardedness boundary for stream definitions remain open. Everything finite in this note was verified by machine on the domains stated in situ; the two hinges are where the theory now asks to be settled by hand.
